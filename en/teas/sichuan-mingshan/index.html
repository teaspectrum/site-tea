Mengding Huangya

China · Sichuan

Mengding Huangya is a yellow bud tea from Mengding Mountain in Ya’an, Sichuan, made from single buds and yellowed through menhuang. It has a golden, glossy appearance and an apricot-yellow liquor with sweet, tender aromas; it shares the Mengding tradition with Mengding Ganlu but is a different tea family.

What it is

Mengding Huangya is a yellow bud tea from the Mengding Mountain area of Ya’an, Sichuan, made from single buds or fine young material through fixation, menhuang and drying. It is a yellow tea, distinct from Mengding Ganlu, a green tea from the same area. Its yellow colour comes from menhuang and the raw material, not from simply letting green tea turn yellow.

Origin

It comes from the Mengding Mountain area in Mingshan District, Ya’an, sharing the traditional Mengding tea area with Mengding Ganlu. Shangqing Peak and Yunü Peak are often mentioned, but exact garden elevation, boundaries and quality differences should follow the GI standard and producer records. The Mengshan tea GI area is defined by GB/T 18665-2008; not every yellow bud tea from Ya’an may use the “Mengding” protected name.

Material and processing

Mengding Huangya traditionally uses plump single buds, complete and even. The key yellow-tea step is menhuang: after fixation, the buds are wrapped, spread and re-baked to turn yellow under warm, humid conditions, creating a yellow liquor and sweet, mellow taste. Old records describe “three wraps and three pan-firings” or multiple yellowing and re-firing cycles; the number and parameters vary by era, producer and product. GB/T 39592-2020 provides the processing boundary.

Quality and flavour

Dry buds are straight, golden and glossy. Liquor is apricot-yellow and bright, with sweet, tender and lightly pan-fired aromas, a mellow sweet taste and low bitterness. Compared with Junshan Yinzhen, Mengding Huangya has a similar bud style but a different origin; compared with Huoshan Huangya, raw-material tenderness and local processing differ. Grade, picking date and menhuang degree affect style; “yellow bud” alone does not guarantee quality.

Brewing

Use a glass or gaiwan with water around 85–90°C and short infusions. Fine buds should not be stewed; water that is too hot or a long infusion flattens the sweetness. Adjust leaf quantity to vessel and taste; a glass helps show the buds opening.

History and boundaries

Mengding tea has a long history, with Tang–Song records and a widely circulated saying, “water from the Yangtze, tea from Mengding.” Mengding Huangya and Mengding Ganlu share the Mengding tradition but belong to different tea families, processes and modern product standards. “Wu Lizhen planted seven tea trees” and “all yellow tea comes from Mengding” are legend and promotional language; check gazetteers, GI standards and research before treating them as history.

Common misconceptions

  • Mengding Huangya is not Mengding Ganlu. The former is yellow tea; the latter is green tea.
  • “Yellow bud” is not a cultivar name. It describes raw-material tenderness and product type.
  • Mengding legends are not historical evidence. Wu Lizhen and the seven trees are cultural narratives.
  • Are all Mengding teas the same grade? No. Origin, material, processing and standard execution all affect quality.

Junshan Yinzhen

Junshan Yinzhen is a yellow bud tea from Junshan Island in Yueyang, Hunan, made from plump single buds and yellowed through menhuang. The golden bud body with white down is traditionally called “gold inlaid with jade”; the brewing display is a visual feature, not a quality guarantee.

China · Anhui

Huoshan Huangya

Huoshan Huangya is a yellow bud tea from Huoshan, Anhui, made from fine young leaves and yellowed through menhuang. It has a straight, slightly open shape and a yellow-green to apricot liquor with sweet, chestnut-like aromas; it is not a green tea, and “Huangjinya” cultivar names are not the same product.

South Korea

Hwangcha (Korean Partially Oxidised Tea)

Hwangcha (황차) is a Korean partially oxidised tea, not a synonym for Chinese yellow tea and its menhuang / sealed-yellow process. Hankook Tea's official tea guide puts hwangcha at roughly 50–80% oxidation and describes it as pan-fired, with a production cycle of about two to three days. Its Hwang Cha Gold product is an early-spring, Sejak-grade tea from the company's Honam gardens, made from traditional jae-rae-jong seed-grown material; the liquor is amber and smooth, sitting between green tea and black tea. The name “yellow tea” describes colour and market usage, not the Chinese yellow-tea process.
