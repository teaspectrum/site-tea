What it is: a regional oolong family, not one brand

Mộc Châu Oolong is an oolong product family from the Mộc Châu plateau in Sơn La, Vietnam. It is not one trademark and not one cultivar. Several companies, cooperatives and farming households make oolong there, and products commonly carry names such as “Trà Ô Long Mộc Châu” or “Mộc Châu Oolong”.

At least two producers can be documented:

  • Mộc Sương: a tea company and eco-tourism area around Tân Lập in Mộc Châu, with its own published timeline and product history.
  • Tân Lập Tea Processing Co., Ltd.: the OCOP 3-star oolong producer reported by the Sơn La provincial newspaper, making Kim Tuyên and Tứ Quý oolong.

These two are not the whole industry. This page uses them as verifiable cases, not as a complete producer directory.

The region: a plateau at 1,054 m

Vietnam's national tourism board says Mộc Châu is 1,054 m above sea level, has a climate suited to tea, and is home to “the largest tea plantations in the land”. Two cautions follow:

  • 1,054 m is the regional plateau altitude, not the uniform altitude of every tea garden.
  • “Largest tea-garden scenery” is a tourism-board phrase, not a national tea-area statistic. It can be quoted as official tourism wording, but it should not be turned into “Mộc Châu has the largest tea area in Vietnam”.

The tea hills are about 25 minutes from the town centre, and tea tours run from April to December. That is travel information, not a tea-quality standard.

Case one: Mộc Sương's Taiwanese-style timeline

Mộc Sương's official page gives a relatively complete account:

Time Official account
2005 Started the Mộc Sương brand, brought oolong stock from Taiwan and planted 20 ha
Tea-garden altitude 1,250 m
2010 Launched Thanh Tâm and Thúy Ngọc oolong
Current scale 30 ha farm with tea and fruit trees
2012 Started the Mộc Sương eco-tourism area

Mộc Sương says that Mộc Châu had a suitable climate at the time but few people were growing high-value oolong for export; the team went to Taiwan to learn tea growing and processing and brought oolong stock back. That is the company's own account, and it should not be expanded into “the first Taiwanese-style oolong garden in Vietnam” as regional history. What can be confirmed is narrower: Mộc Sương's oolong route is connected to Taiwanese tea stock and processing, and the company's own timeline begins in 2005.

Mộc Sương also says its tea garden is at 1,250 m, with cool summers and three months of winter frost. That 1,250 m is the company's garden elevation, not the regional 1,054 m. The two figures do not conflict, but they must not be merged.

Case two: Tân Lập's Kim Tuyên and Tứ Quý

The Sơn La provincial newspaper reported on OCOP products in Tân Yên commune in September 2025. For Tân Lập Tea Processing Co., Ltd.:

  • In 2024, Trà Bản Hoa Kim Tuyên and Trà Bản Hoa Tứ Quý oolong were recognised as OCOP 3-star products.
  • The company manages more than 80 ha of linked tea gardens, mainly the Kim Tuyên cultivar.
  • It uses Taiwanese processing equipment, exports to Taiwan, and consumes about 800–900 tonnes of fresh tea a year.
  • It also makes green tea and black tea, not only oolong.

This shows that Mộc Châu oolong is not only Mộc Sương and not only one cultivar. Tân Lập's Kim Tuyên (Jinxuan) and Tứ Quý (Four Season) are another verifiable product combination.

OCOP stars are not tea grades

In the 2025 Sơn La OCOP list, four Mộc Sương products hold OCOP 4 stars:

  • Trà Ô long xanh Thúy Ngọc
  • Trà Ô long xanh Thanh Tâm
  • Trà Ô long Thúy Ngọc duỗi đỏ
  • Trà Ô long Thanh Tâm GABA

Two Tân Lập products hold OCOP 3 stars:

  • Trà Bản Hoa Kim Tuyên – Jinxuan Oolong Tea
  • Trà Bản Hoa Tứ Quý – Four Season Oolong Tea

OCOP is Vietnam's “One Commune One Product” rural-product rating. It assesses a product and its local supply chain, not tea sensory quality. Four OCOP stars do not mean “Special Grade tea”, and three stars do not mean “Third Grade tea”. Translating OCOP stars directly into tea grades misleads readers.

Likewise, “GABA” is part of a product name and points to a processing direction. This page does not extend that name into any bodily-function claim.

How to describe the flavour

Mộc Sương and Tân Lập do not publish an official cupping table. Mộc Sương emphasises Taiwanese stock, a 1,250 m garden and its own processing; Tân Lập emphasises Taiwanese equipment, the Kim Tuyên cultivar and exports to Taiwan. These facts establish a Taiwanese-style oolong route with highland material, not a single regional flavour of milk, orchid or honey.

A safer description:

  • Mộc Châu oolong follows the Taiwanese-style oolong route, with cultivars such as Qingxin, Jinxuan and Four Season in use.
  • Different producers, cultivars and batches produce different aromas and oxidation levels.
  • Specific flavour notes should come from the pack or an actual tasting, not from one adjective applied to the whole region.

Bao Loc Oolong Tea

Bao Loc oolong (Trà Ô Long Bảo Lộc) is a tea region and a family of oolong-style teas from Bao Loc in Lam Dong, Vietnam. It is not a single brand and not a synonym for “Vietnamese Tieguanyin”. Bao Loc sits at about 800 m on the Lam Dong plateau, with cool nights, thick morning fog and a large day-night temperature swing; its soil is deeply weathered basalt, rich in minerals and both well drained and moisture-retentive. Local gardens grow local seed tea, Shan tea and introduced oolong cultivars. Bao Loc oolong is exported to Russia, France, the United States, India, Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore, Taiwan, Hong Kong and the Middle East. Bao Loc VN and Kim Điền operate at different scales.

Choui Fong Oolong

Choui Fong Oolong is not a single cultivar or a producing region. It is the oolong line of Choui Fong Tea, a family-owned producer in Chiang Rai, Thailand, built mainly on Jin Xuan and Ruan Zhi cultivars and sold as plain, scented and first-flush products.
