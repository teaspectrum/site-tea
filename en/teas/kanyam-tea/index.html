Kanyam Tea Estate

Nepal

Kanyam Tea Estate is a tea garden in Ilam, Nepal, owned by the Nepal Tea Development Corporation (NTDC) and now operated by the Triveni Group. NTDC calls it the “Queen of all Tea Gardens”. Its factory sits at 5,500 ft (about 1,676 m), and NTDC describes the garden as the highest in Ilam district; the estate covers about 216 ha, of which 200 ha are planted to tea, and produces about 250,000 kg of orthodox tea a year. It lies in the foothills of Kanchenjunga, about 2 km from the Darjeeling tea gardens. NTDC says the garden was established with British technical collaboration at the initiative of the late King Birendra, but it gives no exact planting year. Kanyam is a garden and place name, not a single retail brand or a tea cultivar; the official material does not give a cultivar, grade, price, organic certification or specific sensory profile.

What it is: a garden, not a tea brand or a cultivar

Kanyam Tea Estate is a tea garden in Ilam, Nepal, in the foothills of Kanchenjunga. It belongs to the Nepal Tea Development Corporation (NTDC) and is now operated by the Triveni Group. It is a place and estate name, not a single retail brand and not a tea cultivar.

NTDC calls Kanyam the “Queen of all Tea Gardens of Nepal”. That is the company's own branding, not an official competition award and not a certification of “the world's highest tea garden”. The more precise, verifiable statement is: NTDC describes Kanyam as the highest tea garden in Ilam district.

Official facts

Item NTDC statement
Location Ilam district, Nepal, in the Kanchenjunga foothills
Factory altitude 5,500 ft (about 1,676 m)
Altitude claim Highest tea garden in Ilam district (NTDC wording)
Total area About 216 ha
Tea area 200 ha
Annual output About 250,000 kg
Tea type Orthodox tea
Distance to Darjeeling, India About 2 km
Establishment At the initiative of the late King Birendra Bir Bikram Shah, with British government technical collaboration
Operator NTDC estate, privatised in 2000 and operated by the Triveni Group

“5,500 ft” is the official factory altitude, not a uniform elevation for every plot in the garden. The 216 ha total, 200 ha of tea and 250,000 kg annual output are also official approximations, not audited year-by-year figures.

“Queen” is a company title, not an award

NTDC uses “Queen of all Tea Gardens” on its website and in the Tokla brochure. The phrase has a clear source, but it is company marketing language. Writing “Kanyam was judged the Queen of the World's Tea Gardens” or “won an international tea competition's queen award” turns a company title into an award that does not exist.

Likewise, “the highest tea garden in Ilam district” is a citable NTDC claim. It cannot be expanded into “the world's highest tea garden”, “the highest tea garden in the Himalayas” or “2,100 m”. The official factory altitude is 5,500 ft, about 1,676 m.

History and ownership: no exact planting year is given

NTDC's official account provides several firm historical markers:

  • Nepal's first tea garden was planted in 1863 by Gajaraj Singh Thapa, then Governor General of eastern Nepal, using tea seeds gifted by the Chinese Emperor.
  • NTDC was formed in 1966 with seven tea estates. Five were planted under British technical collaboration; two were planted by Gajraj Singh Thapa in 1863.
  • Kanyam was established with British government technical collaboration at the initiative of the late King Birendra Bir Bikram Shah, with the aim of encouraging Nepal's tea sector.
  • In 2000, the government privatised the estates and handed them to the Triveni Group.

The official material does not give an exact planting year for Kanyam. The Tokla brochure says the five British-collaboration estates were established “around 35 years ago” relative to the time of writing, but that relative phrase cannot be converted into a specific year. This page therefore does not use “established in 1982” or “won a London blind test in 1994”.

Orthodox tea versus “organic”

NTDC says Kanyam produces orthodox tea. Orthodox refers to a processing route that keeps the leaf in strips through rolling, breaking and drying, as distinct from CTC ( crush-tear-curl) production. It is not a grade name, and it is not an absolute claim that orthodox is “higher” than CTC.

“Organic” needs a separate check. NTDC's website says Kanyam has begun planning a shift to more fully eco-friendly cultivation for organic orthodox black tea. That is a planned direction, not an existing organic certification. Without a certificate number and certification body, the garden should not be described as certified organic.

Flavour: no official sensory table

NTDC says only that Kanyam produces “the most exquisite orthodox tea”; it does not publish an official cupping table or use specific descriptors such as muscatel, orchid or honey. Its proximity to Darjeeling and similar topography and climate are geographical facts, but they do not justify calling Kanyam “Darjeeling tea” or claiming a specific Darjeeling-style flavour.

A safer description is: Kanyam is a high-elevation orthodox tea garden in Ilam district, about 2 km from Darjeeling, and NTDC describes its tea as exquisite. Aroma, taste and liquor colour should be assessed batch by batch.

Source boundary

This page confirms that Kanyam Tea Estate is a tea garden in Ilam, Nepal, owned by NTDC and operated by the Triveni Group; that NTDC calls it the “Queen of all Tea Gardens”; that the factory is at 5,500 ft (about 1,676 m) and NTDC describes the garden as the highest in Ilam district; that the estate covers about 216 ha, with 200 ha planted to tea and about 250,000 kg of orthodox tea a year; that it is about 2 km from the Darjeeling tea gardens; that it was established at the initiative of the late King Birendra with British technical collaboration; and that NTDC was formed in 1966 with seven estates and privatised them to the Triveni Group in 2000.

This page does not confirm an exact planting year, a tea cultivar, a leaf grade, a retail price, an organic certification, a cupping score, caffeine content, or any health claim. The page image is a photograph of the Kanyam garden, not packaging or a single retail product.

Abigail's Blend

Abigail's Blend is a commemorative black-tea blend created for the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum and Abigail's Tea Room by tea master Bruce Richardson and crafted by Elmwood Inn Fine Teas. It is named for Abigail Adams. It is not the tea thrown into Boston Harbor in 1773, and it is not the “Boston Harbour Tea” commercial brand. The blend uses Chinese Yunnan black tea as its base and adds Assam, Nilgiri and Kenyan black teas. The museum's own description is that it recalls the Chinese black teas thrown overboard in 1773, “but better”. It can be served hot or iced, with or without milk. The product comes as 3.5 oz (99 g) loose tea or 14 pyramid tea bags at $17.99. On 16 December 1773, 340 chests of East India Company tea from the Beaver, Dartmouth and Eleanor were dumped into Boston Harbor, weighing over 92,000 lb (about 46 tonnes); about 80% was Chinese black tea.

American Classic Tea (Charleston Tea Garden)

American Classic Tea is the flagship black tea of Charleston Tea Garden on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina. It is made exclusively from tea grown in the United States, but it is not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic “Charleston-style” black tea. The garden's own material supplies the usable facts: 127 acres, more than 320 tea varieties, a history that runs from Charles Shepard's Pinehurst plantation in 1888 to the 1963 transplant at Wadmalaw Island, commercial operation in 1987 and Bigelow ownership from 2003. The tea itself is a larger-leaf black tea; the official brewing suggestion is 8 ounces of boiling water for 2–4 minutes, hot or iced. The page deliberately leaves cultivar and CTC/Orthodox claims out because the official pages do not publish them.

Assam Tea

Assam is the world’s largest contiguous tea-growing region, producing bold, malty black tea on the Brahmaputra plains; CTC is its everyday volume tea, while orthodox Assam offers a fuller-leaf alternative.
