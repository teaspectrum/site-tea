Kanchanjangha Verde

Nepal

Kanchanjangha Verde is a high-mountain organic green tea made by the Kanchanjangha Tea Estate and Research Center (KTERC) in Panchthar, eastern Nepal. It is also sold as Kanchanjangha Verde (Himalayan Mist). KTERC was founded in 1984 and describes itself as Nepal's first certified organic tea garden; its 95-hectare estate sits in the foothills of Kanchenjunga, with the estate's own materials giving two altitude ranges, 800–2,300 m for the whole property and 1,300–1,800 m for the main garden. The leaf is hand-plucked, briefly rested, quickly dried to stop oxidation, rolled, rested, re-dried, crushed and machine-sorted, then hand-graded. The method is close to classic green tea from the Darjeeling Hills.

What this tea is

“Nepal green tea” sounds like a national category. In practice Nepal has tea gardens at different altitudes and several tea types; green tea is one branch, not the whole country. Kanchanjangha Verde is a specific organic high-mountain green tea from KTERC in Panchthar. “Verde” is a brand name, not a cultivar.

The product is also sold as Kanchanjangha Verde (Himalayan Mist). That second name is useful because it describes the style: a soft, aromatic highland green tea, not a sharp Japanese sencha and not a pan-fired Chinese green.

This page is about KTERC's product only. Nepal has other green teas, other organic gardens and other processing styles. One estate's 95 hectares and certification do not define the country.

The estate: KTERC

KTERC stands for Kanchanjangha Tea Estate and Research Center. It sits near Phidim in Panchthar, eastern Nepal, in the foothills of Kanchenjunga. The estate's own facts are specific:

Item Official position
Founded 1984
Organic status Nepal's first certified organic tea garden
Area 95 ha, with about 87.5 ha of tea garden including extensions
Altitude Two estate figures: 800–2,300 m for the property; about 1,300–1,800 m for the main garden
Ownership A co-operative of about 100 farmer-owners
Employment 150+ direct, about 400 indirect; more than 70% women
Products Black and green tea; the estate says it made Nepal's first organic green tea

“First certified organic tea garden” and “first organic green tea” are KTERC's own claims. They establish the estate's pioneer position in Nepali organic tea; they do not prove that no other Nepali garden is organic today.

The co-operative structure matters to the tea's identity. This is not a single-owner estate with a decorative “farmer story”. The farmers are described as owners through a co-operative, and the estate runs savings and loan services alongside tea. That structure is part of why KTERC can market both organic certification and a social enterprise model.

Processing: a Darjeeling-style green tea

Kanchanjangha Verde is made in a style closer to classic green tea from the Darjeeling Hills than to Japanese steaming or Chinese pan-firing:

  1. Hand-pluck the leaf.
  2. Brief rest to lose some moisture without fermenting.
  3. Quick dry to stop oxidation.
  4. Roll to break cells and release aroma precursors.
  5. Rest so essential oils can develop.
  6. Re-dry at the peak of aroma.
  7. Crush and machine-sort through three machines to collect the finer leaf.
  8. Hand-grade the finished tea by leaf quality.

The product page says this method was brought to KTERC by workers from Darjeeling in the early 1990s. That detail matters: the technique is not an ancient Nepali green-tea tradition. It is a deliberate transfer of Darjeeling know-how into a Nepali organic garden, then adapted locally.

Because it is not a steamed tea, do not expect the marine, bright-green, low-temperature profile of Japanese sencha. The aroma is more highland-floral and grassy, with a nutty edge, and the body can take more heat than a delicate steamed green.

Flavour and brewing

The product page gives the flavour as mellow, grassy meadows, floral, with subtle fruit and nut. That is a useful starting point. It is not a licence to invent “orchid, peach, cream” tasting notes.

The page does not give official gram, temperature or time figures. This page therefore does not present one set of numbers as the product standard. As an editorial starting point, try 3 g tea, 150 ml water, 80–85°C, 1–2 minutes. Keep the first steep short; extend the second. If the grassy edge is too strong, lower the temperature rather than reaching for a hard boil.

Where it sits in Nepali tea

KTERC's importance is not just “high mountain”. It combines organic farming, co-operative ownership, women's employment and orthodox processing in one system. That is a more useful explanation of consistency than the usual “misty Himalaya” scenery.

It is still one producer. Nepal has other tea areas, other orthodox gardens and other green teas. Applying KTERC's 95 hectares, organic certificate and Darjeeling-style process to the whole country would flatten the category.

Common misreadings

Common shorthand Problem Better wording
“All Nepal green tea is high-mountain mist tea” Nepal has different altitudes and tea types KTERC is a high-mountain organic garden in Panchthar
“Everything is handmade” The process uses machine crushing and sorting, then hand grading Hand-pluck + machine sort + hand grade
“Nepal green tea has a specific health benefit” No verifiable clinical or official claim Describe flavour and processing only
“KTERC is a single estate” It is a co-operative of about 100 farmer-owners A cooperative estate, not a single-owner plantation
“Verde is a cultivar” It is a product name Kanchanjangha Verde is the product

Source boundary

This page can confirm: Kanchanjangha Verde is a KTERC product; KTERC was founded in 1984 and is Nepal's first certified organic tea garden; the estate is 95 ha in the Kanchenjunga foothills of Panchthar; the leaf is hand-plucked and processed in a Darjeeling-style green-tea method; the product carries USDA Organic and CERES certification; the flavour is mellow, grassy and floral with subtle fruit and nut.

This page does not confirm: the exact retail price, annual volume, precise cultivar, picking season for each lot, caffeine content, or any health benefit. The image is a tea garden at Ilam, Nepal, not the KTERC estate in Panchthar.

Tea in Nepal

Nepal's tea area concentrates in the eastern foothills — Ilam, Dhankuta, Panchthar and Terhathum — at 1,000–2,200 metres, shaped by monsoon and mountain cloud.

China · Zhejiang

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

China · Jiangsu

Biluochun

Biluochun is a pan-fired spring green tea from the Dongting hills in Lake Tai, Jiangsu. The leaf is tiny, tightly curled and covered with white down; the tea is famous for a fresh, sweet, slightly fruity cup and for the tea–fruit intercropping tradition of Dongting Mountain.

Colombia

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.
