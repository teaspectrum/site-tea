Guiding Yunwu Tribute Tea

China · Guizhou

Guiding Yunwu Tribute Tea is a nationally protected regional green tea from Guiding County in Guizhou, China. Its core production area is Yunwu Town, and it is a geographical indication, not a single company brand. It received national agricultural GI recognition in September 2010 and a geographical indication certification mark in July 2013. At the end of 2024 the county had 255,600 mu (about 17,040 ha) of tea gardens, 247,300 mu (about 16,487 ha) in production, and an output of 11,807 tonnes, up about 5.35% year on year; Xinhua's contemporaneous figure for primary-sector output value was about RMB 580 million. Local records describe 2,000-plus years of tea growing, 1,000-plus years of tribute tea and 500-plus years of Buddhist tea history; in 1790 the Qianlong court allocated 420 taels of silver and a “Wangu Liufang” tribute stele was erected. The main cultivar is Guiding Niaowang, approved as a provincial improved variety in June 2014, and DB52/T 547—2017 grades the product as Zhengong, Special Grade, Grade 1 and Grade 2.

A regional GI, not a company brand

Guiding Yunwu Tribute Tea is a nationally protected regional green tea from Guiding County, Guizhou. Its core production area is Yunwu Town. It is not one factory's trademark and not a single-estate tea: authorised producers in Guiding County use the regional name if they meet the cultivar, origin and processing requirements, and their teas can differ in flavour and price.

The geographical-indication timeline is clear:

  • September 2010: national GI product certification by the Ministry of Agriculture;
  • July 2013: geographical indication certification mark from the State Administration for Industry and Commerce;
  • 2024: Guiding selected as one of China's key tea counties; the tea entered the second batch of the national famous, special and new agricultural products list.

These are two different protections. GI product certification protects the origin-cultivar-process identity; the certification mark protects the right to use the name. They are not the same certificate.

Place and industry: 255,600 mu, 11,807 tonnes

The Yunwu mountain area of Guiding is over 1,500 m, near 26°N, with frequent cloud and mist, limited sunshine and a marked day-night temperature difference. The 2024 figures are:

Indicator 2024 figure
Total tea garden 255,600 mu (about 17,040 ha)
Area in production 247,300 mu (about 16,487 ha)
Annual output 11,807 tonnes
Year-on-year up about 5.35% from 11,208 tonnes in 2023
Primary-sector output value about RMB 580 million (Xinhua)

Xinhua rounds the output to 11,800 tonnes; the county figure is 11,807. They are the same order of magnitude. The RMB 580 million figure comes from Xinhua. Do not combine it with other provincial or prefecture reviews that use a different statistical scope; mixing them would invent an “official” number that does not exist.

Yunwu Town is the core. In March 2025 the town held its spring-tea opening and opened the Baicheng village fresh-leaf trading market. That is a sign that Guiding's tea economy runs through a town-level leaf market, not only through tea-garden tourism.

History: 2,000 years of tea, 1,000 years of tribute

The county and the Qiannan Agriculture Bureau give the framework: 2,000-plus years of tea growing, 1,000-plus years of tribute tea, 500-plus years of Buddhist tea. The timeline comes from local gazetteers and the tribute stele, not from a modern marketing campaign.

The documentary sequence runs like this:

  • In 793, during the Tang Zhenyuan era, the local administration sent tea and other products as tribute;
  • The Jiajing edition of the Guizhou Tongzhi (1522–1566) says: “Of all the tea districts in Guizhou, Guiding's Yunwu Mountain is the most famous; the yield is tiny and the tea is extremely hard to obtain”;
  • In 1790, the Qianlong court allocated 420 taels of silver to Niaowang Miao farmers to expand production and erected the “Wangu Liufang” stele on Yunwu Mountain, known as the tribute-tea stele;
  • In 1805, a production-boundary stele defined the origin area;
  • In the Guangxu era, Guizhou governor Lin Shaonian presented two caskets of “Guiding bud tea”, one for the Empress Dowager and one for the emperor.

The county describes Guiding as “the only court tribute tea recorded on a stele in China”. That is a county-level official statement. This page records it with attribution; it does not turn it into a national academic consensus. The stele can document the Qing court relationship; it cannot prove that every leaf from the Tang dynasty entered the palace.

Cultivar and grades: Niaowang, four levels

The cultivar base is Guiding Niaowang, selected from the seedling tea population of Niaowang village. In June 2014 it passed provincial review as an improved local cultivar, approval number Qian Shen Cha 2014002. The standard and county materials treat it as the main raw material for Guiding Yunwu Tribute Tea.

DB52/T 547—2017 divides the product into four grades:

Grade Raw leaf standard Sensory profile (standard summary)
Zhengong (Tribute Grade) Single bud, or one bud with one freshly opened leaf Tight, fine, curled, white-haired; bright tender green; persistent tender aroma; fresh and brisk
Special Grade One bud with one leaf Relatively tight, curled, visible hair; bright green; tender aroma; fresh and mellow
Grade 1 One bud with two freshly opened leaves Tight, slightly curled, some hair; yellow-green and fairly bright; clean aroma; mellow
Grade 2 One bud with two leaves, or one bud with three freshly opened leaves Compact, slightly curled, some hair; yellow-green and somewhat bright; pure aroma; straightforward mellow

These are product grades, not altitude or tree-age grades. “Zhengong must be ancient-tree tea” has no basis in the standard: the grade is defined by leaf tenderness, sensory quality and chemical indicators.

Guiding Xueya is a separate product name. The provincial government page says it uses one bud and one freshly opened leaf picked 7–21 days after the spring flush from the Niaowang cultivar and is finished by a mechanised “nine-step” process. Xueya is not the top grade of the Yunwu Tribute Tea grade system, and the two names should not be merged.

Flavour and brewing

The standard sensory description is: curled and white-haired, silver-green in dry leaf, clear green-yellow liquor, rich aroma, strong and brisk taste, lasting sweetness, tender green-yellow leaf base. That is the style produced by the local cultivar and the green-tea process: aromatic and substantial, not a thin, sweet water.

The official material does not give one gram, temperature and time set. As an editorial starting point, try 3 g tea, 150 ml water, 80–85°C, 1–2 minutes. Zhengong and Special Grade are tender, so keep the water cooler; Grade 1 and Grade 2 can take 85°C and a slightly longer steep. These are editorial suggestions, not part of the GI standard.

Common misreadings

Common shorthand Problem Better wording
“Lu Yu named Guiding Yunwu” No verifiable passage in the Classic of Tea Do not cite Lu Yu; cite local gazetteers and the stele
“Ancient-tree yield” No ancient-tree production statistic Say 2,000-plus years of tea growing, not ancient-tree volume
“Specific amino-acid or selenium content” No standard or test report supplied Use only the standard's sensory and chemical indicators
“Yunwu Tribute Tea is Guiding Xueya” Xueya is a separate product name Yunwu Tribute Tea is the regional brand; Xueya is a product
“All Guiding Yunwu is handmade” Xueya uses a mechanised nine-step process Traditional and mechanised processes coexist

China · Zhejiang

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

China · Jiangsu

Biluochun

Biluochun is a pan-fired spring green tea from the Dongting hills in Lake Tai, Jiangsu. The leaf is tiny, tightly curled and covered with white down; the tea is famous for a fresh, sweet, slightly fruity cup and for the tea–fruit intercropping tradition of Dongting Mountain.

Colombia

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.
