横县茉莉花茶基茶

China · Guangxi · Hengxian Jasmine Tea Base

“横县茉莉花茶基茶”指横州（原横县）茉莉花茶窨制所用的绿茶茶坯，不是已经窨好的茉莉花茶。横州是全球最大的茉莉花与茉莉花茶加工中心之一，2024 年茉莉花茶加工量超过 9 万吨；基茶多来自福建、云南、贵州、四川等绿茶产区，要求吸香能力强、滋味有骨架。买“基茶”不等于买“茉莉花茶”。

“Hengxian jasmine tea base” is easy to misread. It does not mean a finished jasmine-scented tea. It means the green-tea body — the “tea blank” — selected in Hengzhou (formerly Hengxian) for scenting with fresh jasmine. The base may come from Fujian, Yunnan, Guizhou or Sichuan; it becomes jasmine tea only after scenting in Hengzhou. Buying base tea and expecting a floral finished tea is the first confusion this page has to clear up.

Origin and identity

Hengzhou, in Nanning, Guangxi, is one of the world's largest jasmine-flower and jasmine-tea processing centres. In 2024 it had more than 130,000 mu of jasmine, over 110,000 tonnes of fresh flowers and more than 90,000 tonnes of jasmine-tea processing, with a combined jasmine-flower and tea output value above 18 billion yuan. Local figures say Hengzhou accounts for more than 80% of China's jasmine and jasmine tea and more than 60% of the world's jasmine flowers. Large-scale development began in 1978 and has built a processing cluster over four decades.

But “Hengxian jasmine tea base” is not a botanical cultivar or a single Hengzhou garden. It is a supply-chain category: green-tea blanks chosen for the Hengzhou scenting system. The base can be baked, pan-fired or semi-baked green tea. It usually needs some body, a clean aroma, enough extract and the ability to absorb fragrance. Its origin may be outside Hengzhou, or even outside Guangxi.

History and industry

The key date is 1978, when Hengzhou began large-scale jasmine planting and turned a flower crop into a flower–tea–processing–export chain. In 2006 Hengxian jasmine became a national geographical indication product; in 2013 Hengxian jasmine tea followed; in 2020 both entered the China–EU reciprocal GI list, and the tea was among the first protected under the China–EU GI agreement.

Hengzhou's advantage is not just flower volume. It is that flowers and tea meet in the same place in the same time window. Jasmine opens at night, when fragrance peaks; the tea base must be pre-treated to the right moisture, temperature and aroma state. Hengzhou concentrates flower trading, base blending, scenting, sifting, re-firing and packing in one cluster. In 2024 the county also introduced an automated “no-floor” scenting line: tea and flowers mix, scent and sift inside a closed plant, lifting fragrance utilisation from below 50% in traditional methods to above 80%.

Base tea and scenting

Scenting is physical adsorption and aroma transfer, not spraying flavour. The general flow is: re-fire and clean the base, cool it; condition and sift the flowers; blend tea and flowers; build a pile; turn and cool; re-pile; remove spent flowers; re-fire to fix the aroma. High-grade teas may be scented three, five, seven or more times, each with fresh flowers. The flower-to-tea ratio, temperature and time all matter. More rounds do not automatically mean better: a thin base, wet flowers or excessive re-firing can make the aroma dull or distorted.

The base sets the floor of the finished tea. Dusty base absorbs quickly but gives a thin body; coarse old leaf absorbs slowly and turns rough; a smoky or mouldy base cannot be rescued by more flowers. A good base is tight, green-glossy, clean-smelling and rich in extract, so it can carry the flower without fighting it. The finished tea is judged by “freshness” of the aroma — whether it smells like a newly opened jasmine, not perfume or soap.

Buying and boundaries

First, check whether you are buying base tea or finished scented tea. Base tea has little jasmine aroma and is not labelled with “how many scentings”; it is for further processing or home scenting, not direct brewing as a floral tea. Finished tea carries scenting rounds, flower grade and aroma type. Selling base tea as “premium jasmine tea” is a category confusion.

Second, base tea is not a synonym for “green tea grown in Hengzhou.” Hengzhou's core is flowers and scenting; the base may come from another province. Third, scenting rounds are not the only grade: fresh aroma, a full liquor and no dullness matter more than a “nine-scenting” number. Fourth, jasmine tea is not “the stronger the better”: perfumed, soapy or cooked notes are defects. Brew at 85–90°C, 3 g to 150 ml, 30–40 seconds for the first infusion, and avoid long boiling-water steeps.

Source boundary

Hengzhou's scale, 1978 starting point, GI dates and scenting line come from Hengzhou and Nanning government pages; national and world shares and export destinations come from the Hengzhou government's reprint of a China Daily report. Base-tea sourcing and scenting are described as industry process variables, not as a claim that all Hengzhou base comes from one famous mountain.

China · Zhejiang

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

China · Jiangsu

Biluochun

Biluochun is a pan-fired spring green tea from the Dongting hills in Lake Tai, Jiangsu. The leaf is tiny, tightly curled and covered with white down; the tea is famous for a fresh, sweet, slightly fruity cup and for the tea–fruit intercropping tradition of Dongting Mountain.

Colombia

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.
