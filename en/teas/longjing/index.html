What it is

Longjing is a pan-fired green tea from the Hangzhou area of Zhejiang. It is known for flat, straight leaves, a fresh fragrance and a sweet, refreshing taste. The traditional phrase “green colour, rich aroma, sweet taste, beautiful shape” summarises its aesthetic, but it is not a substitute for a product standard. Not every flat green tea is Longjing.

Origin and protected area

The protected area, natural environment, cultivars and processing requirements are defined by GB/T 18650-2008. The market often speaks of West Lake, Qiantang and Yuezhou production areas; the exact boundaries, permitted cultivars and product requirements should be checked against the current standard and geographical indication announcement. Within the West Lake area, the traditional labels Shi, Long, Yun, Hu and Mei refer to local sites and reputations, not administrative districts or uniform grades.

Cultivars

The standard lists suitable cultivars including Longjing population, Longjing 43, Longjing Changye, Yingshuang and Jiukeng. Population cultivars usually sprout later and vary more between plants; Longjing 43 sprouts early and is relatively uniform, and is widely planted. Cultivar affects sprouting time, leaf shape and aroma, but the same cultivar can taste different under different sites and processing.

Processing

Traditional Longjing is pan-fired: spreading, first firing, resting, final firing and sieving. The first firing removes moisture and begins shaping; the final firing dries, shapes and develops aroma. Both hand and machine processing can produce compliant Longjing, and the standard includes requirements for mechanical processing. Temperatures, leaf load and hand movements vary by factory, product and maker; no single set of numbers describes all Longjing.

Quality and flavour

Dry leaves are flat and straight, yellow-green to emerald. The liquor is pale green and bright, with fresh, bean or chestnut aromas and a refreshing taste; sweetness and aftertaste depend on material and processing. Aroma type, intensity and durability change with origin, cultivar, picking date and processing.

Brewing

Use a glass or gaiwan with water around 80–85°C. Add a little water first to wet the leaf, then fill along the wall and infuse briefly. Water that is too hot or an infusion that is too long can amplify bitterness. Adjust leaf quantity to taste; a glass is convenient for watching the leaves open, but it is not the only correct vessel.

History

The Classic of Tea records tea production at Tianzhu and Lingyin monasteries in Qiantang, showing that tea was documented in the Hangzhou area in the Tang dynasty. Tea culture among monasteries and literati flourished in the Song, and Longjing’s reputation expanded in the Ming and Qing. The story of the Qianlong emperor and the “eighteen imperial tea bushes” at Shifeng is widely told as local lore; its details and date still require archival and gazetteer verification. The traditional processing technique is on China’s national intangible heritage list.

Common misconceptions

  • Flat shape does not make a tea Longjing. Protected Longjing also requires the defined area, cultivars and processing.
  • “Mingqian” and “Yuqian” are picking times, not grades. Early picking does not automatically mean better quality.
  • Hand-made is not automatically better. Hand and machine processing can both produce good tea; control and material matter.
  • “Imperial tea” is cultural narrative. The Qianlong story should not be treated as verified history without sources.

West Lake tea area

West Lake Longjing is a flat green tea from around Hangzhou’s West Lake. Shifeng, Meijiawu, Longwu and Wengjiashan grow Longjing 43 and local population, with ten hand-firing motions producing green colour, rich aroma, sweet taste and beautiful shape.

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

Biluochun

Biluochun is a pan-fired spring green tea from the Dongting hills in Lake Tai, Jiangsu. The leaf is tiny, tightly curled and covered with white down; the tea is famous for a fresh, sweet, slightly fruity cup and for the tea–fruit intercropping tradition of Dongting Mountain.

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.
