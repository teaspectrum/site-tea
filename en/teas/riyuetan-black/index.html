日月潭红茶

China · Taiwan · Riyuetan Black Tea

日月潭红茶是台湾南投鱼池一带的红茶区域名，核心产区在日月潭畔的鱼池乡。日治时期从印度引入阿萨姆种，后来发展出台茶 8 号、台茶 18 号（红玉）与台茶 21 号（红韵）。红玉以肉桂与薄荷般的香气著称，茶汤橙红明亮；它不是单一品种名，也不是所有鱼池红茶都叫红玉。

Riyuetan black tea is a regional name for black tea from the Yuchi area beside Sun Moon Lake in Nantou, Taiwan. It is not a single cultivar, and not every Yuchi black tea is “Ruby.” Sun Moon Lake is the place; Ruby (TTES No. 18) is a cultivar. Keeping the two apart prevents “Riyuetan black tea” from being treated as one fixed flavour.

Origin and identity

Yuchi sits in Nantou County. The lake's mist, water-mediated climate and hilly terrain suit black tea. Official sources describe Yuchi as one of Taiwan's earliest black-tea areas and a main producing region. During the Japanese colonial period the authorities introduced Assam from India and set up a tea research station at Maolan Mountain beside the lake; Yuchi and Puli became the island's black-tea centre. Historic factories such as the Sun Moon Lake Antique Assam Tea Factory still keep the old buildings, rolling machines, breaking machines, sifting machines and fermentation rooms.

Riyuetan black tea is a fully fermented black tea, not an oolong. Products may be strip-shaped, broken or blended; the best-known premium representative is TTES No. 18 Ruby, alongside TTES No. 8 Assam and TTES No. 21 Rhythm. The cultivars differ: Assam is malty and full; Ruby has cinnamon and mint-like cooling notes; Rhythm leans toward pomelo flower, ripe fruit and honey.

History

One of the earliest written records of Taiwanese tea is the 1723 Taihai Shicha Lu, which mentions “Shuishalian tea” in the Sun Moon Lake area. In the Japanese period, after surveys, the authorities judged black tea to be the world's most consumed tea and introduced Assam from India to develop exports. Trial gardens and a research station followed at Sun Moon Lake, and Yuchi and Puli became the main black-tea region. After the 1940s the industry passed through export, decline and premium domestic phases; the old factory turned from industrial production into a tea-tourism site while keeping its Japanese-era buildings and equipment.

Ruby, TTES No. 18, was named and promoted by the Tea Research and Extension Station in 1999. It was selected from a cross between a wild Taiwanese tea and a large-leaf Burmese type. Its cinnamon and mint notes are formed by cultivar and fermentation, not added flavour. TTES No. 21 Rhythm is a separate cultivar with pomelo-flower and ripe-fruit notes. Local sources present these as representative varieties, but garden, lot and processing still create wide variation.

Craft and quality

Black tea follows picking, withering, rolling, fermentation, drying and refining. Riyuetan leaf is relatively large; rolling must break cells enough for oxidation without tearing the strip. Fermentation is the aroma key: temperature, humidity, leaf depth and time set colour and aroma type. Ruby's cinnamon-mint note forms during fermentation and drying; over-fermentation moves toward ripe fruit and fermented notes, under-fermentation leaves green notes.

Ruby is tight, dark and glossy; the liquor is orange-red and bright; the aroma has cinnamon, mint and a cooling menthol-like lift; the taste is sweet, full and returning. TTES No. 8 Assam is more malty and full, suited to milk; TTES No. 21 Rhythm is softer with pomelo-flower and honey-fruit notes. For Ruby, use about 85°C, 5 g to 90 ml in a gaiwan, 40 seconds for the first infusion and 30 seconds for the second. Boiling water amplifies astringency; too-cool water suppresses aroma.

Buying and boundaries

First, distinguish “Riyuetan producing area” from “Riyuetan brand.” The area is Yuchi and Puli, but many products use the name without origin proof. Second, identify the cultivar: Ruby is TTES No. 18, not every Riyuetan tea; Assam, Rhythm and blends have different profiles. Third, the aroma should be natural: cinnamon-mint is Ruby's varietal signature, but a perfume-like menthol blast is not. Fourth, price is not the only cultivar signal; old factories, competition awards and organic certification affect price but do not replace tasting.

Drink it plain. Ruby in particular does not suit milk, which buries the cinnamon-mint note; Assam can take milk. “Taiwan fragrance” and “world's only” are marketing language; the checkable statement is “Sun Moon Lake is one of Taiwan's core black-tea areas, and Ruby is a representative cultivar.”

Abigail's Blend

Abigail's Blend is a commemorative black-tea blend created for the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum and Abigail's Tea Room by tea master Bruce Richardson and crafted by Elmwood Inn Fine Teas. It is named for Abigail Adams. It is not the tea thrown into Boston Harbor in 1773, and it is not the “Boston Harbour Tea” commercial brand. The blend uses Chinese Yunnan black tea as its base and adds Assam, Nilgiri and Kenyan black teas. The museum's own description is that it recalls the Chinese black teas thrown overboard in 1773, “but better”. It can be served hot or iced, with or without milk. The product comes as 3.5 oz (99 g) loose tea or 14 pyramid tea bags at $17.99. On 16 December 1773, 340 chests of East India Company tea from the Beaver, Dartmouth and Eleanor were dumped into Boston Harbor, weighing over 92,000 lb (about 46 tonnes); about 80% was Chinese black tea.

American Classic Tea (Charleston Tea Garden)

American Classic Tea is the flagship black tea of Charleston Tea Garden on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina. It is made exclusively from tea grown in the United States, but it is not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic “Charleston-style” black tea. The garden's own material supplies the usable facts: 127 acres, more than 320 tea varieties, a history that runs from Charles Shepard's Pinehurst plantation in 1888 to the 1963 transplant at Wadmalaw Island, commercial operation in 1987 and Bigelow ownership from 2003. The tea itself is a larger-leaf black tea; the official brewing suggestion is 8 ounces of boiling water for 2–4 minutes, hot or iced. The page deliberately leaves cultivar and CTC/Orthodox claims out because the official pages do not publish them.

Assam Tea

Assam is the world’s largest contiguous tea-growing region, producing bold, malty black tea on the Brahmaputra plains; CTC is its everyday volume tea, while orthodox Assam offers a fuller-leaf alternative.
