Hanzhong Xianhao

China · Shaanxi

Hanzhong Xianhao is a geographical indication regional public brand for green tea in Hanzhong, Shaanxi, China. It is not a single company, a single cultivar, or a single retail SKU. In 2007, AQSIQ Announcement No. 178 placed Hanzhong Xianhao under geographical indication product protection across eight tea-producing counties, 112 townships and 894 villages. The official notice defines three fresh-leaf grades — Special, First and Second — and a seven-step process: spreading, killing the green, carding, shaping, raising the white hairs, drying and sorting. In 2024, Hanzhong had 132.4 wan mu (about 88,270 ha) of tea gardens, 71,000 tonnes of total tea output and CNY 11.07 billion in dry maocha value; Hanzhong Xianhao itself produced 1,675 tonnes worth CNY 1.98 billion. The 2025 brand value was CNY 5.098 billion, and the national geographical indication protection demonstration zone passed an on-site review in September 2025.

What it is: a regional public brand, not one company or one cultivar

“Hanzhong Xianhao” is a geographical indication regional public brand for tea in Hanzhong, Shaanxi. Its protected area covers eight tea-producing counties, 112 tea townships and 894 tea villages. Only tea grown inside that area, picked to the prescribed standard and processed by the specified method may use the name. It is not the trademark of one factory, and it is not the name of one tea cultivar.

Hanzhong also has separate regional public brands such as Hanzhong Maojian, Hanzhong Chaoqing and Hanzhong Hong. They belong to the same local industry but are not the same product. Treating “Hanzhong Xianhao” as a generic synonym for “Hanzhong green tea” blurs the boundaries of grade, origin and process.

As of September 2025, 120 tea enterprises in the city held rights to use the Hanzhong Xianhao geographical indication mark, 81.1% of the city's major producers. That is the practical meaning of a regional public brand: many authorised users, one protected name.

Origin and scale

Hanzhong sits in southwestern Shaanxi, south of the Qinling and north of the Daba Mountains, in the upper Han River valley. The 2007 protection notice sets these site conditions:

Item Official requirement
Protected area Eight tea-producing counties, 112 townships, 894 villages in Hanzhong
Altitude 600–1,400 m
Soil Sandy loam, loam or clay loam; pH 4.5–6.5
Soil depth At least 80 cm, without a hardpan
Organic matter Above 1.5%
Water table Below 1 m

These are eligibility conditions for geographical indication protection, not measurements of every individual garden. Hanzhong's counties and mountain sites differ greatly in altitude, aspect and soil. “600–1,400 m” should not be turned into a precise elevation for a particular tea garden.

The 2024 industry figures show the scale:

Indicator 2024 figure
City tea-garden area 132.4 wan mu (about 88,270 ha)
Total tea output 71,000 tonnes
Dry maocha value CNY 11.07 billion
Hanzhong Xianhao output 1,675 tonnes
Hanzhong Xianhao value CNY 1.98 billion

The 2025 brand value was CNY 5.098 billion. That is an evaluation of the regional public brand, not the revenue of one company, and it is not a retail price per kilogram.

The official grades: Special, First and Second

The 2007 notice defines three fresh-leaf grades. There is no “Third Grade” in the national geographical indication notice. Enterprise or local standards may subdivide further, but those subdivisions should not be presented as the national notice's grades.

Grade Fresh-leaf composition Length Appearance requirement
Special Single buds at least 90%; one-bud-one-leaf initial opening no more than 10% ≤2.0 cm Even, plump, intact; no purple buds, diseased buds or insect-damaged buds
First One-bud-one-leaf initial opening at least 80%; one-bud-one-leaf open no more than 20% ≤2.5 cm Even and plump; no fish leaf, leaf stalk, purple bud, diseased bud or insect-damaged bud
Second One-bud-one-leaf open at least 80%; one-bud-two-leaf initial opening no more than 20% ≤3.0 cm Even and plump; no fish leaf, leaf stalk, purple bud, diseased bud or insect-damaged bud

The official sensory descriptors are:

Grade Appearance Aroma Liquor colour Taste Leaf base
Special Slightly flat, upright, even and elegant; tender green with visible white hairs High, sharp, persistent tender aroma Tender green, clear and bright Fresh and brisk with returning sweetness Even, fresh, tender green and bright
First Slightly flat and upright; emerald green with visible white hairs High and long tender aroma Tender green, clear and bright Fresh, brisk and mellow Even, forming leaf clusters; tender green and bright
Second Slightly flat and upright; emerald green with some white hairs Clean, persistent aroma Clear green and bright Fresh, mellow and refreshing Tender green and bright

These are grade descriptors from an official notice, not a cupping score for a specific batch. For a buyer, the more useful checks are: does the pack carry the geographical indication mark, GB/T 14456.1-2008 and DB6107/T01.13-2016, and does it state the grade and producer?

Processing: seven steps, not “all handmade”

The 2007 notice specifies:

  1. Spreading: fresh leaf is spread in a cool, ventilated, shaded bamboo tray or mat, usually no more than 3 cm thick. It is turned gently every 1–2 hours and processed within 6 hours; 8–10% weight loss is the target.
  2. Killing the green: usually a small drum machine, with a drum temperature of 220–180°C. The end point is the disappearance of grassiness, the appearance of tea aroma, a darker leaf colour and about 60% moisture.
  3. Carding: a five- or seven-groove multi-function machine at 90–80°C. The leaf begins to take shape and reaches about 40% moisture.
  4. Shaping: the same machine at 80–70°C. The shape is basically set and moisture falls to 20–30%.
  5. Raising the white hairs: hand-worked in a tea-firing pan at 70–60°C, with 150–200 g of leaf per batch. The end point is visible white hairs and a fixed shape.
  6. Drying: a hot-air tea dryer. First dry at 100–90°C, rest for 30 minutes, then re-dry at 80–70°C until the leaf can be rubbed to powder and moisture is below 6%.
  7. Sorting: remove broken leaf, yellow leaf, coarse strips, old stalk and foreign matter, then store in a way that preserves freshness.

The chain includes machine killing, carding, shaping and drying as well as hand hair-raising and sorting. “All handmade” is inaccurate; “fully machine-made” is also inaccurate.

Brand timeline

Time Event Verifiable statement
2007 Geographical indication product protection AQSIQ Announcement No. 178 placed Hanzhong Xianhao under protection across eight counties, 112 townships and 894 villages.
2009 Geographical indication product mark registered The Hanzhong Tea Industry Association registered the “Hanzhong Xianhao” geographical indication product mark, registration No. 7306965.
2013 Panama International Exposition gold award The Hanzhong municipal government records a gold award at the Panama International Exposition.
2021 Demonstration zone approved for construction The National Intellectual Property Administration approved construction of the Hanzhong Xianhao national geographical indication protection demonstration zone.
September 2025 Demonstration zone passes on-site review The National Intellectual Property Administration and Shaanxi provincial administration organised an expert on-site review, which approved the zone.
November 2025 Public notice of proposed establishment The National Intellectual Property Administration published a notice proposing 54 national geographical indication protection demonstration zones, including Hanzhong Xianhao.

The table covers only the modern brand and the geographical indication system. Claims about Han-dynasty tribute tea, a Song-dynasty “tea-horse market edict” or a specific Tang-dynasty mention of Hanzhong Xianhao are not supported by the official or historical sources consulted here, so they are left out.

Common misreadings

Misreading Problem Better wording
“Hanzhong Xianhao is one company's brand” It is a regional public brand used by many authorised producers Say it is a Hanzhong geographical indication regional public brand with 120 authorised enterprises
“It has four grades: Special, First, Second and Third” The 2007 national notice lists only three Use the official three grades; attribute any finer subdivision to its own standard
“Hanzhong Xianhao is one tea cultivar” The notice regulates site, picking and process, not a single cultivar It is a regional brand and product grade, not a cultivar name
“Lu Yu's Classic of Tea names Hanzhong Xianhao” The Classic describes wild tea in the Ba and Xia river region, not the modern brand Hanzhong can be placed in the broader Ba-shan tea region, but the modern brand did not exist in the Tang dynasty
“All Hanzhong Xianhao is handmade” The official process includes machine killing, carding, shaping and drying, plus hand hair-raising and sorting Describe it as a combined machine-and-hand process
“Hanzhong Xianhao is China's northernmost quality green tea” No official definition supporting “northernmost” was found Give the Qinling–Daba location and the official altitude range without an absolute ranking

Source boundary

This page confirms that Hanzhong Xianhao is a Hanzhong geographical indication regional public brand; that AQSIQ Announcement No. 178 of 2007 protects it across eight counties, 112 townships and 894 villages; that the official notice defines Special, First and Second fresh-leaf grades and a seven-step process; that the geographical indication product mark was registered in 2009 and a Panama International Exposition gold award followed in 2013; that the national demonstration zone was approved for construction in 2021 and passed an on-site review in September 2025; that 2024 city figures were 132.4 wan mu (about 88,270 ha), 71,000 tonnes and CNY 11.07 billion, with Hanzhong Xianhao at 1,675 tonnes and CNY 1.98 billion; and that the 2025 brand value was CNY 5.098 billion.

This page does not confirm a single cultivar, the exact process parameters of every authorised producer, cupping scores for any batch, retail prices, caffeine content, or health claims. The page image is an 1875 photograph of the Hanzhong Fu tea trading market, not a modern Hanzhong tea garden.

China · Zhejiang

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

China · Jiangsu

Biluochun

Biluochun is a pan-fired spring green tea from the Dongting hills in Lake Tai, Jiangsu. The leaf is tiny, tightly curled and covered with white down; the tea is famous for a fresh, sweet, slightly fruity cup and for the tea–fruit intercropping tradition of Dongting Mountain.

Colombia

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.
