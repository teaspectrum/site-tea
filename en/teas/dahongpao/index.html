What it is

Da Hong Pao is a flagship product name in Wuyi rock tea. Under the current GB/T 18745-2006, Wuyi rock tea is divided into Da Hong Pao, Shuixian, Rougui, Mingcong and Qizhong; Da Hong Pao is one category, not a synonym for all Wuyi rock tea or for tea picked from a single mother tree. The published GB/T 18745-2026, effective 2027-04-01, further defines Da Hong Pao as a product made by blending and refining semi-finished material from several clonally propagated Wuyi rock-tea cultivars. Check the standard and product category on the label.

Origin and protected area

The geographical indication protected area is limited to Wuyishan City, Fujian, and requires suitable cultivars and traditional processing in the Wuyi mountain environment. The market terms “Zhengyan, Banyan and Zhoucha” are customary production and trade divisions; the 2006 national standard does not make them statutory grades. The new standard will adjust the origin description. Whatever the division, the product must still meet the standard's material, processing, sensory and labelling requirements.

Mother trees and commercial tea

The mother trees at Jiulongke are an important genetic and cultural resource and are protected rather than picked as ordinary commercial tea. Most Da Hong Pao on the market is a blend of several cultivars; blending is part of Wuyi rock-tea refining, not evidence of “fake tea”. Terms such as “pure-bred Da Hong Pao” and “Qidan” are cultivar and market names; they do not replace the product category, grade and label. Claims about the number of mother trees, age, annual yield and auction prices are often repeated without verification; check official and archival sources.

Processing

Traditional Wuyi rock tea goes through sun withering, cooling, shaking, fixation, rolling, initial baking, re-baking and low-temperature roasting. Shaking develops floral and fruity aromas and the “green leaf with red edge”; roasting affects roasted aroma, body and keeping quality. Commercial Da Hong Pao is also blended, homogenised and refined. Shaking frequency, pan temperature, roasting time and fire level vary with material, product and maker; no single set of numbers describes all Da Hong Pao.

Quality and flavour

Dry leaves are tightly rolled, dark brown to green-brown. The liquor is orange to orange-red, with floral, fruity, roasted, creamy or woody notes, a full body and a sweet finish. “Rock rhyme” is a traditional tasting term, not a chemical specification. Different blends, roast levels and vintages change the style significantly.

History and boundaries

The name Da Hong Pao is associated with legends such as the scholar who draped an imperial red robe over the tea bushes; these are local narratives, and the person and date are difficult to verify. Wuyi rock tea (Da Hong Pao) traditional processing is on China’s national intangible heritage list; the 2006 geographical indication standard came into effect in 2006. Mother-tree protection, commercial blending and the new standard together shape today’s market; legend, mother-tree stories and commercial tea should not be confused.

Common misconceptions

  • Da Hong Pao is not a single mother-tree product. Most commercial Da Hong Pao is a blend; the mother trees are not picked for sale.
  • “Zhengyan” is not a statutory grade in the 2006 standard. It is a customary division; check the standard and label.
  • Price and story are not quality. Auction prices and imperial legends cannot replace material, processing, storage and testing.
  • Not every rock tea is Da Hong Pao. Shuixian, Rougui, Mingcong and Qizhong are different product categories.
