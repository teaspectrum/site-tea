Baihao Yinzhen (Silver Needle)

China · Fujian · Baihao Yinzhen

Baihao Yinzhen is a white tea made from single buds, with plump, downy needles and a pale apricot liquor. The white-tea national standard defines it as a distinct category; origin, cultivar and processing shape its style.

What it is

Baihao Yinzhen is a white tea made from single buds, plump and covered with white down, straight like a needle. Under GB/T 22291-2017, it is one of the main white-tea categories; the difference from Bai Mudan, Gongmei and Shoumei begins with raw-material grade and picking standard, not simply “higher” or “lower” quality.

Origin and cultivar

It is mainly produced in Fuding and Zhenghe, Fujian, with Fuding one of the most frequently cited origins. Origin, cultivar and spring weather affect bud size, down density, colour and aroma. Dabai and Shuixian cultivars can both be used for white tea, but the exact cultivar and origin should be checked on the package and origin label, not inferred from the name “Silver Needle”.

Picking and processing

Baihao Yinzhen is usually picked from plump spring buds, avoiding rain-damaged, purple or broken buds. Processing is mainly withering and drying, without fixation or rolling. Sun withering, indoor withering and combined withering affect down aroma, sweetness, colour and keeping quality. Fine picking does not guarantee good taste; healthy material, even withering, thorough drying and clean storage matter equally.

Flavour and brewing

Young Silver Needle has a pale apricot to light yellow liquor, a fresh aroma with down, young bamboo shoot, dry grass or light floral notes, and a sweet, soft taste with low bitterness. Use water around 85–90°C, pour along the wall and infuse briefly; a gaiwan can be used for multiple infusions. Water that is too hot or an infusion that is too long flattens the sweetness.

Ageing and boundaries

White tea can be aged, but “older is better” is not a fact. Material, moisture content, storage temperature and humidity, and hygiene determine the direction of change; dampness, odours or mould can ruin the tea. Aged Silver Needle may develop date-like or medicinal notes and a deeper colour, but it should be clean, odour-free and traceable. The market category “aged white tea” is complex; age and grade cannot be judged only from a seller’s story.

Common misconceptions

  • Silver Needle is not a green tea. It is a white tea, processed mainly by withering and drying, without fixation or rolling.
  • Single bud does not automatically mean top quality. Picking standard is only one classification factor; cultivar, origin, processing and storage also matter.
  • More down does not always mean better. Down signals cultivar and tenderness, but aroma, taste and cleanliness are more direct.
  • Aged white tea needs storage evidence. Without reliable age, storage and testing information, colour or story is not enough.

Buying and keeping

When buying Baihao Yinzhen, check that the buds are even and the colour natural, then smell the dry leaf for freshness. More down does not mean better quality; sweetness, cleanliness and endurance in the cup matter more. At home, keep the tea sealed, away from light, moisture and strong odours. Divide a small amount for current drinking to reduce repeated opening. If the leaf develops a stale, sour or mouldy smell, check for dampness first rather than continuing to age it.

Fuding tea area

Fuding is the core of Fujian white tea. Taimu Mountain, Diantou, Panxi and the Fuding Dabai/Dahao cultivars make Silver Needle, White Peony, Gongmei and Shoumei; white tea is neither fired nor rolled, with withering and drying defining style.

China · Fujian

Fuding Bai Mudan

Fuding Bai Mudan is a white tea made from one bud and one or two leaves, with attached bud and leaf, a silver-white bud among green leaves and an apricot-yellow liquor. Under GB/T 22291-2017 it sits between Baihao Yinzhen and Gongmei/Shoumei in raw-material tenderness.

Fuding Gongmei

Gongmei is a white tea made under the national standard from the young shoots of population cultivars, usually less tender than Bai Mudan and more tender than Shoumei. It often has a more complex floral and sweet character; population gardens are now less common than in the past.

Fuding Shoumei

Fuding Shoumei is a white tea made from relatively mature buds and leaves, often one bud with two or three leaves or coarser material. It has a deeper colour and mellow taste, suits daily drinking and ageing, but “older is better” is not a fact; storage and material matter.
