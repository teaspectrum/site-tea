Brazil

Vale do Ribeira Black Tea

Vale do Ribeira black tea is a regional black-tea tradition in southern São Paulo state, centred on Registro, Pariquera-Açu and Sete Barras. It is not a single brand, cultivar or standardized process. In 1913 Torazo Okamoto brought Assam seedlings from Sri Lanka; in the 1930s Japanese immigrant growers expanded the valley to about 5,000 ha, 600 producers and 40 factories, with roughly 90% exported. The 1980s crisis closed many factories. A 1984 state law gave Registro the title “State Tea Capital”, and the city exported more than US$68 million of tea between 1997 and 2023. Three current cases are Amaya's non-orthodox, export-oriented production; Sítio Shimada's organic handmade black tea; and Sítio Yamamaru's agroforestry smallholding. Public sources do not give one regional cultivar, process or sensory standard, so “CTC strong liquor” is not a safe description of the whole valley.

Vietnam

Vietnamese Jasmine Tea

Vietnamese jasmine tea is green tea scented with fresh jasmine blossoms. It is the everyday scented tea of Vietnam: jasmine is picked when its perfume peaks in the evening, layered with dried green tea, and traditionally about 1 kg of flowers scents 1 kg of tea. The result is sweet, clean and light, at home in a teapot or a glass of ice.

Vietnam

Vietnamese Lotus Tea

Vietnamese lotus tea is green tea scented with lotus. The traditional centre is Quảng An village on Hanoi’s West Lake, where makers use the hundred-petal Bach Diep lotus. The high-aroma method gathers the flower anthers, layers them with Thái Nguyên green tea and repeats scenting and drying for roughly 15–21 days; a simpler method stuffs tea into a fresh lotus overnight. The best cups are lightly astringent, sweet at the finish and cleanly floral.

China · Fujian

Wuyi Rougui

Wuyi Rougui is a Wuyi rock tea made from the Rougui cultivar, known for a pungent cinnamon-like aroma with floral and fruity notes. Under GB/T 18745-2006, Rougui is one of the five Wuyi rock-tea product categories; Zhengyan and Banyan are trade divisions, not statutory 2006 grades.

China · Fujian

Wuyi Shuixian

Wuyi Shuixian is a Wuyi rock tea made from the Shuixian cultivar, with a full body and often an orchid aroma; old-bush tea may show woody and mossy notes. Under GB/T 18745-2006 it is one of five Wuyi rock-tea categories; Zhengyan is a trade division, not a 2006 statutory grade.

China · Shaanxi

Xixiang Wuzi Xianhao

Wuzi Xianhao is a strip-shaped green tea from Xixiang County, Shaanxi, standardised in 1985 and named by tea scholar Chen Chuan. Xixiang officials often call it a predecessor of Hanzhong Xianhao, but it is not a separate geographical indication today: Hanzhong began consolidating local tea brands in 2005, and the geographical indication approved in 2007 is “Hanzhong Xianhao”. In 2024 the county's tea-industry figures were 360,000 mu of tea gardens, 20,100 tonnes of dry maocha and CNY 2.946 billion in output value; the county statistical communiqué gives 343,500 mu and 16,780 tonnes. The two sets of figures use different reporting systems and should not be mixed.

Japan

Yame Gyokuro

Yame gyokuro is gyokuro produced in the Yame region of Fukuoka Prefecture, with Kuroki, Kamitsuyo and Hoshino as core districts. Like Uji gyokuro, it is a shade-grown green tea, but Yame emphasises rice-straw shading, hand picking and the narrower GI-protected name Yame Dento Hon Gyokuro. “Hand-picked” is not the same as “hand-rolled”: hand-rolled tea is a separate craft, not the standard gyokuro process.

Japan

Yame Matcha

Yame matcha is matcha made from tea grown and processed in the Yame region of Fukuoka Prefecture. It is a place-plus-style name, not a cultivar or a protected grade. Yame is better known for gyokuro; matcha and tencha are a separate line from the same region. The style is usually described as deep in umami, sweet and relatively low in bitterness, but the details depend on cultivar, shading and milling.

Argentina

Yerba Mate

Yerba mate is an infusion of Ilex paraguariensis, a South American holly, not a Camellia sinensis tea. Argentina calls it the national infusion and grows most of it in Misiones and Corrientes. The same leaf is drunk hot from a gourd, cold as tereré, or boiled and strained as mate cocido.

Argentina

Yerba Mate with Stems

“Con palo” is stemmed yerba mate: dried leaf blended with a controlled share of young stems. The stems soften the cup, add a woody sweetness and keep the bombilla drawing evenly. Argentina’s geographical indication caps stems at 35% and requires at least 65% leaf.

China · Fujian

Zhengshan Xiaozhong

Zhengshan Xiaozhong is a small-leaf black tea from Tongmu Pass, Wuyishan, Fujian, traditionally pine-smoked and known for pine smoke and longan sweetness. It is one of China’s earliest black teas to reach Europe, but “all black tea originated here” is an overstatement.

China · Shaanxi

Ziyang Maojian

Ziyang Maojian is a traditional famous tea from Ziyang County, Shaanxi, and a registered geographical-indication certification trademark. Today it usually appears as a green-tea product under the regional public brand “Ziyang Selenium-Rich Tea”; the 2004 protected product and the 2023 protection upgrade cover “Ziyang Selenium-Rich Tea”, not the four characters “Ziyang Maojian”. In 2024 the county reported 260,000 mu of tea gardens, 12,800 tonnes of tea, CNY 3.55 billion in output value and CNY 7 billion in composite output. These are industry figures, not statistical-communiqué figures.
