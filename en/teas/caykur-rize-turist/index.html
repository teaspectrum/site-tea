Çaykur Rize Turist

Türkiye

Çaykur Rize Turist is a current black-tea product from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. It is not a tea-tourism experience or a fictional tourist souvenir. The official 200 g page calls it a family everyday tea, uses the slogan “The Real Yellow”, and lists 100, 200, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 g packs with 24 pieces per carton. The 200 g box is listed at 90 TL; the 1,000 g page is listed at 409 TL, with 12 pieces per carton and 1.25 kg per piece. Çaykur also sells a separate 200 g “Rize Turist Demlik Süzen Poşet Çay” tea-bag version at 121.50 TL. The official copy does not state a cultivar, a single estate, orthodox or CTC processing, a harvest season, or brewing parameters, so those cannot be presented as established facts about Rize Turist.

What it is: a current black tea, not a “tea-tourism experience”

Read literally, “Rize Turist” can sound like a tourist product or a tea-tourism experience in Rize. Çaykur's official page defines it differently: a family everyday black tea that appears in every household and every gathering. The slogan is “The Real Yellow”, and the copy speaks of a long history and an aroma passed down through generations. “Turist” is part of the product name, not a product category or a tour itinerary.

Rize Turist belongs to Çaykur's black-tea line. Çaykur is Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise, but the official page does not describe Rize Turist as a single-estate tea, a highland ancient-tree tea, or a whole-leaf orthodox black tea. Without a new official source, those descriptions should not be treated as product facts.

The 200 g box and the 200 g tea bag are not the same product

Çaykur's official category page also lists Rize Turist Demlik Süzen Poşet Çay 200 Gr. at 121.50 TL. That is a 200 g tea-bag version, with different packaging and contents from the 200 g loose-leaf box.

The simplest way to tell them apart is to read the product name:

  • Rize Turist Tea / Rize Turist Çayı: loose-leaf box, commonly 100, 200, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 g.
  • Rize Turist Demlik Süzen Poşet Çay: tea-bag version, with “Demlik Süzen Poşet” in the name.

Both carry the Rize Turist name, but they are not the same SKU. Applying the tea-bag price, weight or brewing method to the loose-leaf box would produce the wrong conclusion.

What the official page says — and does not say

The official pages confirm that Rize Turist is a family everyday black tea; that it comes in several pack sizes; that the 200 g box is listed at 90 TL, with 24 pieces per carton and 0.5 kg per piece; that the 1,000 g pack is listed at 409 TL, with 12 pieces per carton and 1.25 kg per piece; and that Çaykur positions it as an economic quality tea under the slogan “The Real Yellow”.

The official pages do not give:

  • a tea cultivar or a blend ratio;
  • a single estate, a specified altitude or a harvest season;
  • orthodox, CTC or another specific rolling/cutting process;
  • a leaf-grade code;
  • caffeine content;
  • official grams, water temperature or steeping time;
  • a first launch year.

This page therefore does not present “whole-leaf orthodox”, “single estate in the Ayder highlands”, “project started in 2015 and launched in 2017” or “far-infrared aroma setting” as facts. None of those claims appears in the official material consulted here.

How to brew it: the usual Turkish double-pot method

Çaykur does not publish a product-specific brewing recipe for Rize Turist. The common Turkish double-pot method can be used:

  1. Fill the lower pot with water and bring it to a boil.
  2. Put tea in the upper pot, add a little boiling water to rinse and settle the leaf, then set the upper pot over the lower pot to heat by steam.
  3. Let the upper pot steep long enough to build strength, usually 10–15 minutes, adjusting to the amount of leaf and personal taste.
  4. Pour the strong upper tea into a tulip-shaped glass, filling about one-third to one-half of the glass, then top up with hot water from the lower pot.
  5. Taste without sugar first, then add sugar cubes if desired.

This is a general Turkish tea method, not a Çaykur specification for Rize Turist. The official copy calls it a family tea and an economic quality choice; it does not give a dedicated leaf weight or steeping time.

Abigail's Blend

Abigail's Blend is a commemorative black-tea blend created for the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum and Abigail's Tea Room by tea master Bruce Richardson and crafted by Elmwood Inn Fine Teas. It is named for Abigail Adams. It is not the tea thrown into Boston Harbor in 1773, and it is not the “Boston Harbour Tea” commercial brand. The blend uses Chinese Yunnan black tea as its base and adds Assam, Nilgiri and Kenyan black teas. The museum's own description is that it recalls the Chinese black teas thrown overboard in 1773, “but better”. It can be served hot or iced, with or without milk. The product comes as 3.5 oz (99 g) loose tea or 14 pyramid tea bags at $17.99. On 16 December 1773, 340 chests of East India Company tea from the Beaver, Dartmouth and Eleanor were dumped into Boston Harbor, weighing over 92,000 lb (about 46 tonnes); about 80% was Chinese black tea.

American Classic Tea (Charleston Tea Garden)

American Classic Tea is the flagship black tea of Charleston Tea Garden on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina. It is made exclusively from tea grown in the United States, but it is not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic “Charleston-style” black tea. The garden's own material supplies the usable facts: 127 acres, more than 320 tea varieties, a history that runs from Charles Shepard's Pinehurst plantation in 1888 to the 1963 transplant at Wadmalaw Island, commercial operation in 1987 and Bigelow ownership from 2003. The tea itself is a larger-leaf black tea; the official brewing suggestion is 8 ounces of boiling water for 2–4 minutes, hot or iced. The page deliberately leaves cultivar and CTC/Orthodox claims out because the official pages do not publish them.

Assam Tea

Assam is the world’s largest contiguous tea-growing region, producing bold, malty black tea on the Brahmaputra plains; CTC is its everyday volume tea, while orthodox Assam offers a fuller-leaf alternative.
