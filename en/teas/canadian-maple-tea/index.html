JusTea Canadian Maple Tea

Canada

JusTea Canadian Maple is a maple-flavoured black tea from JusTea, a Vancouver tea company. Its base is Rainforest Alliance-certified Kenyan black tea, blended with real Quebec maple syrup flakes, safflower petals and natural maple flavour. The point is that the maple is real: the blend uses maple flakes rather than an artificial maple extract, and the official brewing advice is one teaspoon, 175°F water, three minutes, about 50 mg caffeine per cup, hot, iced or with milk. The old draft's claims about Canada's “most classic” maple tea, a 1980s Toronto or Montreal origin story and a 2017 Canada 150 edition have no source and are gone.

What this tea is

“Canadian maple tea” sounds like a national category. In practice it is a flavour direction: black tea with maple sugar or maple flavour. JusTea Canadian Maple is one concrete product in that direction, not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic Canadian tea tradition.

The important distinction is between real maple and maple-flavoured tea. JusTea's official launch note is explicit: the blend contains real Quebec maple syrup flakes and natural maple flavour, but no artificial maple extract and no concentrate. The flakes dissolve gradually during the steep, so the sweetness arrives in layers. An artificial maple flavour tends to give a flat, immediate sweetness that fades as the cup cools.

This page is about the JusTea version only. Canada has other “maple tea” products with different bases, different maple content and different flavour systems. One product's spec sheet is not a category standard.

The blend: Kenyan tea, Quebec maple, safflower

JusTea lists four components:

Component What the official material says
Tea base Black tea from smallholder mashamba in Kenya's Western Highlands, Rainforest Alliance-certified
Maple Real Quebec maple syrup flakes plus natural maple flavour
Petals Hand-picked safflower petals, mainly for visual colour
Caffeine About 50 mg per cup, roughly half a standard black coffee

The most common mistake is to turn “maple” into “maple syrup poured over tea.” JusTea uses maple flakes in a dry blend, not a wet syrup infusion. It is genuinely maple, but it is not a maple-syrup drink.

The base is not a single estate. It is a farmer-direct network of smallholder farms. Rainforest Alliance certification is a farm and supply-chain certification; it is not the same as organic certification, and the page does not claim organic status.

Brewing: 175°F, three minutes

The official brew is precise:

  • one teaspoon of loose leaf per cup;
  • 175°F / about 79°C water;
  • three minutes;
  • six minutes for a second steep;
  • about 50 mg caffeine per cup.

175°F is not boiling. Brewing it with fully boiling water will push the black tea's tannins forward and flatten the maple top notes. The lower temperature is there to keep the cup smooth.

JusTea describes the result as smooth enough to drink straight, strong enough to carry through milk, and stable over ice. That is a useful guide, but do not invent a tasting note of caramel, toffee or vanilla: the official page does not publish one.

Three ways to drink it:

  1. Straight — one teaspoon, 175°F, three minutes, no sugar first.
  2. With milk — brew slightly stronger, then add dairy or oat milk; the official note says the maple carries through both.
  3. Iced — brew normally, remove the leaf, chill or pour over ice. There is no fixed iced recipe; adjust sweetness and ice to taste.

What “Canadian” means here

The Canadian element has three layers:

  • JusTea is a Vancouver company, founded in 2013, working farmer-direct in Kenya;
  • the maple flakes come from Quebec;
  • the product is developed and sold under a Canadian brand.

It does not mean the tea is grown in Canada. Canada is not a commercial tea-producing country, and JusTea's base is Kenyan. Calling this “Canadian-grown black tea” confuses the brand country, the ingredient country and the flavour source.

Quebec's maple industry gives useful context: the provincial producers' association reports 58.6 million taps, 8,400 maple enterprises and 229.5 million pounds of syrup in 2026. That is an industry-scale fact, not proof that every maple tea uses the same proportion of maple.

What it is not

The old draft carried several claims that read like common knowledge but had no traceable source:

Old claim Problem Better wording
“Canada's most classic maple tea” No national ranking or industry census A JusTea maple-flavoured black tea
“Created by Toronto/Montreal tea merchants in the 1980s–90s” No earliest-recipe record Do not name an inventor; describe JusTea's product
“2017 Canada 150 limited edition” No such version on the official pages Do not write a commemorative edition
“Black tea soaked in maple syrup” The official blend uses flakes and natural flavour Maple-flake blend, not a wet syrup tea
“Tea grown in Canada” The base is Kenyan Canadian brand, Kenyan base, Quebec maple

The common thread is a shortcut: maple is a Canadian symbol, so the product gets promoted into a Canadian tea tradition. Maple is a Quebec industry, tea is a Kenyan crop, and the brand is Vancouver-based. All three can be true at once without substituting for one another.

Who it suits

This tea suits someone who wants a maple-flavoured black tea with real maple flakes, not a sweetened syrup drink. The base is whole-leaf Kenyan black tea, the brew is deliberately gentle, and it works straight, with milk or over ice.

It is less suitable for someone looking for a strong, fast-brewing breakfast tea or a cup as sweet as maple syrup. The maple is a flavour layer, not a sugar replacement.

Source boundary

Confirmed here: the product name, 50 g loose-leaf pack, Rainforest Alliance Kenyan black tea base, Quebec maple flakes, safflower petals, natural maple flavour, about 50 mg caffeine per cup, one teaspoon/175°F/three minutes, milk and iced options.

Canadian Icewine Tea (Icewine-Flavoured Black Tea)

Canadian Icewine Tea is a flavoured black-tea blend sold in Canada, not icewine and not tea grown in a Canadian garden. The base is usually Sri Lankan or other black tea, combined with icewine, natural flavours or grape flavour; formulations vary, and some contain real Ontario icewine while others use flavour only. Ontario Icewine itself is regulated: grapes must be naturally frozen on the vine, picked at -8°C or lower and pressed immediately, with juice at least 35 Brix. The old draft's claims of a Canadian tea garden, a single Niagara origin and fixed Darjeeling/Fuding bases had no source and have been rewritten.
