Fengqing Dianhong

China · Yunnan

What it is

Fengqing Dianhong is a congou black tea from Fengqing, Yunnan, made from large-leaf tea. “Dianhong” is the common name for Yunnan black tea. It is a black tea, not Pu-erh: both may use large-leaf material, but their processing and product types are different. Dianhong is produced in several Yunnan areas; Fengqing is one of the most representative.

Origin and cultivar

Fengqing lies in Lincang, Yunnan, at relatively high elevation with a mild, humid climate suited to large-leaf tea. Yunnan large-leaf material has plump buds and relatively high polyphenol content, giving a strong body and visible golden tips. Lincang, Baoshan and Xishuangbanna also produce Dianhong; altitude, cultivar and picking season change aroma and strength. Check origin, material, standard and grade on the label.

Processing

Dianhong uses congou black-tea processing: withering, rolling, fermentation, drying, refining, sieving and blending. Large-leaf material ferments relatively quickly; temperature, humidity and time must be controlled to avoid under-fermented green notes or over-fermented sourness. Drying and baking affect sweetness, fire character and keeping quality; factories differ in parameters.

Quality and flavour

Dry leaves are plump, tightly rolled, dark and glossy, with visible golden tips. Liquor is bright red, with a high aroma, often honey, floral and sweet notes, and a strong, fresh taste; astringency and strength are usually more pronounced than in small-leaf black teas. It is often drunk with milk, where the tea remains noticeable. More golden tips or buds do not automatically mean better; aroma, taste, body and cleanliness need balanced judgement.

History and boundaries

Old records say Dianhong was trial-produced in Fengqing in the late 1930s by Feng Shaoqiu and others, then exported; they also mention 1958 “foreign affairs tea” and a 1986 gift to the British queen. These stories are widely repeated in Yunnan tea history, but exact years, batches and gift details need checking against gazetteers, archives and company records. Dianhong played an important role in twentieth-century Chinese black-tea exports, and Fengqing is a core origin.

Common misconceptions

  • Dianhong is not Pu-erh. Dianhong is black tea; Pu-erh is a post-fermented/dark tea with different processing.
  • Large-leaf does not automatically mean better. Material, picking, fermentation and drying decide quality.
  • More golden tips is not a higher grade. Golden tips signal cultivar and tenderness, not quality alone.
  • Is all Yunnan black tea Fengqing Dianhong? No. Fengqing is a representative origin; other Yunnan areas also produce Dianhong.

Abigail's Blend

Abigail's Blend is a commemorative black-tea blend created for the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum and Abigail's Tea Room by tea master Bruce Richardson and crafted by Elmwood Inn Fine Teas. It is named for Abigail Adams. It is not the tea thrown into Boston Harbor in 1773, and it is not the “Boston Harbour Tea” commercial brand. The blend uses Chinese Yunnan black tea as its base and adds Assam, Nilgiri and Kenyan black teas. The museum's own description is that it recalls the Chinese black teas thrown overboard in 1773, “but better”. It can be served hot or iced, with or without milk. The product comes as 3.5 oz (99 g) loose tea or 14 pyramid tea bags at $17.99. On 16 December 1773, 340 chests of East India Company tea from the Beaver, Dartmouth and Eleanor were dumped into Boston Harbor, weighing over 92,000 lb (about 46 tonnes); about 80% was Chinese black tea.

American Classic Tea (Charleston Tea Garden)

American Classic Tea is the flagship black tea of Charleston Tea Garden on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina. It is made exclusively from tea grown in the United States, but it is not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic “Charleston-style” black tea. The garden's own material supplies the usable facts: 127 acres, more than 320 tea varieties, a history that runs from Charles Shepard's Pinehurst plantation in 1888 to the 1963 transplant at Wadmalaw Island, commercial operation in 1987 and Bigelow ownership from 2003. The tea itself is a larger-leaf black tea; the official brewing suggestion is 8 ounces of boiling water for 2–4 minutes, hot or iced. The page deliberately leaves cultivar and CTC/Orthodox claims out because the official pages do not publish them.

Assam Tea

Assam is the world’s largest contiguous tea-growing region, producing bold, malty black tea on the Brahmaputra plains; CTC is its everyday volume tea, while orthodox Assam offers a fuller-leaf alternative.
