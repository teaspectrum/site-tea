Pingli Jiaogulan

China · Shaanxi

Pingli Jiaogulan is a geographical indication product from Pingli County in Ankang, Shaanxi. Its raw material is the aerial part of Gynostemma pentaphyllum, a perennial cucurbitaceous vine — not a tea plant (Camellia sinensis) and not a medicine. AQSIQ Announcement No. 189 of 2004 protects the whole county and sets site conditions of 400–1,600 m altitude, pH 6.0–7.5 and organic matter above 2.0%. The 2019 Shaanxi food safety standard divides the product into longxu tips, leaves and stems, recommends no more than 6 g a day, and lists pregnant or nursing women and children under 14 as unsuitable. In 2024 Pingli had 5 wan mu (about 3,330 ha) of jiaogulan, 6,500 tonnes of dried tea and CNY 700 million in output value; the 2025 brand value was CNY 934 million.

What it is: not a tea plant, not a medicine

Pingli Jiaogulan is a geographical indication product from Pingli County in Ankang, Shaanxi. Its raw material is the perennial herbaceous vine Gynostemma pentaphyllum (Cucurbitaceae), not the tea plant Camellia sinensis (Theaceae). It therefore belongs with herbal infusions and “substitute teas”, not with green, black or dark tea.

This is not a semantic quarrel. Tea-plant products are discussed through fresh-leaf picking, kill-green, rolling, fermentation and drying. Jiaogulan is discussed through the harvest of vines, leaves and “longxu” tips, and through kill-green or drying under food-ingredient rules. Calling Pingli Jiaogulan “Shaanxi green tea” or “Pingli green tea” gets the plant family, the product class and the regulatory category wrong at the same time.

It is also not a medicine. The local standard treats it as a food ingredient. This page covers identity, origin, processing, flavour and drinking boundaries; it does not offer health or therapeutic conclusions.

Origin: the whole county, 400–1,600 m

Pingli County lies in southeastern Shaanxi, in the southeastern part of Ankang, on the northern slopes of the Daba Mountains in the central Qinling–Daba belt. The county falls from south to north: high mountains in the south, hills and river terraces in the north. That range makes “Pingli” a county-level origin, not the name of one fixed hillside.

On 13 December 2004, the former General Administration of Quality Supervision, Inspection and Quarantine issued Announcement No. 189, placing Pingli Jiaogulan under origin-area product protection. The protected area is the entire administrative territory of Pingli County, and the announcement sets these cultivation conditions:

Item 2004 announcement
Protected area The entire administrative territory of Pingli County
Altitude 400–1,600 m
Soil Sandy or loam soil; pH 6.0–7.5; organic matter above 2.0%
Terrain Level, well drained and irrigated, deep fertile soil
Sowing Once temperature is stable above 10°C; 0.5–1 kg per mu; 8,000–12,000 plants per mu
Field care Soil moisture 70–80%; manual weeding; chemical herbicides prohibited

These are eligibility conditions for geographical indication protection, not measured values for every field in the county. Altitude, aspect and soil vary widely between Pingli’s townships. “400–1,600 m” should not be read as the exact elevation of any one bag of product.

The announcement also gives a quality characteristic: “green colour, dense longxu, selenium at or above 0.02 mg/kg”. That is one of the original minimum quality thresholds, not the full definition of today’s “selenium-rich” label; the relationship is set out below.

Plant and product cuts: tips, leaves and stems

Jiaogulan is a perennial herbaceous vine with fine tendrils at the nodes and bird’s-foot-shaped compound leaves. Cultivated plants usually carry five or seven leaflets. “Five-leaf” and “seven-leaf” refer to the number of leaflets on a leaf stalk, not to a tea cultivar and not to a grade.

The 2019 Shaanxi food safety local standard DBS61/0019-2019, Food safety local standard — Pingli Jiaogulan, divides cultivated Pingli Jiaogulan by harvest part into three products:

Product Harvest part Standard process Key point
Longxu tips The top 6–12 cm of the tender shoot, including tendril, young stem and young leaf Sort, wash, kill-green, dry “Longxu” is a trade name, not simply the botanical tendril
Leaves Fresh leaves from the middle to upper part of the vine, between two-fifths and four-fifths of the plant Sort, wash, kill-green, dry No old stems, yellow leaves or foreign matter
Stems and leaves The whole vine from 15 cm above the ground Sort, wash, dry This class may skip kill-green

These are not a simple “higher grade costs more” ladder. Longxu picks the youngest tip, so yields are lower and the pieces are finer. Leaves and stems come from the middle and upper vine or the whole vine, so volumes are larger and the pieces coarser. All three are sold as “jiaogulan tea”, but their brewing behaviour, pack sizes and prices differ; identify the class before comparing.

Processing: kill-green is not universal

The local standard gives a minimum process path. Longxu and leaves must be sorted, washed, kill-greened and dried. Stems and leaves need only sorting, washing and drying, and may skip kill-green. Companies may add rolling, shaping, sieving, baking or ultrafine milling on top of the standard, but “all Pingli Jiaogulan is kill-greened” is not a standard requirement.

Two processing techniques promoted in Pingli are steam kill-green and ring-pipe hot-air drying. Steam kill-green uses a short high-temperature steam pass to curb enzymatic oxidation and reduce the grassy note; the ring-pipe hot-air drier is used for more even drying. Temperature, timing and leaf load are company parameters, and public sources do not give one set of values, so this page does not invent them.

Quality and flavour: what the standard says

DBS61/0019-2019 sets these sensory requirements for the three products:

Product Shape Colour Aroma and taste
Longxu tips Fine, tight, curled, fluffy Green to yellow-green Lasting tender aroma; fresh and sweet, or slightly bitter with a sweet finish
Leaves Curled, compact Green to dark green Lasting clean aroma; slightly bitter and refreshing, or slightly bitter and mellow
Stems and leaves Curled, fluffy Yellow to yellow-green Lasting clean aroma; slightly bitter and fresh, or slightly bitter and mellow

The standard also sets chemical minima and limits: total saponins at least 0.4 g/100 g for longxu, 0.5 g/100 g for leaves and 0.4 g/100 g for stems and leaves; moisture no more than 10.0 g/100 g; total ash no more than 10.0 g/100 g for longxu and leaves, and 12.0 g/100 g for stems and leaves. These are product-compliance metrics, not “effective doses”; a saponin number cannot be used to infer what drinking the infusion will do.

Selenium labelling is easier to confuse. The 2004 announcement says “selenium at or above 0.02 mg/kg”, a geographical-indication quality characteristic. The 2019 food standard separates three labels:

Label Selenium (mg/kg)
Jiaogulan 0–4.0
Selenium-containing jiaogulan 0.075–4.0
Selenium-rich jiaogulan 0.15–4.0

So “Pingli Jiaogulan” is an origin and site-condition concept, while “selenium-rich jiaogulan” is a more specific label grade. Pingli sits in Ankang’s selenium-rich belt, and local reports often use that to explain selenium levels, but buyers still need to check which grade is printed on the pack. “From Pingli” does not automatically mean “selenium-rich”, and “selenium-rich” is not an efficacy promise.

This table contains only modern institutional milestones with a public record. Earlier folk-drinking stories, Qing-era tribute claims and 1980s industry-start dates are not written here as fixed dates because this page has not found a complete official text that supports them.

Industry: 5 wan mu, CNY 700 million, CNY 934 million brand value

Pingli treats tea and jiaogulan as two parallel specialty industries, and their figures should be read separately. A May 2025 case study from the Shaanxi Provincial Development and Reform Commission gives 20 wan mu of tea and 5 wan mu of jiaogulan in Pingli, with CNY 5.8 billion in tea-chain output value for 2024. Jiaogulan is a different series. Treating the CNY 5.8 billion as jiaogulan output would merge two industries. Tea-area figures also vary between statistical dates, so they should not be added to the jiaogulan area.

Public reports give several adjacent jiaogulan figures:

Date and source Area Output Value
August 2024, China Economic Net 5 wan mu (about 3,330 ha) 6,500 tonnes of dried tea CNY 700 million
March 2025, China Net report 5 wan mu More than 7,000 tonnes Above CNY 700 million; about CNY 7,000 per mu
November 2025, Shaanxi Provincial Government 5 wan mu — Annual output value above CNY 700 million

Area is stable at about 5 wan mu, output falls between 6,500 and 7,000-plus tonnes, and value is around CNY 700 million. The figures have different statistical dates, so they should not be added together, and “CNY 7,000 per mu” is not a guaranteed return for every field.

A June 2025 reply from the Ankang Municipal People’s Congress adds two brand figures: 12 enterprises authorised to use the geographical indication mark, and a 2025 brand value of CNY 934 million. Brand value is an assessment, not company revenue and not a retail price. Reports of March and November 2025 also mention four enterprises with annual output above CNY 50 million and 30 above CNY 1 million, making longxu tea, ultrafine powder, teabags, jiaogulan noodles and more than 20 processed products.

Drinking boundaries and common confusions

Under DBS61/0019-2019, the recommended maximum is 6 g a day. Pregnant women, nursing women and children under 14 are listed as unsuitable. That boundary applies to the food-ingredient products covered by the standard, not to jiaogulan extracts, compound formulations or medicines treated as ordinary tea.

This page gives no fixed brewing numbers. Longxu, leaves and stems differ in shape and extraction speed; the pack label’s dose, water temperature and steeping time come first. Applying “3 g, 85°C, 3 minutes” to every Pingli Jiaogulan product would treat different products as one tea.

Common claim Problem More accurate wording
“Pingli Jiaogulan is a kind of tea plant” It is a cucurbitaceous herbaceous vine, not a Theaceae tea plant Pingli Jiaogulan is a geographical-indication herbal infusion, not a green tea
“Longxu means tea buds” “Longxu” is the trade name for the top 6–12 cm tender shoot of jiaogulan Longxu is a harvest part and product name, not a tea bud
“Pingli Jiaogulan is the same as Pingli Nüwa tea” They are parallel industries, with separate area, output and value figures Tea and jiaogulan are counted separately; jiaogulan had 5 wan mu, 6,500 tonnes and CNY 700 million in 2024
“The geographical indication belongs to one company” It is a regional public brand; 12 enterprises were authorised in June 2025 Write “geographical indication regional public brand”, not “one company’s exclusive mark”
“Selenium-rich means it works” Selenium-rich is a content label, not an efficacy claim Check the label grade and test basis; do not treat selenium content as a therapeutic claim
“Wild is always better than cultivated” The local standard covers only cultivated five- or seven-leaflet jiaogulan in Pingli County Wild material is a separate question and cannot be graded under the cultivated-product standard

Source boundary

This page can confirm that Pingli Jiaogulan is a geographical indication product of Pingli County; that AQSIQ Announcement No. 189 of 2004 protects the entire county and sets conditions of 400–1,600 m, pH 6.0–7.5 and organic matter above 2.0%; that the geographical indication certification trademark was registered in 2011; that DBS61/0019-2019, issued in 2019, divides the product into longxu tips, leaves and stems, sets a 6 g daily maximum and lists unsuitable groups; that DB61/T 1684-2023 was issued in 2023; that Pingli had 5 wan mu, 6,500 tonnes of dried tea and CNY 700 million in output value in 2024; and that the 2025 brand value was CNY 934 million, with 12 enterprises authorised to use the geographical indication mark.

It cannot confirm the measured saponins, selenium or sensory scores of any one batch; the steam-kill-green temperature, timing or rolling parameters of any company; the caffeine content of Pingli Jiaogulan; any health, therapeutic or weight-loss effect; or a “world/China number one” ranking. Argentina

Argentine Tereré

Tereré is yerba mate brewed with cold water and ice. It uses the same leaf and often the same bombilla as hot mate, but the lower temperature gives a lighter, sweeter, less bitter drink. In Argentina it is a summer habit, not a separate tea plant.

Canada

Canadian Boreal Herbal Tea

Canadian boreal herbal tea is not a fixed national recipe and not Camellia sinensis tea. It is a working label for caffeine-free infusions made from plants of Canada's boreal forest, most commonly Labrador tea (Rhododendron groenlandicum / R. tomentosum) and spring spruce tips (Picea glauca / P. mariana), with balsam fir and pine sometimes added. It has a long Indigenous and settler drinking tradition, but Labrador tea contains ledol and grayanotoxins; excessive or badly prepared infusions carry real risk. It is not a detox tea and not a substitute for medicine.

Thailand

Doi Pui Herbal Tea

Doi Pui is a Hmong village inside Doi Suthep-Pui National Park near Chiang Mai, not a verified green-tea growing area. The traceable “Cha Doi Pui” product is an herbal blend built on jiaogulan, mulberry, stevia, ginseng, safflower and ginkgo — a botanical infusion, not tea from Camellia sinensis. The old Chinese draft invented a university tea plantation; that claim has been removed.
