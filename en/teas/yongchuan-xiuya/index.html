永川秀芽

China · Chongqing · Yongchuan Xiuya Green Tea

永川秀芽是重庆永川的针形名优绿茶，因“形秀、色绿、香清、味醇”得名。1959 年重庆市农科院茶叶研究所开始研制手工针形名茶，后经陈椽教授指导定名；2005 年获国家发明专利，2010 年获地理标志证明商标。它比同纬度江浙茶区早 20–25 天开采，是重庆早市名优茶的代表。

Yongchuan Xiuya is a needle-shaped green tea from Yongchuan, Chongqing. The name is literal: “Xiu” for elegant shape, “Ya” for tender bud. It is not a generic name for Chongqing green tea, and not any bud tea rolled into a needle. It has a geographical indication certification mark, a group standard, a list of licensed producers and a quality definition built around “elegant shape, green colour, clear aroma and mellow taste.”

Origin and identity

Yongchuan lies in western Chongqing in a humid mid-subtropical monsoon climate: distinct seasons, ample rain and a long frost-free period. The annual mean is about 17.9°C, rainfall about 1,034.9 mm, sunshine about 1,362.5 hours and frost-free days about 317. Some 96.8% of the district is hills and low mountains, with soil pH mostly 4.5–5.5. Spring warms early, so the gardens pick 20–25 days earlier than comparable latitudes in Zhejiang and Anhui — an “early-market” advantage.

It is a green tea, not a black tea or a scented tea. The standard defines it as a needle- or strip-shaped famous green tea made from young buds in Yongchuan, processed locally by spreading, fixing, rolling, carding and drying, with “elegant shape, green colour, clear aroma and mellow taste.” The raw material is one bud with one or two leaves, or equally tender shoots.

History

Tea in Yongchuan goes back to the Qin and Han, but Yongchuan Xiuya is a modern 20th-century famous tea. In 1959 the Chongqing Academy of Agricultural Sciences tea institute (then the Sichuan tea institute) began making handmade needle-shaped tea. After stages of creation, improvement and refinement, the tea was named “Yongchuan Xiuya” under the guidance of the tea scientist Chen Chuan. It was once called “Yinfeng”; in the early 1960s it was tasted and praised during an inspection of the Jishan tea garden. In the mid-1970s the district built more than 500 hectares of gardens and more than 50 factories, beginning industrial scale. The making process received a national invention patent in 2005; the geographical indication certification mark followed in 2010; in 2019 Yongchuan was named a national distinctive agricultural product (tea) advantage zone; in 2021 it ranked first among Chongqing tea-producing districts.

These dates come from the Yongchuan Xiuya website and the district agriculture committee. They are local brand narratives that show craft continuity, not a substitute for independent archival history.

Craft and quality

The standard flow is spreading, fixing, rolling, carding and drying. Needle shape is the core: carding straightens and tightens the strip, and drying fixes a tight, round, fine needle. The top grade is tight, straight, even and green; the aroma is clean; the liquor is yellow-green and bright; the taste is mellow; the infused leaf is yellow-green and fairly bright. It does not chase high-fire aroma or strong bitterness; “clear” and “mellow” are the key words.

The group standard requires no more than 6.5% moisture, 7.5% total ash, 1.0% powder, at least 34.5% water extract and no more than 15.5% crude fibre. These thresholds describe a clean, extract-rich, not-overly-coarse famous green tea. Brew at 80–85°C, 3 g to 150 ml in a glass, about one minute for the first infusion. The needles stand upright in the glass — a visual signature, not a grade.

Buying and boundaries

First check the GI mark and licensed producer. The Yongchuan tea association manages the mark, and licensed firms must follow the group standard; many products with “Xiuya” in the name are not GI products. Second, look at shape: tight, round, fine, straight, even and green is positive; loose, broken or grey is negative. Third, aroma: clean, tender or chestnut notes are fine; perfume or smoke is a defect. Fourth, “early” is not “better”: early picking is a regional advantage, not proof that every early lot beats a later lot.

Drink it plain; milk and sugar do not suit it, and boiling water with a long steep flattens it. The page avoids absolutes such as “Chongqing's only” or “China's earliest”; the checkable statement is “a representative of Chongqing's early-market famous tea” and “20–25 days earlier than comparable latitudes in Jiangsu and Zhejiang.”

China · Zhejiang

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

China · Jiangsu

Biluochun

Biluochun is a pan-fired spring green tea from the Dongting hills in Lake Tai, Jiangsu. The leaf is tiny, tightly curled and covered with white down; the tea is famous for a fresh, sweet, slightly fruity cup and for the tea–fruit intercropping tradition of Dongting Mountain.

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.
