Abigail's Blend

Abigail's Blend is a commemorative black-tea blend created for the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum and Abigail's Tea Room by tea master Bruce Richardson and crafted by Elmwood Inn Fine Teas. It is named for Abigail Adams. It is not the tea thrown into Boston Harbor in 1773, and it is not the “Boston Harbour Tea” commercial brand. The blend uses Chinese Yunnan black tea as its base and adds Assam, Nilgiri and Kenyan black teas. The museum's own description is that it recalls the Chinese black teas thrown overboard in 1773, “but better”. It can be served hot or iced, with or without milk. The product comes as 3.5 oz (99 g) loose tea or 14 pyramid tea bags at $17.99. On 16 December 1773, 340 chests of East India Company tea from the Beaver, Dartmouth and Eleanor were dumped into Boston Harbor, weighing over 92,000 lb (about 46 tonnes); about 80% was Chinese black tea.

United States

American Classic Tea (Charleston Tea Garden)

American Classic Tea is the flagship black tea of Charleston Tea Garden on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina. It is made exclusively from tea grown in the United States, but it is not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic “Charleston-style” black tea. The garden's own material supplies the usable facts: 127 acres, more than 320 tea varieties, a history that runs from Charles Shepard's Pinehurst plantation in 1888 to the 1963 transplant at Wadmalaw Island, commercial operation in 1987 and Bigelow ownership from 2003. The tea itself is a larger-leaf black tea; the official brewing suggestion is 8 ounces of boiling water for 2–4 minutes, hot or iced. The page deliberately leaves cultivar and CTC/Orthodox claims out because the official pages do not publish them.

Anhua dark tea

Anhua dark tea is a family of dark teas from Anhua, Hunan, including Tianjian, Gongjian, Shengjian, Fu brick, Hei brick, Hua brick and Hua Juan. They share pile fermentation but differ in material, pressing, fungal growth and drying; check the product standard and label.

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness. The 2006 standard is still in force; a 2026 revision takes effect on 2027-04-01.

Argentine Tereré

Tereré is yerba mate brewed with cold water and ice. It uses the same leaf and often the same bombilla as hot mate, but the lower temperature gives a lighter, sweeter, less bitter drink. In Argentina it is a summer habit, not a separate tea plant.

Vietnam

Bao Loc Oolong Tea

Bao Loc oolong (Trà Ô Long Bảo Lộc) is a tea region and a family of oolong-style teas from Bao Loc in Lam Dong, Vietnam. It is not a single brand and not a synonym for “Vietnamese Tieguanyin”. Bao Loc sits at about 800 m on the Lam Dong plateau, with cool nights, thick morning fog and a large day-night temperature swing; its soil is deeply weathered basalt, rich in minerals and both well drained and moisture-retentive. Local gardens grow local seed tea, Shan tea and introduced oolong cultivars. Bao Loc oolong is exported to Russia, France, the United States, India, Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore, Taiwan, Hong Kong and the Middle East. Bao Loc VN and Kim Điền operate at different scales.

Biluochun

Colombia

Bitaco Colombian Black Tea

“Colombian highland black tea” is neither a protected appellation nor a national grade. The public, traceable object is the Bitaco black-tea line made by Agrícola Himalaya in the Bitaco area of Valle del Cauca, Colombia. Bitaco describes its black teas as organic, single-origin specialty tea with an amber liquor, smooth and subtly sweet, mellow, with raisin and dried-plum notes. The garden sits at about 1,800 metres and coexists with the Bitaco Regional Forest Reserve. The company's earlier commodity brand was Té Hindú; Bitaco was launched in 2016 as a premium organic line. Cultivar, oxidation percentage and rolling method are not published, so this page does not invent them.

Colombia

Bitaco Green Tea

Bitaco is the specialty tea brand of Agrícola Himalaya S.A., grown at Hacienda Himalaya in La Cumbre, Bitaco, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. The estate is about 254 ha at roughly 1,700–2,050 m, with about half conservation forest and half organic tea, and is described by its owner as Colombia's only commercial tea plantation. Its green tea is not Japanese steamed tea: a publicly verifiable Bitaco green, Selva Curly Green, is pan-fired, with a sweet, nutty, mellow profile rather than a marine sencha profile. The old draft's "1998 Japanese steaming line," "Yabukita/Asatsuyu cultivars," "JAS certification" and "Andean gyokuro" claims have no source and have been removed.

Canada

Canadian Boreal Herbal Tea

Canadian boreal herbal tea is not a fixed national recipe and not Camellia sinensis tea. It is a working label for caffeine-free infusions made from plants of Canada's boreal forest, most commonly Labrador tea (Rhododendron groenlandicum / R. tomentosum) and spring spruce tips (Picea glauca / P. mariana), with balsam fir and pine sometimes added. It has a long Indigenous and settler drinking tradition, but Labrador tea contains ledol and grayanotoxins; excessive or badly prepared infusions carry real risk. It is not a detox tea and not a substitute for medicine.

Canada

Canadian Icewine Tea (Icewine-Flavoured Black Tea)

Canadian Icewine Tea is a flavoured black-tea blend sold in Canada, not icewine and not tea grown in a Canadian garden. The base is usually Sri Lankan or other black tea, combined with icewine, natural flavours or grape flavour; formulations vary, and some contain real Ontario icewine while others use flavour only. Ontario Icewine itself is regulated: grapes must be naturally frozen on the vine, picked at -8°C or lower and pressed immediately, with juice at least 35 Brix. The old draft's claims of a Canadian tea garden, a single Niagara origin and fixed Darjeeling/Fuding bases had no source and have been rewritten.

Türkiye

Çaykur Altınbaş (Golden Head Black Tea)

Çaykur Altınbaş is a Turkish black-tea product and grade, not a cultivar and not the “golden head” plant part itself. Çaykur's official product copy says the bud at the top and the two small fresh leaves beneath it are the secret of Altınbaş; the blend carries the highest proportion of golden-tipped, curled leaf, a trademark astringency, a high aroma and enough strength that a small amount makes a large brew, with no additives or chemicals. It comes in a 400 g tin and in 200 g and 500 g bags. Çaykur's training notes explain the Altın Tip / Altınbaş look as an orange-gold “golden tip” formed when the buds are heavily hairy and processing is appropriate. It is not a Turkish Jin Junmei, not a single-estate tea and not an ancient-tree product.

Çaykur Filiz (Bud-Leaf Black Tea)

Çaykur Filiz is a black-tea blend from Türkiye's state tea enterprise, named after the Turkish word for a shoot or bud. The official description is narrow and useful: it contains a high proportion of tea buds, has no additives, and is described as smooth with a lasting aroma. It is not a cultivar, a single estate, or the product of the old draft's ÇT-9 clone, 2008 project, 2011 launch or “feminine afternoon tea” story. The official pack sizes are 500 g, 1,000 g and 2,000 g. Filiz belongs to the everyday blended side of the Çaykur range, while Altınbaş is the golden-tip, more astringent line.

Çaykur Karadeniz Çayı (Bergamot Flavoured)

Çaykur Karadeniz Çayı is a bergamot-flavoured black tea from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. This page covers the official “Bergamot Aromalı Karadeniz Çayı 1000gr” SKU. The product page lists 100% Turkish tea, 100% natural, a soft character and a bulk-use positioning; one box holds 10 units, each with a gross weight of 1.25 kg, at a listed price of TRY 398.50, out of stock at the time of review. Karadeniz means “Black Sea” and is a brand/product name, not a declared blend of Rize, Trabzon, Artvin, Giresun and Ordu. Bergamot is an added flavour, not a tea cultivar. Old claims about a five-province blend, a 2010 “Black Sea corridor” project, a 2013 launch and “Karadeniz Koyu/Bahar” versions have no official basis and are removed.

Çaykur Rize Turist

Çaykur Rize Turist is a current black-tea product from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. It is not a tea-tourism experience or a fictional tourist souvenir. The official 200 g page calls it a family everyday tea, uses the slogan “The Real Yellow”, and lists 100, 200, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 g packs with 24 pieces per carton. Çaykur Tiryaki

Çaykur Tiryaki is a current blended black tea from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. “Tiryaki” in Turkish means a habitual devotee — someone who cannot do without tea, coffee or another daily habit. The official copy calls Tiryaki a special blend that can be drunk at any hour, emphasises quality, ideal taste, aroma and a soft texture, and states that it contains no additives. The 500 g pack is listed at 200.50 TL, with 15 pieces per carton and 0.7 kg per piece; the 1,000 g pack is listed at 400 TL, with 12 pieces per carton and 1.25 kg per piece; 2,000 g and 5,000 g packs also exist. The official pages do not give a cultivar, grade code, caffeine figure or “Plus/Sade” sub-line. A third-party exporter describes Tiryaki as a strong blend of mature Rize leaf with deep mahogany liquor that withstands long boiling; that is a trade description, not an official specification.

Thailand

Cha DoiChang Oolong Green Tea

Cha DoiChang is a small organic tea garden and tea company in Pong Phrae, Mae Lao district, Chiang Rai, Thailand. It is not Doi Chaang Coffee, the well-known coffee brand from Doi Chang village. The tea garden started in 1999, covers 50 rai (about 8 hectares) at roughly 600 m, uses a natural stream that flows down from Doi Chang, and receives about 2,500 mm of rain a year. The company was founded in 2001 with three shareholders and combines Taiwanese tea-making technology with local know-how. Its line includes Superior, Premium, Pure Green, Red Oolong, Eternity and Peaceful Rest.

Thailand

Doi Ang Khang Green Tea (Royal Project Organic Green Tea)

Doi Ang Khang Green Tea is an organic green-tea product from the Royal Project at Doi Ang Khang in Fang District, Chiang Mai, Thailand. It is not a tea cultivar and not a generic name for Thai green tea. The Royal Project product page confirms only that it is organic, unfermented green tea with a nutty or seaweed-like aroma and a mild taste; it does not publish the cultivar, the kill-green method or fixed brewing parameters. The place matters: the station was founded in 1969 as the first Royal Project agricultural research station, at about 1,400 metres on the Thai-Myanmar border, as an alternative to opium cultivation. The page keeps those confirmed layers and refuses the old draft's unverified tea-garden, tree-age and processing claims.

Doi Mae Salong Oolong

Doi Mae Salong oolong is a place-based style from the highlands of Chiang Rai, not a single cultivar or official grade. Growers work mainly with Taiwanese cultivars such as Jin Xuan (TTES No.12) and Ruan Zhi (TTES No.17), and the cup can range from floral and milky to honeyed or roasted. The tea culture grew out of a former KMT settlement that moved from opium to legal highland crops.

Doi Tung Oolong

Doi Tung Oolong is a regional and trade name for oolong tea from the Doi Tung highlands in Chiang Rai, northern Thailand. It is not a current official tea SKU of the DoiTung social enterprise. On 15 January 1987, the Princess Mother arrived at Doi Tung at the age of 87 and launched the Doi Tung Development Project (DTDP), replacing opium income with alternative crops such as coffee and macadamia; the project says it became self-sufficient in 2000. DoiTung's current corporate site lists Coffee and Macadamia, Handicrafts, Café DoiTung, Tourism and Horticulture, with no separate tea business. Third-party tea traders describe gardens at about 1,200–1,400 m or 1,600 m, mainly planted with Taiwanese cultivars such as Ruan Zhi and Jin Xuan, harvested in May–June. The official project pages do not give a single garden elevation, cultivar ratio or sensory standard, so Doi Tung Oolong should be treated as a regional/trade tea, not an official branded product.

Duyun Maojian Tea

Duyun Maojian is a curled green tea from Qiannan, Guizhou, and a geographical-indication protected product spanning Duyun, Fuquan, Weng'an and other counties. Often called “Yugou tea” (fish-hook tea), it has tight curly leaf, abundant white hairs, a tender and chestnut aroma and a fresh, sweet finish. In 2024 Duyun City reported 378,300 mu of tea gardens, 12,600 tonnes of tea and RMB 3.305 billion in output value, with a brand value of RMB 5.223 billion.

Fuding Gongmei

Gongmei is a white tea made under the national standard from the young shoots of population cultivars, usually less tender than Bai Mudan and more tender than Shoumei. It often has a more complex floral and sweet character; population gardens are now less common than in the past.

Fuding Shoumei

Fuding Shoumei is a white tea made from relatively mature buds and leaves, often one bud with two or three leaves or coarser material. It has a deeper colour and mellow taste, suits daily drinking and ageing, but “older is better” is not a fact; storage and material matter.
