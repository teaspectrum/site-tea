Fuding Shoumei

China · Fujian

Fuding Shoumei is a white tea made from relatively mature buds and leaves, often one bud with two or three leaves or coarser material. It has a deeper colour and mellow taste, suits daily drinking and ageing, but “older is better” is not a fact; storage and material matter.

What it is

Shoumei is a white tea made from relatively mature material and is one of the larger-production white-tea categories. Under GB/T 22291-2017, it uses mainly mature leaves, often one bud with two or three leaves or coarser picking. It does not rely on fine buds; instead, the leaf gives sweetness, body and durability.

Origin and material

Fuding is a major Shoumei origin, with Diantou, Bailin, Panxi and Taimu Mountain all producing; Zhenghe also produces Shoumei. Picking usually comes later than Silver Needle and Bai Mudan, with more mature leaf and a higher proportion of stem and vein. Cultivar, altitude, picking batch and weather strongly affect style; “Shoumei” is not a single cultivar.

Processing

Shoumei is mainly withered and dried, without fixation or rolling. Because leaves are large and moisture content is relatively high, spreading thickness and withering time must follow the weather; thick piles or uneven water loss can cause stewed notes, reddening or mould risk. Sun withering can add sweetness and a “sun” note, but strong direct sun can damage the leaf, so producers often alternate sun and indoor withering.

Quality and flavour

Dry leaves are loosely rolled, spreading, grey-green to brown-green. Liquor is apricot-yellow to orange-yellow, with down, honey, dry-grass and woody notes, a mellow sweet taste and relatively low bitterness. The high leaf proportion usually gives more body and durability than Bai Mudan, but coarse does not mean high quality: clean material, even withering, thorough drying and good storage are prerequisites.

Brewing and ageing

Use a gaiwan with water around 90–95°C and short infusions; it can also be boiled, which amplifies sweetness and woody notes. Shoumei can be aged, but “the older the better” is not unconditional: material, moisture, storage temperature and humidity, and odour control determine the direction. “Aged Shoumei” should have traceable age, storage and testing information, not just dark colour, old packaging or a story.

Common misconceptions

  • Shoumei is not “the worst white tea”. It is a distinct category based on mature leaves, with a different style from Silver Needle and Bai Mudan.
  • Coarse does not mean high quality. Excessively coarse leaf, too much stem or poor storage lowers quality.
  • Old tea is not automatically good tea. Damp, odorous or mouldy Shoumei does not improve with age.
  • Boiling is not the only way to drink it. Gaiwan, pot and boiling each suit different settings; adjust leaf and time.

Fuding tea area

A core white-tea area in Fuding, Fujian, where Fuding Dabai and Dahao cultivars and withering-drying produce Baihao Yinzhen, Bai Mudan, Gongmei and Shoumei. Fuding is a major origin, but not every white tea comes from Fuding.

Fuding Bai Mudan

Fuding Bai Mudan is a white tea made from one bud and one or two leaves, with attached bud and leaf, a silver-white bud among green leaves and an apricot-yellow liquor. Under GB/T 22291-2017 it sits between Baihao Yinzhen and Gongmei/Shoumei in raw-material tenderness.

Fuding Gongmei

Gongmei is a white tea made under the national standard from the young shoots of population cultivars, usually less tender than Bai Mudan and more tender than Shoumei. It often has a more complex floral and sweet character; population gardens are now less common than in the past.

Baihao Yinzhen (Silver Needle)

Baihao Yinzhen is a white tea made from single buds, with plump, downy needles and a pale apricot liquor. The white-tea national standard defines it as a distinct category; origin, cultivar and processing shape its style.

Darjeeling Moonlight

“Moonlight” is a specialty tea name in Darjeeling, usually used for a low-oxidation white tea made from tender spring buds and young leaves, and occasionally for a first-flush black or lightly oxidised lot. It is not a cultivar, an estate name or a protected grade. The full-moon picking story is tradition and marketing, not a rule that applies to every Moonlight.
