Çaykur Tiryaki

Türkiye

Çaykur Tiryaki is a current blended black tea from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. “Tiryaki” in Turkish means a habitual devotee — someone who cannot do without tea, coffee or another daily habit. The official copy calls Tiryaki a special blend that can be drunk at any hour, emphasises quality, ideal taste, aroma and a soft texture, and states that it contains no additives. The 500 g pack is listed at 200.50 TL, with 15 pieces per carton and 0.7 kg per piece; the 1,000 g pack is listed at 400 TL, with 12 pieces per carton and 1.25 kg per piece; 2,000 g and 5,000 g packs also exist. The official pages do not give a cultivar, grade code, caffeine figure or “Plus/Sade” sub-line. A third-party exporter describes Tiryaki as a strong blend of mature Rize leaf with deep mahogany liquor that withstands long boiling; that is a trade description, not an official specification.

What it is: a blended black tea, not a “strength grade”

“Tiryaki” in Turkish means a habitual devotee — someone who cannot do without a daily tea, coffee or other routine. Çaykur uses the word as a product name. The official copy says Tiryaki creates a palate that turns tea lovers into habitues, but that is marketing language, not an official strength grade or a “strongest tea in Turkey” certificate.

Tiryaki belongs to Çaykur's black-tea line and is a special blend. The official phrase “can be drunk at any hour of the day” matters: Çaykur positions it as a soft, smooth, all-day blend, not an extreme brew intended only for people who want the strongest possible cup.

Official says “soft”; a third-party exporter says “strong”

The official keywords are: any hour, special blend, quality, ideal taste, aroma, soft texture and no additives. The official pages do not call Tiryaki the highest-caffeine, highest-tannin or darkest tea.

The third-party exporter Ismar Export uses a different register: Tiryaki is for committed tea drinkers, uses mature Rize leaf, has deep red liquor and withstands long boiling, and is sold in 500 g retail packs and 10 kg bulk. That description is useful for understanding the export market, but it is not an official Çaykur specification. “Mature leaf” and “deep red liquor” should not be presented as the official formula.

A safe split is:

  • Official: special blend, any-hour drinking, no additives, soft texture, 500/1,000/2,000/5,000 g packs.
  • Third-party description: mature Rize leaf, strong brew, deep mahogany liquor, long-boil tolerance. Label it as the exporter's wording.

How to brew it: the usual Turkish double-pot method

Çaykur does not publish a product-specific leaf weight, water temperature or steeping time for Tiryaki. The common Turkish double-pot method can be used:

  1. Fill the lower pot with water and bring it to a boil.
  2. Put Tiryaki tea in the upper pot, add a little boiling water to rinse and settle the leaf, then set the upper pot over the lower pot to heat by steam.
  3. Let the upper pot steep long enough to build strength, usually 10–15 minutes; extend if you want a stronger cup, but do not boil it indefinitely into bitterness.
  4. Pour the strong upper tea into a tulip-shaped glass, filling about one-third to one-half of the glass, then top up with hot water from the lower pot.
  5. Taste without sugar first, then add sugar cubes if desired.

This is a general Turkish tea method, not a Çaykur specification for Tiryaki. The exporter's claim that the tea withstands long boiling is not an official recommendation to steep it indefinitely.

Tea in Türkiye

Turkish tea is concentrated on the eastern Black Sea coast in Rize, Trabzon and Artvin. Black tea dominates, with Çaykur and private factories, the çaydanlık double pot and tulip glass shaping national tea culture.

Abigail's Blend

Abigail's Blend is a commemorative black-tea blend created for the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum and Abigail's Tea Room by tea master Bruce Richardson and crafted by Elmwood Inn Fine Teas. It is named for Abigail Adams. It is not the tea thrown into Boston Harbor in 1773, and it is not the “Boston Harbour Tea” commercial brand. The blend uses Chinese Yunnan black tea as its base and adds Assam, Nilgiri and Kenyan black teas. The museum's own description is that it recalls the Chinese black teas thrown overboard in 1773, “but better”. It can be served hot or iced, with or without milk. The product comes as 3.5 oz (99 g) loose tea or 14 pyramid tea bags at $17.99. On 16 December 1773, 340 chests of East India Company tea from the Beaver, Dartmouth and Eleanor were dumped into Boston Harbor, weighing over 92,000 lb (about 46 tonnes); about 80% was Chinese black tea.

United States

American Classic Tea (Charleston Tea Garden)

American Classic Tea is the flagship black tea of Charleston Tea Garden on Wadmalaw Island, South Carolina. It is made exclusively from tea grown in the United States, but it is not a cultivar, a protected origin or a generic “Charleston-style” black tea. The garden's own material supplies the usable facts: 127 acres, more than 320 tea varieties, a history that runs from Charles Shepard's Pinehurst plantation in 1888 to the 1963 transplant at Wadmalaw Island, commercial operation in 1987 and Bigelow ownership from 2003. The tea itself is a larger-leaf black tea; the official brewing suggestion is 8 ounces of boiling water for 2–4 minutes, hot or iced. The page deliberately leaves cultivar and CTC/Orthodox claims out because the official pages do not publish them.

Assam Tea

Assam is the world’s largest contiguous tea-growing region, producing bold, malty black tea on the Brahmaputra plains; CTC is its everyday volume tea, while orthodox Assam offers a fuller-leaf alternative.
