Tea Tasting Vocabulary

Guide

Tasting vocabulary is not a contest in mysticism. A good description points to a specific object and intensity: dry leaf, liquor, aroma, taste, mouthfeel and infused leaf. Terms such as “orchid” or “rock rhyme” must be unpacked into observable cues.

Tasting vocabulary is not there to make tea sound profound. It lets two people discuss the same thing. A useful term has three qualities: it points to a specific object (dry leaf, liquor, aroma, taste, mouthfeel, infused leaf), it carries intensity (none / weak / medium / strong), and another person can check it. Without these, even a beautiful word is a monologue.

Object first, then word

Object What to describe Do not mix in
Dry leaf Tight/loose roll, wholeness, colour, evenness, tips “Floral” is not a dry-leaf shape term
Liquor Colour, brightness, clarity/turbidity, depth “Thick” is mouthfeel, not colour
Aroma Family (floral/fruity/roasted/woody/grassy), height, persistence, purity “Sweet” is taste, not aroma
Taste Sweet, bitter, umami, sour, salty, aftertaste “Silky” is mouthfeel, not taste
Mouthfeel Thick/thin, silky/rough, astringent, coating, grainy “Aromatic” is not mouthfeel
Infused leaf Soft/hard, evenness, colour, liveliness, fragmentation “Liquor colour” is not leaf colour

ISO 6078 separates black tea terms into dry leaf, liquor, infused leaf, manufacture and general. It also warns that the same word can describe different objects: dull can describe dry leaf, infused leaf or liquor. Name the object before the adjective.

Aroma: families, not mysticism

Family Observable cue Common in
Floral Orchid, jasmine, osmanthus, rose, orange blossom Light oolong, white tea, some black tea
Fruity Peach, apricot, citrus, lychee, cooked fruit Oolong, black tea, some white tea
Green Fresh grass, spinach, bean, nori Green tea, lightly oxidised tea
Roasted / nutty Chestnut, walnut, toast, caramel Roasted oolong, black tea, pan-fired green tea
Woody / aged Cedar, dry wood, damp wood, medicinal Post-fermented tea, aged tea
Creamy / sweet Cream, honey, brown sugar Black tea, some oolong

“Orchid” is not one molecule; it is a group of volatiles giving a similar impression. “A clean, sweet floral note like orchid, strong in the hot cup and weaker in the empty cup” is more checkable than “orchid”.

Taste and mouthfeel: bitter, astringent, thick, silky

  • Bitter: caffeine and some catechins activate bitter receptors.
  • Astringent: polyphenols bind salivary proteins, producing drying, tightening and roughness.
  • Umami: L-theanine, succinic acid and gallic acid contribute.
  • Sweet aftertaste: sweetness returns after swallowing, linked to catechin hydrolysis and saliva changes.
  • Thick: structure from pectin, soluble sugars and amino acids.
  • Silky: low friction in the mouth, not “weak flavour”.

A tea can be bitter without astringency, astringent without bitterness, thick without being strong, or aromatic without much taste. Separate the dimensions.

Defect words: confirm before concluding

ISO 6078 and the green tea vocabulary list musty, stale, burnt, harsh, grassy/green (often under-withering or insufficient fixation), withered and smoky. A defect word points to a processing or storage problem, not simply “unpleasant”.

Principles:

  1. Describe before judging. Wet cardboard and mustiness first; “damp storage” second.
  2. Grade intensity. Slight vs clear vs undrinkable.
  3. Name the object. Dry-leaf mustiness, liquor mustiness and infused-leaf mustiness may have different sources.
  4. Do not romanticise defects. Mustiness is not “aged charm”; burnt is not “fire character”.

Intensity scale

Use 0–5 rather than “very / extremely”:

Score Meaning
0 None
1 Faint, needs attention
2 Light, clear but not dominant
3 Medium, balanced and recognisable
4 Strong, dominant but acceptable
5 Extreme, possibly unbalanced or defective

Intensity is not preference. A 4 roast note can be pleasant; a 4 astringency may be hard to accept. Record them separately.

Record template

Item Example
Dry leaf Tight roll, deep green with some white tips, even
Liquor Pale yellow-green, bright, clear
Aroma Hot: tender chestnut and fresh grass; warm: orchid, intensity 3; empty cup: weak
Taste Umami 3, bitter 1, astringent 1, sweet aftertaste 3
Mouthfeel Medium thickness, silky, slight tightening
Infused leaf Soft, even, yellow-green and bright
Defects No mustiness, burnt or sourness

Source boundary

This page can establish: tasting terms should be organised by object, intensity and observable cue; ISO 6078 and the green tea vocabulary provide systematic terms; bitter, astringent, umami, thick and silky are different dimensions; defect words point to processing or storage.

This page cannot establish: that one word maps to one chemical; that a specific tea must show a specific aroma; that terminology can replace personal preference. Judge with standard brewing, references and blind tasting; discard tea with mustiness or sourness on food-safety grounds.
