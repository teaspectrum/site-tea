Tea Buying Basics

Buying tea is not memorising grade acronyms or chasing a famous origin. Decide what kind of tea you want, then check dry leaf, aroma, processing, date, packaging and a trial brew. Grade letters describe leaf size and tip content, not the quality in the cup.

Tea Cultivar Biology

A cultivar is not a brand and not a tea name. It is a suite of observable, heritable traits: leaf type, flushing time, bud colour, stress tolerance and processing suitability. Keep cultivar, style and geographical indication apart.

Tea Liquor Colour

Liquor colour is not one axis called "depth". It is a spectrum written by pigment composition, oxidation level, concentration and water chemistry. Green-tea yellow-green, black-tea orange-red and dark-tea brown-red come from different ratios of chlorophyll, flavonoids, theaflavins, thearubigins and theabrownins.

Tea Plant Botany

The tea plant is not any evergreen with tea-like leaves. It is one species in the genus Camellia. Start with root, stem, leaf, flower and fruit, then add the two botanical varieties, the plucking standard and acid soil.

Tea Tasting Vocabulary

Tasting vocabulary is not a contest in mysticism. A good description points to a specific object and intensity: dry leaf, liquor, aroma, taste, mouthfeel and infused leaf. Terms such as “orchid” or “rock rhyme” must be unpacked into observable cues.

Theaflavins

Theaflavins are orange-red polyphenols formed when catechins oxidise and condense during black-tea fermentation. They shape brightness, body and part of the astringency of the cup — but they are not a single “health ingredient”.

Water and Extraction

Temperature, time, leaf-to-water ratio and water mineralisation together determine the strength and flavour of a cup. Hotter water extracts faster, hard water can flatten aroma and darken liquor, but “purer is better” is not the answer either.

Where Tea Aroma Comes From

Tea aroma is not one molecule but a shifting mix of volatiles shaped by cultivar, processing and brewing. Learn to separate grassy, floral, fruity, sweet and roasted notes before asking whether a tea is merely “aromatic”.
