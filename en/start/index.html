China · Zhejiang

Longjing tea

Longjing is a pan-fired green tea from the Hangzhou area, known for its flat, straight leaves and fresh, fragrant taste. The geographical indication standard defines its protected area, suitable cultivars and processing requirements; West Lake’s traditional labels are not the same as statutory grades.

China · Zhejiang

West Lake tea area

A traditional Longjing tea area around West Lake in Hangzhou, known for the local Shi, Long, Yun, Hu and Mei labels and pan-fired processing. It is one traditional part of the Longjing protected area, not every flat green tea.

Vessel

Gaiwan

A three-part vessel with a lid, bowl and saucer, used both for brewing and for drinking. A white porcelain gaiwan is neutral, easy to observe and a practical tool for learning and comparing teas.

China · Beijing

Lao She Teahouse

Lao She Teahouse is on Qianmen West Street in Beijing, opened in 1988 and named after the writer Lao She. It combines tea, Beijing snacks and stage performances such as quyi, Peking opera and acrobatics; it is a modern cultural teahouse, not the original site of Lao She’s fictional teahouse.

The Ancient Tea Horse Road

A network of trade routes linking southwest China's tea regions with the Tibetan plateau and South Asia, shaped by horse caravans, Pu-erh tea and multi-ethnic exchange from the Tang to the Qing.
