Cultivars.

Meet the cultivars behind different styles of tea.

11entries
TeasRegionsCultivarsProcessingBrewingStorageGuides

A cultivar is not a brand and not a product name. The same cultivar can make very different teas under different origins and processes, and the same tea name can come from different cultivars. This page catalogues botanical identities: start with origin, processing suitability and breeding background, then decide which tea it is worth following in.

A shared name is not a shared identity: Zhenghe Dabaicha and Fuding Dabaicha are both “Dabaicha” but different cultivars, and Jin Xuan’s milky note is not present in every natural batch. When a product page gives only a tea name and no cultivar, treat it as a lead to verify, not as proof of the cultivar.

Anji Baicha cultivar

Anji Baicha is a temperature-sensitive albino tea cultivar from Anji, Zhejiang, mainly known as Baiye No. 1; its pale spring shoots are still processed as green tea, and the fresh, umami cup comes from the cultivar and a narrow picking window.

Fuding Dabaicha

Fuding Dabaicha is a clonal tea cultivar from Fuding, Fujian, with plump buds, heavy pekoe and early budbreak; it is a classic source for Silver Needle and Bai Mudan, also made as green tea, and is not the same cultivar as Fuding Dahao.

Huangjin Gui

An Anxi oolong made from the Huangdan (Huangyan) cultivar, native to Luoyan, Huqiu. It is early-ripening and high-aroma, known as “one early, two unusual” and “sky-piercing fragrance”.

Jin Xuan

Taiwan Tea No. 12, bred at the Tea Research and Extension Station and named in 1981. Known as “27仔” from its experimental code. Suitable for pouchong and oolong; natural milky aroma is not present in every batch.

Longjing 43

Longjing 43 is a clonal cultivar selected from the Longjing population at the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, with early budbreak and even shoots, suited to flat green tea; it is not the same as the Longjing population, nor is it all Longjing tea.

Meizhan

A clonal cultivar from Sanyang village, Lutian, Anxi. Its upright habit and long internodes give it the nickname “high-legged oolong”. It can be made into oolong, black, green and white tea.

Quntizhong (local population)

Quntizhong is not one cultivar but a local seed-propagated, naturally hybridised tea population; it is uneven, flavoursome and resilient, and includes the Longjing population, Wuyi caicha and Anxi local groups.

Tieguanyin cultivar

Tieguanyin is a clonal cultivar from Anxi, Fujian, known for red heart, skewed leaf tip and peach-shaped leaf; the qingxiang, nongxiang and aged styles come from processing, not from the cultivar name alone.

Wuyi caicha

Wuyi caicha is the seed-propagated local population of the Wuyi Mountains, not a wild vegetable tea; it is variable and forms the breeding base of many named bushes and single bushes, and an important source of traditional rock-tea flavour.

Yunnan large-leaf cultivar

Yunnan large-leaf tea is the local population and selected cultivars of Camellia sinensis var. assamica, with large leaves, high polyphenols and downy buds, suited to pu-erh, black and green tea; it is not a synonym for ancient-tree tea.

Zhenghe Dabaicha

A clonal tea cultivar from Tieshan, Zhenghe, Fujian. Late-ripening, large-leaf and hairy, suitable for white, black and green tea. It is not the same cultivar as Fuding Dabaicha.
