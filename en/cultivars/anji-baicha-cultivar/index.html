Anji Baicha is a cultivar; Anji Bai Cha is a green tea. The “white” refers to pale spring shoots under cool temperatures, not to white-tea processing.

The short answer

Anji Baicha is a temperature-sensitive albino tea cultivar from Anji, Zhejiang, mainly propagated as Baiye No. 1. In cool spring weather the new shoots turn pale yellow or creamy white, then green as temperatures rise; the picking window is short. The leaf is made as green tea, not as a withered white tea. The fresh, sweet cup comes from the cultivar, the degree of albinism, the picking date and the processing together, not from “high amino acids” alone.

Origin and naming

The mother bush was found in the Anji mountains in the 1980s and was propagated vegetatively as Baiye No. 1. Anji Baicha is at once a place name and a product name, and the cultivar shares the name, which invites confusion. Some tea made from pale-leaf cultivars grown elsewhere is sold under the Anji name, so geographical indication, provenance and picking date matter more than the word “white”.

How the albinism works

The trait is temperature-sensitive. Early-spring cold blocks normal chloroplast development, so shoots appear pale yellow, milky white or white-green; colour returns as the weather warms. It is not a disease, and it is not simply the result of shading. Too little albinism gives an ordinary green tea; too much lowers yield and thins the leaf. The best lots are usually picked in the few days when the pale colour is clear but the shoots have not hardened.

Plant characters

Baiye No. 1 is a shrubby, medium-leaf cultivar with early budbreak, plump buds and good tenderness. Branch angle and leaf shape vary between propagation batches. It grows well in Zhejiang, Anhui, Jiangsu and similar climates, but not every new site expresses the trait reliably. Altitude, slope, the speed of spring warming and soil moisture all shift the duration of albinism and the picking window. Clonal gardens are uniform, but genetically narrow, with less buffering against extreme weather and disease.

Processing and liquor

Fresh leaf is spread, fixed, shaped into straight strips and dried — a green-tea route. Fixation must be quick so the pale leaf does not stew yellow; shaping determines whether the buds stay intact; a slow, low bake keeps the sweetness. A good lot has a pale yellow-green liquor, notes of young chestnut, orchid and a faint seaweedy freshness, a soft sweet entry, low bitterness and a long finish. Late picking or weak albinism gives a thinner cup or the green astringency of ordinary green tea.

Market and identification

Prices range from everyday tea to gift boxes. Look at the dry leaf: intact buds and a tender green-white colour, not chalky white or grey-green. Look at the liquor: pale yellow-green and clear. Taste: fresh sweetness rather than sugariness. GI products have a defined origin and process; pale-leaf tea from elsewhere is not Anji tea just because the label says so. Check picking date, origin, standard and batch rather than judging by whiteness alone.

Degree of albinism and the picking window

Judge the shoot at one bud and two leaves: creamy to pale yellow, veins still green and texture soft means ready; all-white, thin leaf with faded veins means overripe or frost-hit. Picking usually runs late March to mid-April, several days later for every 100 m of altitude. A cold snap can deepen albinism, then warmth turns the leaf green again, so batches from one garden vary widely. Spread the leaf thin and cool it quickly after fixation to keep the sweetness.

Brewing

Use 80–85°C water, a glass or white porcelain gaiwan, about 1:50 leaf to water, and 60–90 seconds for the first infusion. Boiling water exaggerates astringency in these tender leaves, and long stuffing dulls them. Shorten the second infusion slightly, then lengthen the third. Pour along the wall rather than straight onto the buds.

Limits

Anji Baicha is not a white tea; whiter is not better, since extreme albinism often means low yield and a thin cup; “high amino acids” is not by itself a quality guarantee. Keep cultivar, product and process separate.

Anji Baicha

Anji Baicha is a green tea from Anji, Zhejiang, despite its name. It is made from the Baiye Yihao cultivar during its spring pale-leaf stage, then fixed, shaped and dried as a green tea. The leaf is jade-white with green veins, the liquor pale yellow, and the taste unusually fresh, sweet and low in bitterness.
