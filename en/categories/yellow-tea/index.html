Yellow tea.

This shelf holds Chinese yellow tea, which adds a sealed-yellow step after firing, and Korean hwangcha, a partially oxidised tea named for its amber liquor. They share a catalogue name, not a process.

China · Anhui

Huoshan Huangya

Huoshan Huangya is a yellow bud tea from Huoshan, Anhui, made from fine young leaves and yellowed through menhuang. It has a straight, slightly open shape and a yellow-green to apricot liquor with sweet, chestnut-like aromas; it is not a green tea, and “Huangjinya” cultivar names are not the same product.

South Korea

Hwangcha (Korean Partially Oxidised Tea)

Hwangcha (황차) is a Korean partially oxidised tea, not a synonym for Chinese yellow tea and its menhuang / sealed-yellow process. Hankook Tea's official tea guide puts hwangcha at roughly 50–80% oxidation and describes it as pan-fired, with a production cycle of about two to three days. Its Hwang Cha Gold product is an early-spring, Sejak-grade tea from the company's Honam gardens, made from traditional jae-rae-jong seed-grown material; the liquor is amber and smooth, sitting between green tea and black tea. The name “yellow tea” describes colour and market usage, not the Chinese yellow-tea process.

China · Hunan

Junshan Yinzhen

Junshan Yinzhen is a yellow bud tea from Junshan Island in Yueyang, Hunan, made from plump single buds and yellowed through menhuang. The golden bud body with white down is traditionally called “gold inlaid with jade”; the brewing display is a visual feature, not a quality guarantee.

China · Sichuan

Mengding Huangya

Mengding Huangya is a yellow bud tea from Mengding Mountain in Ya’an, Sichuan, made from single buds and yellowed through menhuang. It has a golden, glossy appearance and an apricot-yellow liquor with sweet, tender aromas; it shares the Mengding tradition with Mengding Ganlu but is a different tea family.
