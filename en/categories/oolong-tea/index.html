Vietnam

Bao Loc Oolong Tea

Bao Loc oolong (Trà Ô Long Bảo Lộc) is a tea region and a family of oolong-style teas from Bao Loc in Lam Dong, Vietnam. It is not a single brand and not a synonym for “Vietnamese Tieguanyin”. Bao Loc sits at about 800 m on the Lam Dong plateau, with cool nights, thick morning fog and a large day-night temperature swing; its soil is deeply weathered basalt, rich in minerals and both well drained and moisture-retentive. Local gardens grow local seed tea, Shan tea and introduced oolong cultivars. Bao Loc oolong is exported to Russia, France, the United States, India, Japan, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore, Taiwan, Hong Kong and the Middle East. Bao Loc VN and Kim Điền operate at different scales.

Choui Fong Oolong

Choui Fong Oolong is not a single cultivar or a producing region. It is the oolong line of Choui Fong Tea, a family-owned producer in Chiang Rai, Thailand, built mainly on Jin Xuan and Ruan Zhi cultivars and sold as plain, scented and first-flush products.

Da Hong Pao

Da Hong Pao is a flagship Wuyi rock-tea product name. GB/T 18745-2006 groups Wuyi rock tea into Da Hong Pao, Shuixian, Rougui, Mingcong and Qizhong; commercial Da Hong Pao is usually a blend, and the mother trees are protected rather than picked for sale.

Thailand

Doi Mae Salong Oolong

Doi Mae Salong oolong is a place-based style from the highlands of Chiang Rai, not a single cultivar or official grade. Growers work mainly with Taiwanese cultivars such as Jin Xuan (TTES No.12) and Ruan Zhi (TTES No.17), and the cup can range from floral and milky to honeyed or roasted. The tea culture grew out of a former KMT settlement that moved from opium to legal highland crops.

Thailand

Doi Tung Oolong

Doi Tung Oolong is a regional and trade name for oolong tea from the Doi Tung highlands in Chiang Rai, northern Thailand. It is not a current official tea SKU of the DoiTung social enterprise. On 15 January 1987, the Princess Mother arrived at Doi Tung at the age of 87 and launched the Doi Tung Development Project (DTDP), replacing opium income with alternative crops such as coffee and macadamia; the project says it became self-sufficient in 2000. DoiTung's current corporate site lists Coffee and Macadamia, Handicrafts, Café DoiTung, Tourism and Horticulture, with no separate tea business. Third-party tea traders describe gardens at about 1,200–1,400 m or 1,600 m, mainly planted with Taiwanese cultivars such as Ruan Zhi and Jin Xuan, harvested in May–June. The official project pages do not give a single garden elevation, cultivar ratio or sensory standard, so Doi Tung Oolong should be treated as a regional/trade tea, not an official branded product.

Light-Aroma Tieguanyin

Light-aroma Tieguanyin is one of the three product types of Anxi Tieguanyin, alongside roasted and aged styles. It is made from the Tieguanyin cultivar through withering, shaking, fixing, rolling, wrap-rolling and drying, then sorted, blended and given a low-temperature “literary fire” dry. The leaf is tight and sandy-green, the liquor golden, the aroma clear and orchid-like, and the taste fresh, sweet and distinctly “yin-rhymed”.

Vietnam

Mộc Châu Oolong

Mộc Châu Oolong is an oolong product family from the Mộc Châu plateau in Sơn La, Vietnam. It is not a single brand and not a single cultivar. Vietnam's national tourism board describes Mộc Châu as a plateau at 1,054 m with some of the country's largest tea-garden scenery. Two producers can be documented: Mộc Sương and Tân Lập. Mộc Sương's own page says it began in 2005 by bringing oolong stock from Taiwan and planting 20 ha; its tea garden is at 1,250 m; it launched Thanh Tâm and Thúy Ngọc oolong in 2010; it now has a 30 ha farm with tea and fruit trees and started an eco-tourism area in 2012. In 2025, the Sơn La provincial newspaper reported that four Mộc Sương oolong products held OCOP 4-star status and that Tân Lập's Kim Tuyên and Tứ Quý oolong held OCOP 3-star status. OCOP is Vietnam's “One Commune One Product” rural-product rating, not a tea grade. The official material does not give a single regional cultivar, sensory standard or price for all Mộc Châu oolong.

Nepal

Nepal Oolong Tea

Nepal oolong is a partially oxidised tea made in the eastern hills of Nepal, mainly around Ilam, Dhankuta and Panchthar, at roughly 1,200–2,100 m. It is a style category, not a protected appellation and not a single cultivar: oxidation can range from about 20% to 80%, and flavour from light floral to nutty, stone-fruit and darker profiles. The old draft turned “Sundarpani”, Jinxuan/Qingxin, “snow-mountain oolong” and “world’s highest” into national facts. Those claims apply, at most, to individual gardens and do not define Nepal oolong.

Phoenix Dancong

Phoenix Dancong (Fenghuang Dancong) is a half-fermented oolong from the Phoenix Mountains of Chaozhou, Guangdong. It is made from the leaves, dormant buds and young shoots of Dancong lines, through withering, shaking, fixing, rolling and baking. The defining feature is aromatic individuality: honey-orchid, gardenia, magnolia, almond and cinnamon are sensory names for different lines, not botanical varieties.

Wuyi Rougui

Wuyi Rougui is a Wuyi rock tea made from the Rougui cultivar, known for a pungent cinnamon-like aroma with floral and fruity notes. Under GB/T 18745-2006, Rougui is one of the five Wuyi rock-tea product categories; Zhengyan and Banyan are trade divisions, not statutory 2006 grades.

Wuyi Shuixian

Wuyi Shuixian is a Wuyi rock tea made from the Shuixian cultivar, with a full body and often an orchid aroma; old-bush tea may show woody and mossy notes. Under GB/T 18745-2006 it is one of five Wuyi rock-tea categories; Zhengyan is a trade division, not a 2006 statutory grade.

漳平水仙饼茶

漳平水仙饼茶是福建龙岩漳平南洋、双洋一带的紧压乌龙茶，原产双洋镇溪口村大会自然村。它把闽北水仙与闽南乌龙做青技术结合，经晒青、凉青、摇青、杀青、揉捻、压模、烘焙做成小方饼，具有兰花香、桂花香和醇爽细润的滋味。2022 年漳平水仙茶制作技艺随“中国传统制茶技艺及其相关习俗”列入 UNESCO 人类非遗。
