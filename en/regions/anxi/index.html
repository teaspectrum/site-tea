Anxi tea area

China · Fujian

Anxi is the core of Fujian Tieguanyin. Xiping, Gande, Xianghua and Jiandou use the Tieguanyin cultivar, shaking and ball rolling to make “Guanyin rhyme” and orchid aroma; light, strong and aged styles differ.

Tieguanyin

Tieguanyin is both a tea cultivar and a commercial oolong tea name, originally from Anxi, Fujian. Light-aroma, strong-aroma and aged styles coexist; the protected Anxi Tieguanyin product has specific origin, cultivar and processing requirements.

Light-Aroma Tieguanyin

Light-aroma Tieguanyin is one of the three product types of Anxi Tieguanyin, alongside roasted and aged styles. It is made from the Tieguanyin cultivar through withering, shaking, fixing, rolling, wrap-rolling and drying, then sorted, blended and given a low-temperature “literary fire” dry. The leaf is tight and sandy-green, the liquor golden, the aroma clear and orchid-like, and the taste fresh, sweet and distinctly “yin-rhymed”.

Anxi Tieguanyin is the combined result of cultivar, shaking and ball rolling: the hills and red soil of Xiping, Gande, Xianghua and Jiandou, the orchid aroma of the Tieguanyin cultivar, the “green leaf with red edge” from shaking, and the tight ball from cloth rolling create the distinctive “Guanyin rhyme”.

The short answer

Anxi lies in western Quanzhou, Fujian, on the upper Xixi of the Jin River, the core of Tieguanyin; Xiping, Gande, Xianghua, Jiandou, Longjuan and Huqiu at about 300–800 metres, with acidic red soil and good drainage. Tieguanyin dominates, with Maoxie, Huangjin, Benshan and Meizhan; craft is shaking, cloth rolling and drying, with light, strong and aged styles. Read Anxi by cultivar and shaking first, then rolling and roast.

Geography and growing conditions

Anxi sits on the southeastern Daiyun Mountains, with hills, valleys and mountains, about 1,800 mm of rain, high humidity and cloud; gardens at 300–800 metres in acidic, well-drained red soil. Xiping is earlier, Gande aromatic, Xianghua fuller, Jiandou stronger; altitude, soil and microclimate create style differences.

Gardens are mostly terraced, and soil conservation matters; summer heat, intense rain and pests are risks. Tieguanyin is sensitive to altitude and management, shaking is weather-dependent, and ecology and pesticide control are industry issues.

History and industry

Anxi tea began in the late Tang, and the Tieguanyin cultivar was found and spread in Xiping in the Ming–Qing; it exported to Southeast Asia in the nineteenth century, and state factories and oolong craft matured in the twentieth. In the 2000s, the domestic market boomed and Anxi became a national oolong name; light, strong and aged styles followed.

Today companies, cooperatives and family farmers coexist, and Tieguanyin is a regional public brand; the market went through light-aroma heat, strong-aroma return and rational adjustment, with quality and brand central. Ecology, pesticide control and price stratification are issues; tea tourism and festivals support the region.

Cultivars and craft

Anxi plants Tieguanyin, plus Maoxie, Huangjin, Benshan and Meizhan; Tieguanyin is orchid-like with Guanyin rhyme, Maoxie aromatic but thin, Huangjin early and honeyed, Benshan similar but shorter, Meizhan stronger. Craft runs withering, shaking, kill-green, rolling, cloth rolling, drying and sorting; shaking is the aroma core, cloth rolling the shape.

Shaking alternates shaking and spreading for edge oxidation and “green leaf with red edge”; cloth rolling presses repeatedly into a tight ball; drying has first and second stages. Light style is lightly oxidised and roasted, strong style heavier, aged style stored long. Masters differ widely in shaking and rolling.

Flavour and grades

Light Tieguanyin is yellow-green, high orchid, fresh; strong style is orange, floral-fruity, roasted, mellow; aged is deep, camphor, medicinal and soft. Guanyin rhyme is a combined feel of cultivar, garden and shaking, hard to quantify. Maoxie and Huangjin are different and should not be sold as Tieguanyin.

Grade depends on cultivar, sub-area, shaking, rolling, roast and aroma; Xiping, Gande and Xianghua core areas are expensive. Buy by cultivar, style, craft and origin, not the “Tieguanyin” pack alone; light is not low-end, strong is not high-fire.

Market and brewing

Anxi Tieguanyin sells mainly domestically, with Fujian, Guangdong, Beijing and Shanghai as key markets; light, strong and aged are layered, with brands and tea markets. The market saw light-aroma heat and strong-aroma return, with wide price bands; e-commerce and livestreaming grow, but origin and craft claims need care.

Brew light style at 90–95°C for 15–25 seconds; strong at 95–100°C for 10–20; aged at 100°C or boiled. Use 7–8 g per 120 ml in a gaiwan, rinsing first; light should not over-steep, strong can rest. Visit Xiping, Gande and Xianghua in spring or autumn.

Myths and boundaries

“Tieguanyin equals light style” ignores strong and aged. “Light is low-end” ignores craft and leaf. “Guanyin rhyme is mysticism” ignores cultivar, garden and shaking. “Anxi tea is all Tieguanyin” ignores Maoxie, Huangjin and Benshan. “Tieguanyin is green tea” ignores oolong craft.

Anxi is a region and cultivar system, not one tea; cultivar, sub-area, shaking, rolling and roast together define it. This page gives structure; tea pages and producers carry specifics.
