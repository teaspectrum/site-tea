Alishan is not one mountain but a high-mountain oolong region in Chiayi County, Taiwan. Shizhuo, Xiding, Taihe and Zhangshuhu, at about 1,000–1,700 metres, use Qingxin and Jinxuan to make the flower, cream and high-mountain sweetness of Taiwan’s light-aroma ball oolong.

The short answer

Alishan lies in Chiayi County and is a representative high-mountain oolong area. Gardens are spread on slopes roughly 1,000–1,700 metres; Shizhuo, Xiding, Taihe and Zhangshuhu are often cited as sub-areas. Qingxin oolong dominates, with Jinxuan second; craft is light oxidation, ball rolling and light roast. Alishan is a broad place name, and garden elevation, cultivar, management and roast vary greatly, so “Alishan tea” is not one product.

Geography and growing conditions

The Alishan belt runs through Meishan, Zhuqi, Fanlu and Alishan Township near the Tropic of Cancer, with high elevation, cloud and day–night range; slopes drain well and soils are acidic. Altitude slows growth, softens leaf and raises amino acids; cloud reduces direct light and supports flower and sweetness. Spring and winter are main seasons: spring is aromatic, winter sweet, summer and autumn cheaper.

High mountain is not automatically high quality: lower gardens can also make light-aroma oolong, and elevation is one factor. Typhoons, intense rain, frost and soil conservation are risks, and high-mountain gardens raise long-term land and landscape issues.

History and industry

Taiwan’s high-mountain tea grew with domestic demand in the late twentieth century; Alishan, Lishan and Shanlinxi became light-aroma ball-oolong names. Alishan expanded in the 1980s–90s, with Shizhuo and Xiding forming processing and trading centres. Farmers’ associations and competition tea built the brand, and tourism and gift demand expanded.

Today Alishan tea is mostly family gardens and small factories, with some cooperatives, associations and brands; domestic demand, gifts and small export form the market. Prices range from daily tea to competition lots, set by elevation, cultivar, season and craft.

Cultivars and craft

Alishan grows Qingxin, Jinxuan, Cuoyu and Sijichun; Qingxin is floral and site-driven, Jinxuan creamy and soft, Cuoyu fresh, Sijichun early and aromatic. Craft runs withering, shaking, kill-green, rolling, ball rolling, drying and light roast; oxidation is about 15–25%, and ball rolling shapes the pellet.

Shaking is the aroma key: repeated shaking and spreading gives edge oxidation and flower; ball rolling and loosening build the tight shape; light roast stabilises aroma and lowers moisture. Farmers vary oxidation, pressure and roast temperature; light aroma does not mean no craft character.

Flavour and grades

Dry tea is ball-shaped, emerald to dark green; liquor golden to pale yellow, with flower, cream and fruit, sweet and smooth, low bitterness. Jinxuan cream is not an absolute cultivar marker and may come from craft or additives; Qingxin emphasises flower and site. Competition tea scores aroma, liquor, taste and leaf, with high premiums.

Grade depends on elevation, cultivar, season, craft and competition: spring is floral, winter sweet, summer and autumn cheaper. Buy by origin, cultivar, season and roast, not the “Alishan” name alone.

Market and brewing

Alishan tea is mainly domestic, gifts and small export, with shops and experience stores in Shizhuo and Xiding; competition and brands carry premiums. Brew at 90–95°C for 20–30 seconds, in gaiwan or Yixing, rinsing the ball first; do not over-steep light aroma, and use slightly cooler water for creamy Jinxuan.

Visit Shizhuo, Xiding and Taihe for gardens and factories; spring plucking and winter season are best, and mountain weather changes fast, so book factory visits.

Myths and boundaries

“High mountain equals high quality” ignores craft and storage. “Alishan tea is all Qingxin” ignores Jinxuan, Cuoyu and Sijichun. “Cream equals Jinxuan” ignores craft and additives. “Light aroma means no roast” ignores light roasting. “Alishan is one mountain” ignores the broad region and sub-areas.

Alishan is a region and brand system, not one tea; elevation, cultivar, craft and batch together define it. This page gives structure; tea pages and producers carry specifics.
