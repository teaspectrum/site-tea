Türkiye

Çaykur Altınbaş (Golden Head Black Tea)

Çaykur Altınbaş is a Turkish black-tea product and grade, not a cultivar and not the “golden head” plant part itself. Çaykur's official product copy says the bud at the top and the two small fresh leaves beneath it are the secret of Altınbaş; the blend carries the highest proportion of golden-tipped, curled leaf, a trademark astringency, a high aroma and enough strength that a small amount makes a large brew, with no additives or chemicals. It comes in a 400 g tin and in 200 g and 500 g bags. Çaykur's training notes explain the Altın Tip / Altınbaş look as an orange-gold “golden tip” formed when the buds are heavily hairy and processing is appropriate. It is not a Turkish Jin Junmei, not a single-estate tea and not an ancient-tree product.

Çaykur Filiz (Bud-Leaf Black Tea)

Çaykur Filiz is a black-tea blend from Türkiye's state tea enterprise, named after the Turkish word for a shoot or bud. The official description is narrow and useful: it contains a high proportion of tea buds, has no additives, and is described as smooth with a lasting aroma. It is not a cultivar, a single estate, or the product of the old draft's ÇT-9 clone, 2008 project, 2011 launch or “feminine afternoon tea” story. The official pack sizes are 500 g, 1,000 g and 2,000 g. Filiz belongs to the everyday blended side of the Çaykur range, while Altınbaş is the golden-tip, more astringent line.

Çaykur Karadeniz Çayı (Bergamot Flavoured)

Çaykur Karadeniz Çayı is a bergamot-flavoured black tea from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. This page covers the official “Bergamot Aromalı Karadeniz Çayı 1000gr” SKU. The product page lists 100% Turkish tea, 100% natural, a soft character and a bulk-use positioning; one box holds 10 units, each with a gross weight of 1.25 kg, at a listed price of TRY 398.50, out of stock at the time of review. Karadeniz means “Black Sea” and is a brand/product name, not a declared blend of Rize, Trabzon, Artvin, Giresun and Ordu. Bergamot is an added flavour, not a tea cultivar. Old claims about a five-province blend, a 2010 “Black Sea corridor” project, a 2013 launch and “Karadeniz Koyu/Bahar” versions have no official basis and are removed.

Çaykur Organic Tea

Çaykur Organic Tea is a certified product line from Turkey’s state-owned tea enterprise, not a single estate and not “all of Rize is organic.” The official sales page lists 18 organic products, including Organic Rize, Organic Hemşin, Organic Çamlıhemşin, Organic İkizdere and Organic Zümrüt Green Tea, and names an Ekotar certificate. The old draft claimed the whole line was EU Organic and TR Organic and turned conventional Rize into a caricature of “every family pouring on fertiliser and pesticides.” The official page only confirms Ekotar; some retailers add EU/USDA claims of their own. This page follows the official scope.

Çaykur Rize Turist

Çaykur Rize Turist is a current black-tea product from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. It is not a tea-tourism experience or a fictional tourist souvenir. The official 200 g page calls it a family everyday tea, uses the slogan “The Real Yellow”, and lists 100, 200, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 g packs with 24 pieces per carton. The 200 g box is listed at 90 TL; the 1,000 g page is listed at 409 TL, with 12 pieces per carton and 1.25 kg per piece. Çaykur also sells a separate 200 g “Rize Turist Demlik Süzen Poşet Çay” tea-bag version at 121.50 TL. The official copy does not state a cultivar, a single estate, orthodox or CTC processing, a harvest season, or brewing parameters, so those cannot be presented as established facts about Rize Turist.

Çaykur Tiryaki

Çaykur Tiryaki is a current blended black tea from Çaykur, Turkey's state-owned tea enterprise. “Tiryaki” in Turkish means a habitual devotee — someone who cannot do without tea, coffee or another daily habit. The official copy calls Tiryaki a special blend that can be drunk at any hour, emphasises quality, ideal taste, aroma and a soft texture, and states that it contains no additives. The 500 g pack is listed at 200.50 TL, with 15 pieces per carton and 0.7 kg per piece; the 1,000 g pack is listed at 400 TL, with 12 pieces per carton and 1.25 kg per piece; 2,000 g and 5,000 g packs also exist. The official pages do not give a cultivar, grade code, caffeine figure or “Plus/Sade” sub-line. A third-party exporter describes Tiryaki as a strong blend of mature Rize leaf with deep mahogany liquor that withstands long boiling; that is a trade description, not an official specification.

Türkiye

Rize Çayı (Rize Tea)

Rize Çayı is a protected geographical indication for black tea from Rize province, Türkiye. It was registered on 26 March 2021 as a “menşe adı” (designation of origin), registration no. 707, by the Rize Commodity Exchange. It is not a brand and not a synonym for all Turkish black tea: only tea that meets the origin, material, processing and sensory standard may use the name. Rize is the centre of the Turkish tea belt on the eastern Black Sea, with a PGI dossier that gives roughly 2,300 mm annual rainfall, 14°C mean temperature and 78.8% relative humidity; Rize accounts for 67% of Türkiye's fresh tea output, and about 90% of its agricultural land is tea. The tea is fully oxidised, processed through Orthodox and CTC lines, brewed in a double pot and served in the thin-waisted ince belli glass.

Turkey has one of the world’s highest per-capita tea consumption, yet its gardens sit on a narrow, wet Black Sea mountain strip: Rize, Trabzon and Artvin. Çaykur and private factories, the çaydanlık double pot and the tulip glass make strong black tea the centre of daily social life.

The short answer

Turkish tea is concentrated on the eastern Black Sea coast around Rize, Trabzon, Artvin and Giresun, from sea level to about 1,000 metres, with over 2,000 mm of rain and high humidity. Orthodox and CTC black tea dominate; green and white tea are small. Çaykur is the largest state producer, with private factories alongside. Turks brew strong tea in a çaydanlık, dilute with hot water, serve in a tulip glass and add sugar cubes. Read Turkey by its strong, hot, everyday drinking style first, then region and brand.

Geography and growing conditions

The tea belt faces the Black Sea and backs onto the Kaçkar mountains, with high rain, humidity, cloud and a long growing season. Rize is the core; Trabzon, Artvin and Giresun also grow tea. Gardens run from the coast to about 1,000 metres on steep slopes, making mechanisation and plucking costly. Winter and spring frost are northern risks; intense rain and landslides affect gardens and roads.

The Black Sea climate gives fat buds and strong flavour, but also bitterness; shade, plucking standard and fermentation control decide quality. Most gardens are smallholdings delivering to Çaykur or private factories, with prices shaped by state purchase prices and policy.

History and industry

Tea arrived in Turkey in the late nineteenth and early twentieth century, spread around Rize in the 1920s and gained state factories in the 1930s. Çaykur and private factories coexisted later. The sector is shaped by state policy, purchase prices and cross-border trade, with huge domestic demand and some imports.

Turkey is a major producer and consumer. Rize is the core, Çaykur the largest firm, and private brands and local factories fill the rest. Per-capita consumption is high: tea is central to breakfast, hospitality, shops and offices. Export is limited to nearby markets and diaspora. Prices follow purchase prices, exchange rates and energy costs.

Tea types and flavour

Black tea dominates: deep red, strong, malty and woody, boiled in a çaydanlık and diluted with hot water; sugar cubes are normal. Green and white tea are small and lighter; herbal teas such as linden, sage and mint are also popular. Turkish black tea suits strong, sweet drinking; plain, it can be astringent.

Turkish grades are less fine than South Asian systems, based more on factory, season and brand. Small particles brew fast and suit repeated boiling in the double pot. Strong-to-light ratio is personal taste, not a fixed recipe.

Cultivars and craft

Turkish gardens use Chinese and Georgian introductions plus local selections, mainly for black tea. Processing runs withering, rolling, fermentation, drying and sorting, with some CTC. Black Sea heat pushes growth; plucking standard and fermentation time decide strength and aroma. Smallholders deliver leaf and factories process centrally.

Quality depends on region, season, factory and freshness; state and private brands differ. Buy by dry-leaf colour, aroma and particle, then brewing performance, not the “Rize” label alone.

Market and grades

The market is mainly domestic, with Rize and Çaykur as core brands and prices from everyday loose tea to gifts. Tea is embedded in breakfast, hospitality, shops and offices; coffee and herbal tea compete, but black tea remains national. Export is limited to the Middle East, European diaspora and nearby markets.

Buy fresh black tea with a suitable particle for daily use; a clean, low-astringency batch for plain drinking; a stronger batch for sugar or milk. Factory and brand matter more than the country label.

The cup and brewing

The standard method: water in the lower çaydanlık, leaf and a little water in the upper pot, boiled into strong tea. Pour strong tea first, dilute with hot water, serve in a tulip glass with sugar. Use about 5–8 g per pot and boil 10–15 minutes; strength is set by dilution. Brew green at 80–85°C, white at 85–90°C. Visit Rize from May to September for gardens, factories and Black Sea mountains.

Myths and boundaries

“Turkish tea means Arab mint tea” is a confusion: Turkey drinks plain black tea, while mint tea is another tradition. “Turkish tea is all CTC” ignores orthodox and factory differences. “The çaydanlık is just a prop” ignores that it is the core of strength control. “Rize tea is all Turkish tea” ignores Trabzon, Artvin and Giresun.

Turkey’s tea belt is on the eastern Black Sea coast, not nationwide; administrative borders differ from gardens, and brand or factory cannot replace batch. This page gives structure; tea pages and producers carry grade and price.
