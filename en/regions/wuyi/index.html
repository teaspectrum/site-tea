Wuyi Mountains tea area

China · Fujian

Wuyi rock tea is a Danxia-landscape oolong from northern Fujian. True rock, half-rock, river tea and gorge microclimates, with shaking, rolling and charcoal roast, produce the “rock bone and flower aroma” of Da Hong Pao, Rou Gui and Shui Xian.

Wuyi rock tea is not one name, Da Hong Pao, but a Danxia-landscape oolong system in northern Fujian: true rock, half-rock and river tea, the microclimates of three gorges and two streams, shaking, rolling and charcoal roast, making the depth and layers of “rock bone and flower aroma”.

The short answer

Wuyi Mountain lies in Wuyishan, Nanping, Fujian, where Danxia landforms, the Nine-Bend River and thirty-six peaks create “every rock has tea, no rock no tea”; core true rock includes the three gorges and two streams (Huiyuan, Niulan, Dakengkou, Liuxiang and Wuyuan) plus Jiulongke and Tianxin. Wuyi rock tea (oolong) leads, with Da Hong Pao, Rou Gui, Shui Xian and famous bushes; black tea (Lapsang Souchong/Jin Jun Mei) comes from Tongmuguan. Read by true/half/river rock first, then cultivar, shaking and charcoal roast.

Geography and growing conditions

Wuyi sits in northern Fujian, where Danxia rock, gorges, streams and forest create microclimates; gardens on cliffs, gorges and slopes at about 300–600 metres, with gravelly, acidic, well-drained soil. Rock stores heat by day and releases it at night; stream mist and shade slow growth and refine aroma; “rock bone” comes from soil, microclimate and tree age.

True, half and river rock differ widely: true rock is in the core scenic area, small and expensive; half rock on the edge; river tea on flat stream banks, thinner. Wuyi is also a World Heritage and protected area, so garden expansion is limited; climate, tourism and ecology are issues.

History and industry

Wuyi tea was recorded in the Tang, Jian tea and tea contests flourished in the Song, an imperial garden was set in the Yuan, and rock-tea craft matured in the Ming–Qing; the Da Hong Pao mother trees and famous-bush legends built the brand. In the nineteenth century, Lapsang Souchong and Wuyi tea exported to Europe; state factories and bush selection continued in the twentieth.

Today Wuyi rock tea is layered by true/half/river rock and cultivar, with Da Hong Pao, Rou Gui and Shui Xian as market leaders; companies, farmers and brands coexist, and Tianxin, Xingcun and Wuyi Palace are trading and tourism centres. The market saw a price boom and rational return, with rock, cultivar, craft and roast driving price; ecology and over-plucking are issues.

Cultivars and craft

Wuyi has many cultivars: Shui Xian, Rou Gui, Da Hong Pao (blend or bush), Tie Luohan, Bai Jiguan, Shui Jingui and Bantianyao, plus high-aroma Huang Guanyin and Jin Mudan. Craft runs withering, shaking, kill-green, rolling, drying, sorting and charcoal roast; shaking is the aroma and rock-rhyme core, roast the style key.

Shaking alternates shaking and spreading for edge oxidation, flower and fruit; rolling shapes strips, drying makes maocha; charcoal roast has first, second and warming stages, with heat and time setting fire. Light fire is aromatic, medium fire floral-fruity, high fire woody and caramel; true rock tolerates roast, river tea does not.

Flavour and grades

True rock tea is orange to deep orange, with flower, fruit, cinnamon and orchid, mellow taste, rock rhyme and sweet aftertaste, soft bright leaf; Rou Gui is pungent and cinnamon-like, Shui Xian orchid-like and soft, Da Hong Pao balanced as a blend. Light fire is aromatic, medium floral-fruity, high fire woody, caramel and enduring.

Grade depends on rock (true/half/river), cultivar, tree age, shaking, roast and year; core true rock is expensive, and blended Da Hong Pao and single Rou Gui/Shui Xian both sell. Buy by rock, cultivar, fire and year, not the “Da Hong Pao” pack alone.

Market and brewing

Wuyi rock tea sells mainly domestically, with gifts, collectors and daily drinking; true rock is expensive, half and river rock better value; brands, farmers and tea markets are layered. Tea tourism and the World Heritage site support sales; e-commerce and livestreaming grow, but rock and fire information needs care.

Brew at 100°C for 5–10 seconds in gaiwan or Yixing, using 7–8 g per 120 ml; light fire quick, high fire can rest, old tea can boil. Visit the three gorges and two streams, Jiulongke and Tianxin in spring or autumn; core scenic rules apply.

Myths and boundaries

“Da Hong Pao equals all Wuyi rock tea” ignores Rou Gui, Shui Xian and famous bushes. “True rock is always best” ignores craft and fire. “High fire is good tea” ignores cultivar and rock. “Rock rhyme is mysticism” ignores the combined feel of soil, microclimate and craft. “Wuyi equals the mother trees” ignores the region and commercial tea.

Wuyi is a region and rock system, not one tea; rock, cultivar, shaking, roast and year together define it. This page gives structure; tea pages and producers carry specifics.
