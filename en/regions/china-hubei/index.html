Hubei spans mountain tea in the west and the Jianghan plain: steamed green Enshi Yulu, Yichang black, Chibi green brick and Yuan'an yellow tea form an underrated central-China region.

The short answer

Hubei is not a single tea region. Western Hubei — Enshi, Yichang, Wufeng, Hefeng — makes green, black and steamed green tea; Chibi's Zhaoliqiao makes green brick (a dark tea) for border and export markets; Yuan'an makes yellow tea; Laifeng's teng cha is a different plant. Read Hubei by asking which tea you mean, because leaf, process and market differ.

Geography and growing conditions

Hubei has three layers: the Wuling, Wu and Wudang mountains of western Hubei at 300–1,200 metres, cloudy with acid red-yellow soils, good for green and black tea; the southern Dabie Mountains in the east, hills and low mountains for green tea; and the Jianghan plain and river hills, mainly processing, trade and consumption. Yichang, Enshi, Wufeng, Hefeng, Yingshan and Chibi are the main origins.

Terroir and craft

Hubei tea spreads across western Enshi, Yichang, Wufeng and Hefeng and the eastern/southern hills, roughly 500–1,500 metres, planted with Enshi populations, Fuding and Longjing 43. The range is wide: Enshi Yulu is steamed green, Yihong is congou black, and western Hubei also makes dark tea and vine tea. Steaming, fixation, rolling and fermentation each form a system.

History and industry

Hubei is tied to Lu Yu, born in Jingling (today Tianmen), whose Classic of Tea took shape in the middle Yangtze tea country. In the nineteenth century, Hankou became a tea collection and Russian-trade hub, and green brick travelled via the Han River and Zhangjiakou to Mongolia and Russia. In the twentieth century, Yihong, Enshi Yulu and Yuan'an yellow tea developed; today green, black, dark tea and export blends coexist.

Tea types and everyday drinking

Enshi Yulu is a steamed needle-shaped green tea, fixed with steam, bright green and clean; Yihong Gongfu is a fine black tea with golden tips and honeyed sweetness; Chibi green brick is a dark tea pressed into bricks, red and thick, suited to boiling and milk tea; Yuan'an yellow tea is a small yellow tea, mellow after menhuang; Laifeng teng cha is a Vitaceae plant, not Camellia sinensis.

Industry and market

Hubei produces a moderate volume. Enshi Yulu, Yihong, Wufeng Maojian and Zhaoliqiao brick tea are regional brands sold within the province and nationally. Enshi selenium tea uses a “selenium” story, with prices from everyday green to gift boxes. Wuhan is an active tea market and transit point: Zhaoliqiao brick tea travelled via Hankou and Zhangjiakou to Mongolia and Russia and is still made for border markets and collectors, while Enshi Yulu sells to the north on its steamed-green and selenium story.

The cup and brewing

Enshi Yulu is clear green, with seaweed, bean and fresh sweetness, a steamed style close to Japanese green tea. Yihong is bright red, with honey, fruit and malt; brick tea is fuller and suited to boiling. Brew Yulu at 75–80°C and fast; Yihong at 90–95°C. Visit Enshi in April–May for steaming and selenium gardens.

Buying and boundaries

For Enshi Yulu, check steam fixation, colour and aroma, and avoid long hot steeps; for Yihong, look at golden tips, sweetness and body; for brick tea, check leaf, pressing and storage, and rinse before boiling; for Yuan'an yellow, check even menhuang. Teng cha is a herbal substitute — read the plant name. Hubei has many brands; origin, process and batch matter more than the label.

Myths and boundaries

“Hubei only makes green tea” ignores Yihong, brick tea and vine tea. Hubei is a multi-category origin with western green, black and dark tea. Nor is “Enshi selenium tea” the whole province: Yichang, Wufeng and Hefeng have their own styles.

Source boundary

Hubei tea spans western, eastern and river regions; not all Hubei tea comes from one mountain. Green brick is a dark tea, not a green tea despite the name. Teng cha is not tea. This page does not reduce Hubei to a “green tea province”.
