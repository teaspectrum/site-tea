China · Taiwan

Alishan tea area

A high-mountain oolong area in Chiayi, Taiwan, known for elevation, mist, light oxidation and ball-shaped oolong. High mountain is not a quality guarantee; cultivar, picking, shaking and roasting decide the style.

China · Hunan

Anhua tea area

A core Anhua dark-tea area in Hunan, known for Tianjian, Fu brick, Hei brick and Hua Juan. Anhua dark tea is a product family, not one tea; “golden flowers” require specific fungal-growth conditions.

China · Fujian

Anxi tea area

A core Tieguanyin area in Anxi, Fujian, where cultivar, shaking and roasting produce light-aroma, strong-aroma and aged styles. Anxi Tieguanyin is a protected product name; check origin, cultivar and process on the label.

Sri Lanka

Ceylon highlands

A highland tea region in central Sri Lanka, known for Nuwara Eliya, Dimbula, Uva and other high-grown black teas. Altitude and monsoon shape a bright style; “Ceylon highlands” is a regional name, not a single grade.

India

Darjeeling tea area

A Himalayan tea area in West Bengal, India, known for first and second flush, muscatel notes and high-elevation gardens. Darjeeling tea has GI protection, but “Darjeeling style” is not the same as a genuine origin.

China · Guangdong

Fenghuang Mountain tea area

A core single-bush oolong area in Chaozhou, Guangdong, known for single-tree picking, floral-honey character and roasting. “Milan Xiang” and “Yashixiang” are aroma or commercial names, not separate botanical species.

China · Fujian

Fuding tea area

A core white-tea area in Fuding, Fujian, where Fuding Dabai and Dahao cultivars and withering-drying produce Baihao Yinzhen, Bai Mudan, Gongmei and Shoumei. Fuding is a major origin, but not every white tea comes from Fuding.

China · Sichuan

Mengding Mountain tea area

A historic tea area around Mengding Mountain in Ya’an, Sichuan, known for Mengding Ganlu, Mengding Huangya and Mengding tea culture. Legend, tribute-tea narratives and modern product standards need to be kept distinct.
