China · Taiwan

Alishan tea area

Alishan is a high-mountain oolong region in Chiayi, Taiwan, at about 1,000–1,700 metres. Shizhuo, Xiding and Taihe grow Qingxin and Jinxuan, with light oxidation, ball rolling and light roast giving flower, cream and high-mountain sweetness.

Anhua tea area

Anhua is the core of Hunan dark tea. The Zi River, northern Xuefeng Mountains and the “golden flower” Fu brick craft make Anhua a key name in China’s border tea and today’s dark-tea market.

Anxi tea area

Anxi is the core of Fujian Tieguanyin. Xiping, Gande, Xianghua and Jiandou use the Tieguanyin cultivar, shaking and ball rolling to make “Guanyin rhyme” and orchid aroma; light, strong and aged styles differ.

Ceylon highlands

The Ceylon Highlands are Sri Lanka’s central highland tea region. Nuwara Eliya, Dimbula, Uva and Dickoya are known for high-grown orthodox black tea, with monsoon and estate shaping aroma and price.

Fenghuang Mountain tea area

Fenghuang Mountain is the core of Guangdong Chaozhou dancong, at about 600–1,400 metres. Wudong, Dazhi and more than eighty aroma types produce honey-orchid, duck-shit and yellow-branch “one tree, one aroma” dancong.

Fuding tea area

Fuding is the core of Fujian white tea. Taimu Mountain, Diantou, Panxi and the Fuding Dabai/Dahao cultivars make Silver Needle, White Peony, Gongmei and Shoumei; white tea is neither fired nor rolled, with withering and drying defining style.
