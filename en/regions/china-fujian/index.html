Wuyi Rougui

Wuyi Rougui is a Wuyi rock tea made from the Rougui cultivar, known for a pungent cinnamon-like aroma with floral and fruity notes. Under GB/T 18745-2006, Rougui is one of the five Wuyi rock-tea product categories; Zhengyan and Banyan are trade divisions, not statutory 2006 grades.

Wuyi Shuixian

Wuyi Shuixian is a Wuyi rock tea made from the Shuixian cultivar, with a full body and often an orchid aroma; old-bush tea may show woody and mossy notes. Under GB/T 18745-2006 it is one of five Wuyi rock-tea categories; Zhengyan is a trade division, not a 2006 statutory grade.

Zhengshan Xiaozhong

Zhengshan Xiaozhong is a small-leaf black tea from Tongmu Pass, Wuyishan, Fujian, traditionally pine-smoked and known for pine smoke and longan sweetness. It is one of China’s earliest black teas to reach Europe, but “all black tea originated here” is an overstatement.

Jinjunmei

Jinjunmei is a single-bud black tea developed around the 2000s in the Tongmu Pass area of Wuyishan. The name is a commercial name, not a cultivar; it is usually lightly oxidised and not heavily smoked, with honey and floral-fruity notes. “All golden buds” and “top grade” are marketing claims, not origin or process proof.

Light-Aroma Tieguanyin

Light-aroma Tieguanyin is one of the three product types of Anxi Tieguanyin, alongside roasted and aged styles. It is made from the Tieguanyin cultivar through withering, shaking, fixing, rolling, wrap-rolling and drying, then sorted, blended and given a low-temperature “literary fire” dry. The leaf is tight and sandy-green, the liquor golden, the aroma clear and orchid-like, and the taste fresh, sweet and distinctly “yin-rhymed”.

Fuding Bai Mudan

Fuding Bai Mudan is a white tea made from one bud and one or two leaves, with attached bud and leaf, a silver-white bud among green leaves and an apricot-yellow liquor. Under GB/T 22291-2017 it sits between Baihao Yinzhen and Gongmei/Shoumei in raw-material tenderness.

Fuding Gongmei

Gongmei is a white tea made under the national standard from the young shoots of population cultivars, usually less tender than Bai Mudan and more tender than Shoumei. It often has a more complex floral and sweet character; population gardens are now less common than in the past.

Fuding Shoumei

Fuding Shoumei is a white tea made from relatively mature buds and leaves, often one bud with two or three leaves or coarser material. It has a deeper colour and mellow taste, suits daily drinking and ageing, but “older is better” is not a fact; storage and material matter.

Scope

Fujian sits on China's southeast coast, and its tea areas span northern, southern, eastern and western Fujian. A 2021 provincial policy on tea culture, industry and science set a target of more than RMB 200 billion in total industry-chain output by 2025. The same document names five tea-related modern agricultural parks (Anxi, Fuding, Zhenghe, Zhangping and Yongchun) and five distinctive industry clusters: Wuyi rock tea, Anxi Tieguanyin, Fuding white tea, Fuzhou jasmine tea and Fujian black tea. Fujian produces oolong, white, black and green tea as well as scented tea; calling it simply an "oolong province" or a "white-tea province" misses most of the picture.

Geography and terroir

Northern Fujian is dominated by the Wuyi Mountains, where Danxia landforms, ravines and gravelly soils provide the setting for rock tea. In southern Fujian, Anxi and Yongchun sit among the hills of the Daiyun Mountains, with gardens on slopes and at the edges of woodland. Eastern Fujian, including Fuding, Zhenghe and Shouning, faces the sea from the Taimu and Donggong mountains, where mist and maritime air are pronounced. Western Fujian around Longyan and Sanming mixes mid-altitude mountains, hills and basins. The province has a subtropical monsoon climate; the ocean brings earlier springs to the coast, while northern mountain areas see larger swings in temperature and humidity. Soil, aspect, shade and surrounding trees shape each mountain site, and altitude alone is a poor guide.

Main tea areas

  • Wuyishan–Jianyang–Jian'ou in Nanping: the core of Wuyi rock tea, Lapsang Souchong, Jian'ou Shuixian and northern Fujian oolong; Wuyi is also a World Heritage site for both culture and nature.
  • Anxi–Yongchun–Nan'an in Quanzhou: Anxi Tieguanyin, Yongchun Foshou and Huangjin Gui, with gardens close to mountain villages and streams.
  • Fuding–Zhenghe–Shouning–Fu'an in Ningde: Fuding and Zhenghe white teas, Tanyang Gongfu and eastern Fujian green teas.
  • Fuzhou and the lower Min River: Fuzhou jasmine tea, a reprocessed tea in which green-tea base is scented with fresh jasmine.
  • Zhangzhou–Longyan–Sanming: Zhangping Shuixian cake tea, Baiya Qilan, Yongfu high-mountain tea and Hakka green and black teas.

Tea types and processing

Fujian has one of the most complete oolong repertoires in China. Wuyi rock tea goes through withering, shaking, kill-green, rolling and roasting, with attention to "rock bone and floral fragrance" and layered roast. Anxi Tieguanyin is lightly oxidized; shaking and wrapping shape its orchid aroma and "Guanyin rhyme". White tea relies mainly on withering and drying, with needle, bud-and-leaf and leaf grades from Fuding and Zhenghe. Among black teas, Lapsang Souchong is known for traditional pine-smoke drying, while Tanyang Gongfu and Zhenghe Gongfu emphasize fine strands and sweet aroma. Fuzhou jasmine tea scents the base repeatedly with fresh flowers and a final lift. Green tea is also made in eastern, western and northern Fujian through kill-green, rolling and drying.

History and market

Tang-era texts already record Fujian tea. In the Song dynasty, the Beiyuan tribute gardens in Jian'an brought northern Fujian into the national spotlight. In the late Ming and early Qing, Lapsang Souchong from the Tongmu area of Wuyi entered maritime trade and is often described as one of the early exported black teas. In the nineteenth century, Tanyang Gongfu and Zhenghe Gongfu followed. Today Fujian's five industry clusters link tea with teaware, tea spaces, tea tourism and brand building. Market fashion can move prices and product mix, but it does not replace judgement about mountain site, cultivar and processing.

Common misunderstandings

  • Dahongpao is an oolong, not a black tea. Its "red" name refers to liquor colour and tradition, not to black-tea classification.
  • Tieguanyin is not a green tea. Light-aroma Tieguanyin looks green and is lightly oxidized, but its process is oolong.
  • "Old white tea" is not automatically better with age. Raw material, processing, moisture and storage decide the outcome.
  • "Rock rhyme" is not roast flavour. It comes from mountain site, cultivar, shaking and roast together.
  • Fuzhou jasmine tea is a reprocessed tea. The base is usually green tea; the floral note comes from scenting, not from a tea cultivar.
