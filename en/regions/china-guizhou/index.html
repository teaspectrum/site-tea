Tea in Guizhou

China · Guizhou

An overview of Guizhou tea: seven million mu of mountain gardens, green and black tea, matcha exports and ancient-tree tea.

China · Guizhou

Guiding Yunwu Tribute Tea

Guiding Yunwu Tribute Tea is a nationally protected regional green tea from Guiding County in Guizhou, China. Its core production area is Yunwu Town, and it is a geographical indication, not a single company brand. It received national agricultural GI recognition in September 2010 and a geographical indication certification mark in July 2013. At the end of 2024 the county had 255,600 mu (about 17,040 ha) of tea gardens, 247,300 mu (about 16,487 ha) in production, and an output of 11,807 tonnes, up about 5.35% year on year; Xinhua's contemporaneous figure for primary-sector output value was about RMB 580 million. Local records describe 2,000-plus years of tea growing, 1,000-plus years of tribute tea and 500-plus years of Buddhist tea history; in 1790 the Qianlong court allocated 420 taels of silver and a “Wangu Liufang” tribute stele was erected. The main cultivar is Guiding Niaowang, approved as a provincial improved variety in June 2014, and DB52/T 547—2017 grades the product as Zhengong, Special Grade, Grade 1 and Grade 2.

Shiqian Taicha

Shiqian Taicha is a geographical-indication green tea from Shiqian County, Guizhou, and the name covers both the local tea population and the product. It received national GI product protection in 2009, was registered as a GI certification mark in 2010 and as a China Well-Known Trademark in 2013; local material calls it one of Guizhou’s three famous teas. In 2025 the county reported 29,510 tonnes of finished tea, RMB 3.2 billion in combined output value, 436,000 mu of tea gardens and more than 50,000 ancient tea plants.

Duyun Maojian Tea

Duyun Maojian is a curled green tea from Qiannan, Guizhou, and a geographical-indication protected product spanning Duyun, Fuquan, Weng'an and other counties. Often called “Yugou tea” (fish-hook tea), it has tight curly leaf, abundant white hairs, a tender and chestnut aroma and a fresh, sweet finish. In 2024 Duyun City reported 378,300 mu of tea gardens, 12,600 tonnes of tea and RMB 3.305 billion in output value, with a brand value of RMB 5.223 billion.

Scope

Guizhou sits on the eastern Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau. Tea gardens follow the Dalou, Wuling, Miaoling and Wumeng mountains and the Wu, Qingshui, Hongshui and Chishui rivers. By the end of 2024 the province held 7 million mu of tea gardens, among the largest in China; output reached 307,700 tonnes, primary output value RMB 41.57 billion and composite output value more than RMB 90 billion. More than 6,000 tea processors and cooperatives operated, including 11 national key leading tea enterprises. Guizhou matcha exports ranked first nationally; Duyun Maojian ranked 13th among Chinese tea regional public brands by value; and Meitan County ranked first among national key tea counties for five consecutive years. Guizhou is not a green-tea monoculture: Zunyi black tea, Pu'an black tea, Fanjingshan matcha, ancient-tree tea and Kuding tea together form a multi-category landscape.

Geography and terroir

Guizhou occupies the eastern slope of the Yunnan–Guizhou Plateau. Mountains and hills cover most of the province, karst landforms are widespread, and river valleys cut deeply between scattered basins. The climate is subtropical monsoonal: winters are mild, summers are cool rather than hot, cloud and mist are frequent and sunshine is limited. Spring warming comes early, but late frost remains a risk. Yellow, red, yellow-brown and purple soils dominate; acid soils, slope drainage and forest shade are common terroir factors. Meitan and Fenggang in Zunyi consist of low, broad hills and basin floors. Duyun and Guiding in Qiannan sit in the Miaoling mountains, where mist and river valleys interlace. Shiqian, Yinjiang and Yanhe in Tongren lie in the Wuling interior, with high forest cover around Fanjingshan. Pu'an and Qinglong in Qianxinan are higher, with ancient tea trees and mountain gardens side by side. "High elevation, low latitude, much cloud" is a common combination, but aspect, altitude, soil and shade differ sharply from garden to garden; "plateau mist" explains very little by itself.

Main tea areas

  • Zunyi–Meitan–Fenggang–Zheng'an–Yuqing (green tea, black tea and new tea drinks): the core block of Guizhou tea. Meitan Cuiya, Fenggang zinc-selenium tea, Zheng'an white tea, Yuqing Kuding tea and Zunyi black tea cluster here; Meitan was also home to the 1939 Central Experimental Tea Farm and to Zhejiang University during its wartime westward migration.
  • Qiannan–Duyun–Guiding–Pingtang (Duyun Maojian and Guiding Yunwu): Miaoling mountain gardens. Duyun Maojian, Guiding Yunwu and Pingtang green tea rely on fine buds and fresh aromas; mist, valleys and acid yellow soils are common.
  • Tongren–Shiqian–Yinjiang–Yanhe (Taicha, Fanjingshan greens and matcha): the Wuling interior. Shiqian Taicha, Yinjiang Fanjingshan Cuifeng, Yanhe ancient tea and Fanjingshan matcha create a landscape of green tea, matcha and ancient-tree tea.
  • Qiandongnan–Leishan–Liping (Yinjiu tea and Miaoling greens): Leishan Yinjiu tea, Liping green tea and ethnic-minority tea customs; elevation and forest cover vary widely, so picking windows are staggered.
  • Qianxinan–Pu'an–Qinglong (ancient-tree tea and black tea): Pu'an ancient tea, Pu'an black tea and Qinglong green tea; ancient-tree resources and early spring greens are the two poles of this line.
  • Guiyang–Anshun–Bijie (urban consumption and high-mountain greens): Yang'ai Maofeng, Duobei tea and Bijie high-mountain green tea, close to consumers and also used for high-elevation garden and processing trials.

Tea types and processing

Green tea dominates, while black tea, matcha, ancient-tree tea and herbal infusions all have a place. Duyun Maojian, Meitan Cuiya, Fenggang zinc-selenium tea, Zheng'an white tea and Guiding Yunwu follow green-tea processing: spreading, kill-green, rolling, shaping and drying. Zheng'an white tea uses the spring flush of a white-leaf cultivar and is processed as green tea, not as white tea. Zunyi black tea and Pu'an black tea follow withering, rolling, fermentation and drying. Guizhou matcha is shaded, steamed, cooled and ground, with colour, fineness and umami as the priorities; matcha is a tea product, not a Japanese-only category. Ancient-tree tea emphasises tree age and mountain ecology, but "ancient tree" does not mean wild, and does not guarantee better quality. Shiqian Taicha is a local population cultivar, mostly made into green tea. Yuqing Kuding tea and Zunyi small-leaf Kuding belong to other plants, not to Camellia sinensis. Temperature, timing and hand pressure change with cultivar, grade and product.

History and market

In 1939 the Central Experimental Tea Farm was established in Meitan, and Zhejiang University's wartime migration to Meitan brought it into contact with local tea production. Meitan Cuiya developed from Longjing pan-firing techniques applied to local leaf. Duyun Maojian had a reputation by the Ming and Qing, Guiding Yunwu appears as tribute tea in local gazetteers, and Shiqian Taicha continues through a local population cultivar. In 2024 Guizhou produced 307,700 tonnes of tea, up 7.0%; primary output value reached RMB 41.57 billion, up 5.3%; composite output value exceeded RMB 90 billion; harvested tea area was 5.3939 million mu. Zunyi's new-tea-drink cluster reached RMB 718 million in output value in 2024. Brand values for Meitan Cuiya, Fenggang zinc-selenium tea, Zunyi black tea, Zheng'an white tea and Yuqing Kuding tea were RMB 4.573 billion, RMB 3.917 billion, RMB 2.79 billion, RMB 2.113 billion and RMB 693 million respectively. Area, output, primary value, composite value and brand value are different measures and cannot substitute for one another.

Common misunderstandings

  • Guizhou is not only green tea. Zunyi black tea, Pu'an black tea, matcha, ancient-tree tea and Kuding tea all have stable production.
  • Duyun Maojian is not every "Maojian". It has a specific origin and processing; Maojian from other counties is a different product.
  • Matcha is not Japanese-only. Guizhou produces and exports matcha at scale; shading, steaming and grinding are the processing core.
  • Ancient-tree tea is not wild tea, and not automatically superior. Tree age is one variable among cultivar, ecology, picking and processing.
  • Kuding tea is not a Camellia sinensis tea. Yuqing Kuding and Zunyi small-leaf Kuding come from other plants and are not the same category as green or black tea.
