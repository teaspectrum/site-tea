Xinyang tea area

China · Henan

Xinyang is a famous green-tea region in southern Henan. Xinyang Maojian is known for fine, round, glossy, straight tips with white hair; the Dabie Mountains and north–south climate transition shape its chestnut aroma, freshness and endurance.

Xinyang Maojian is Henan’s tea signature: fine, round, glossy, straight and white-haired, with chestnut aroma, green liquor and fresh taste. It grows on the northern slopes of the Dabie Mountains in China’s north–south transition, a wet mountain pocket that produces a rare northern famous green tea.

The short answer

Xinyang lies in southern Henan, on the upper Huai River and the northern Dabie Mountains, in a north–south climate transition. Gardens concentrate in Shihe, Pingqiao, Luoshan, Guangshan, Xinxian and Shangcheng at about 300–800 metres. Xinyang Maojian is the core, plucked fine and shaped by hand or machine, with chestnut aroma and freshness; Xinyang red, white and oolong also exist. Read Xinyang by Maojian plucking and shaping first, then Dabie terroir and market.

Geography and growing conditions

Xinyang has about 1,000–1,200 mm of rain, four distinct seasons, hot wet summers and cool winters; gardens sit on 300–800-metre slopes in acidic yellow-brown soil with good drainage. The transition shortens the growing season compared with Jiangnan, giving fat buds, high amino acids and lower astringency; slow spring warming concentrates plucking.

Core areas are the “five clouds, two pools and one village”: Cheyun, Jiyun, Yunwu, Tianyun and Lianyun mountains, Heilong and Bailong pools, and Hejiazhai; altitude, cloud and soil create style differences. Late cold, drought and intense rain are risks, and soil conservation matters on high slopes.

History and industry

Xinyang has a long tea history, with Tang production and Song tribute; Xinyang Maojian formed a brand in the early twentieth century, won at the 1915 Panama Exposition, and became a national famous green tea in the 1990s. The industry is Maojian-led, with Xinyang red, white and oolong as supplements; Shihe, Guangshan, Xinxian and Shangcheng are core.

Today Xinyang has large area and output, with companies, cooperatives and family farmers; Maojian is a regional public brand, and competition tea and brands carry premiums. Domestic green-tea competition is intense, and brand protection, outside-leaf blending and price stratification are issues; tea tourism and festivals support sales.

Cultivars and craft

Xinyang plants local population, Fuding Dabai, Longjing 43 and Wuniuzao; local population has small buds, white hair and chestnut aroma, while Fuding Dabai yields larger buds. Maojian picks one bud with one opening leaf, graded special, first and second; craft runs spreading, kill-green, rolling, shaping and drying, with hand and machine shaping.

The “fine, round, glossy, straight, white-haired” look comes from shaping and drying: straightening makes tight strips, rolling is moderate, and drying uses two stages; chestnut aroma comes from kill-green and drying heat. Do not over-roll, which brings bitterness; white hair sits on the bud and is visible in the cup.

Flavour and grades

Dry tea is fine, round, glossy and straight with white hair and emerald colour; liquor is bright green, aroma high chestnut, taste fresh and sweet, leaf bright green. Special grade is early with many buds and expensive; first grade is fine with chestnut aroma; second grade is coarser and cheaper. Xinyang red is bright and sweet; white tea is fresh and sweet.

Grade depends on plucking standard, strip, white hair, aroma, liquor and leaf; competition tea and core areas carry premiums. Buy by season (pre-Qingming or pre-rain), region and craft, not the “Xinyang Maojian” name alone.

Market and brewing

Xinyang Maojian sells mainly in the domestic green-tea market, with Henan, Beijing, Shanghai and Guangzhou as key markets; brands, tea markets and e-commerce coexist, with wide price bands. Xinyang red and white supplement, and tea tourism and festivals support sales.

Brew at 80–85°C in glass or gaiwan for one to two minutes; rinse first, then pour high, avoiding heat that cooks the leaf. Pre-Qingming is fresh, pre-rain stronger; use about 3 g per 150 ml; Xinyang red at 90–95°C. Visit Shihegang, Cheyun and Heilong pools in spring.

Myths and boundaries

“Xinyang Maojian is the only tea” ignores Xinyang red, white and oolong. “The greener the better” ignores kill-green; over-green may mean under-firing. “More white hair is better” ignores cultivar and plucking; hair is a feature, not the only standard. “Xinyang is Jiangnan” ignores the Dabie transition.

Xinyang is a region and brand system, not one tea; altitude, sub-area, cultivar, plucking and shaping together define it. This page gives structure; tea pages and producers carry specifics.
