Xishuangbanna tea area

China · Yunnan

Xishuangbanna is the core of Yunnan puer, with the Lancang River, six famous tea mountains and ancient trees shaping sheng, shou and mountain-specific tea. Ancient tree does not equal quality; craft and storage matter equally.

Raw Pu-erh

Ripe Pu-erh

Ripe Pu-erh (shou pu'er) is the pile-fermented branch of geographical-indication Pu-erh. Xishuangbanna is the homeland of puer: the Lancang River, the six famous tea mountains of Menghai and Yiwu, and ancient-tree resources on Bulang and Bada make “mountain tea” a complex geography and craft language. But ancient tree does not equal quality; craft and storage decide a cup as much.

The short answer

Xishuangbanna lies in southern Yunnan, crossed by the Lancang, with Menghai, Mengla and Jinghong at the core. Puer areas include the old six mountains of Yiwu, Yibang, Gedeng, Mangzhi, Manzhuan and Youle, plus newer Bulang, Bada, Nannuo and Mengsong. Puer sheng and shou lead, with red, white and green tea also made. Read by sheng/shou and mountain first, then tree age, craft and storage.

Geography and growing conditions

Xishuangbanna is south of the Tropic of Cancer with a tropical monsoon climate, distinct wet and dry seasons and about 1,200–1,800 mm of rain; mountains on both banks of the Lancang run 500–2,000 metres, with acidic red soils and good drainage. Ancient gardens sit in forest with high biodiversity; terrace gardens are dense and high-yield. Dry season (November–April) and wet season (May–October) shape plucking and fermentation.

Mountain differences come from altitude, aspect, soil, tree type and microclimate: Yiwu is soft and sweet, Bulang strong, Nannuo floral, Bada wild. Ancient-tree tea is expensive, but age, ecology and management matter more than the label; terrace tea can also be good, with leaf and craft decisive.

History and industry

Puer was recorded in the Tang, and the Ming–Qing tea–horse road carried Xishuangbanna tea to Tibet and inland China; Yiwu became a tribute and tea-house centre in the Qing, and the six mountains formed. In the twentieth century, the state Menghai factory and shou craft (1970s) reshaped the industry; after the 2000s, ancient-tree and mountain tea rose.

Today Xishuangbanna is the puer core, with Menghai, Yiwu, Bulang and Nannuo brands; large factories, small factories, primary workshops and farmers coexist, with loose-leaf trading and brand blending. Ancient-tree protection, over-plucking, ecology and price bubbles are issues; storage (dry or wet) shapes ageing and price.

Tea types and craft

Sheng puer: fresh leaf kill-green, rolling, sun-drying into maocha, steaming/pressing into cake, brick or tuocha, natural ageing. Shou puer: maocha pile fermentation, sorting, steaming/pressing and drying. Sheng is yellow-green to orange-red, floral, honey and wild, with sweet aftertaste; shou is red-brown, camphor, wood and date, mellow.

Craft keys: kill-green heat and time, rolling pressure, sun-drying, pressing tightness and storage humidity. Sheng is said to improve with age, but poor storage brings mould; shou pile degree sets body and aroma. Mountain blending and single-origin both sell: single-origin stresses terroir, blending stresses balance.

Flavour and grades

Yiwu is soft, honeyed and fine; Bulang strong with bitter base and quick sweetness; Nannuo floral and sweet; Bada wild and bitter; Jingmai orchid-like. Sheng grade depends on leaf, mountain, tree age, craft and storage; shou on pile, liquor, sweetness and camphor. Ancient tree, arbor and terrace are leaf categories, not absolute quality grades.

Buy by sheng/shou first, then mountain, year, craft and storage; judge by cake, liquor, leaf and aroma together. Do not rely on “ancient tree”, mountain name or packaging alone; storage and brewing matter.

Market and brewing

Xishuangbanna puer sells mainly domestically, with collectors, gifts and daily drinking; Menghai, Yiwu and Bulang are price highs, with large brands and single-mountain lots layered. Ancient-tree prices are high and volatile, terrace tea is better value; e-commerce and tea tourism support sales.

Brew sheng at 95–100°C for 5–10 seconds in gaiwan or Yixing, rinsing first; shou at 100°C for 10–20 seconds, or boil. Old tea takes hotter water and quick pours; new tea should not be over-steeped. Visit Yiwu, Bulang and Nannuo in spring or autumn; mountain transport and bookings need planning.

Myths and boundaries

“Ancient tree equals quality” ignores craft and storage. “Mountain is everything” ignores blending and year. “Puer always improves with age” ignores storage and leaf. “Sheng equals green tea” ignores post-fermentation and ageing. “Shou is low-end” ignores pile craft and grade.

Xishuangbanna is a region and mountain system, not one tea; mountain, tree age, craft, year and storage together define it. This page gives structure; tea pages and producers carry specifics.

Yunnan large-leaf cultivar

Yunnan large-leaf tea is the local population and selected cultivars of Camellia sinensis var. assamica, with large leaves, high polyphenols and downy buds, suited to pu-erh, black and green tea; it is not a synonym for ancient-tree tea.
