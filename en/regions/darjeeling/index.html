Darjeeling tea area

India

A Himalayan tea area in West Bengal, India, known for first and second flush, muscatel notes and high-elevation gardens. Darjeeling tea has GI protection, but “Darjeeling style” is not the same as a genuine origin.

Darjeeling Tea

Extent

Darjeeling lies in northern West Bengal, on the southern slopes of the Himalayas. Gardens are spread across roughly 600–2,200 metres. Darjeeling tea has geographical indication protection: only products meeting the origin, cultivar and processing requirements may use the protected name. “Darjeeling style” or “Himalayan flavour” is not the same as a genuine Darjeeling origin.

Environment

High elevation, steep slopes, mist, day–night temperature differences and well-drained soil slow leaf growth and produce fine shoots. Different gardens and aspects vary markedly; spring and summer pickings also differ. Darjeeling contains many gardens, each with its own microclimate and management.

Seasons and processing

Darjeeling is mainly black tea, with some green, white and oolong. First flush is usually lightly oxidised, with a bright aroma and lighter body; second flush is more oxidised, with stronger fruit and body; autumn pickings may show different floral and woody notes. “Muscatel” is a aroma description resembling muscat grape, not added grape or flavour.

Quality and flavour

First-flush liquor is pale gold to orange, with floral, green-apple and citrus notes and a fresh taste. Second flush is darker, with more fruit, honey and body. Altitude, cultivar, picking standard and oxidation determine style. Darjeeling is not “the younger the better”, and not every first flush is superior to second flush; it depends on the tea and intended use.

History and boundaries

Darjeeling developed as a commercial tea area in the nineteenth century under British colonial rule, linked to railways and trade networks. Today it is an important Indian export tea and one of the world’s most imitated origin names. Specific development dates, volumes and “three high-aroma” narratives should be checked against Tea Board of India and tea-industry archives.

Common misconceptions

  • “Darjeeling style” is not a Darjeeling origin. GI products have specific origin and labelling requirements.
  • First flush is not always better than second flush. They are different styles.
  • Muscatel is not added grape flavour. It is a aroma description.
  • Is all Darjeeling suitable for milk? First flush is often better plain; second flush more often takes milk, but the tea itself decides.

Representative teas

  • Darjeeling black tea: the general name and regional style.
  • Darjeeling first flush: the first spring picking, light and fragrant.
  • Darjeeling second flush: the summer picking, with more fruit and muscatel character.
  • Darjeeling muscatel: an entry describing a specific aroma type.
