Tea in Anhui

China · Anhui

An overview of Anhui tea: the southern mountains, the Dabie Mountains and the riverine hills, with tea types, representative teas, processing and markets.

Huangshan Maofeng

Huangshan Maofeng is a baked green tea from Huangshan, Anhui, and a geographical-indication product. It is made from the buds and leaves of local cultivars such as Huangshan cultivar, Qimen cultivar, Dishuixiang and Mingzhou cultivar, through withering, fixing, shaping, first baking and final baking. The leaf is slightly curled like a sparrow tongue, ivory-green and downy; the cup is fresh, fragrant and sweet.

Lu'an Guapian

Lu'an Guapian is a flat, melon-seed-shaped green tea from the Dabie Mountains of Anhui, and a geographical-indication product. It is unusual among famous Chinese greens for using a single mature leaf with no bud and no stem. The leaf is pan-fixed, shaped, baked and finished with repeated charcoal “old-fire” passes, giving a chestnut or floral aroma and a thick, sweet cup.

Huoshan Huangya

Huoshan Huangya is a yellow bud tea from Huoshan, Anhui, made from fine young leaves and yellowed through menhuang. It has a straight, slightly open shape and a yellow-green to apricot liquor with sweet, chestnut-like aromas; it is not a green tea, and “Huangjinya” cultivar names are not the same product.

Keemun black tea

Keemun is a congou black tea from the Qimen area of Anhui, known for “Keemun aroma”, often described as a mix of floral, fruity and honey notes. Under GB/T 13738.2-2017 it is a congou black tea; origin, grade and GI status should be checked on the label.

Taiping Houkui

Taiping Houkui is a baked green tea from the Taiping area of Huangshan, Anhui, and a geographical-indication product. It is made mainly from the Shidacha cultivar, picked as one bud and two leaves, then sorted, withered, fixed, shaped and baked. The leaf is large, flat and upright, with two leaves holding one bud; the cup is orchid-fragrant, fresh and sweet.

Jinshan Green Tea (Jinshan Shiyu)

Jinshan green tea usually refers to Jinshan Shiyu, a historical green tea from Jixi county in Anhui, China. Its core village sits at about 500–900 m, and the tea is picked around Guyu, when the tightly rolled strips are said to resemble rain threads. It received national geographical indication product recognition in 2025. The old machine draft placed it in a Chinese village in Pai, Thailand, and invented a “Veteran Duan” origin story; that claim has no reliable source and has been corrected.

Scope

Anhui lies between the Yangtze and Huai rivers. Its tea gardens are concentrated south of the Huai, in the mountains of southern Anhui, the Dabie Mountains of western Anhui and the hills along the Yangtze. In 2024 the province maintained 3.2 million mu of tea gardens and produced 181,000 tonnes of made tea worth RMB 23.9 billion; tea is grown across 10 municipalities and 56 counties. Export volume and value have long ranked second and third nationally. Anhui is not a one-note green-tea province: Huangshan Maofeng, Liuan Guapian, Taiping Houkui and Keemun black tea sit alongside Huoshan Huangya, Huoshan Huangdacha, Jingting Green Snow and Yongxi Huoqing.

Geography and terroir

Southern Anhui is dominated by the Huangshan, Jiuhua and Qiyun mountains and the Xin'an River basin, with tea gardens on lower slopes and in intermontane basins. The Dabie Mountains run through Lu'an and Anqing, where altitude and aspect vary sharply. The riverine hills near Wuhu, Hefei and Chuzhou sit closer to major markets. The climate is subtropical monsoonal: springs warm quickly and turn wet, summers are hot and humid, and autumns cool down. Red, yellow and yellow-brown soils dominate, and slope drainage and spring mist are common terroir factors. Aspect, shade, soil and farm management still differ from garden to garden; "high mountain and cloud mist" alone explains little.

Main tea areas

  • Huangshan–Taiping–Qimen: the core of Huangshan Maofeng, Taiping Houkui and Keemun black tea, and the protected geographical areas for Maofeng and Keemun.
  • Lu'an–Huoshan–Jinzhai: Liuan Guapian, Huoshan Huangya, Huoshan Huangdacha and the yellow teas of western Anhui, with many gardens at higher elevations in the Dabie Mountains.
  • Xuancheng–Langxi–Guangde: Jingting Green Snow, Yongxi Huoqing and Guangde Huangjinya.
  • Chizhou–Anqing–Tongling: green and black teas around Jiuhua Mountain, Xiaokeng and Yuexi, where mountain tea gardens overlap with Buddhist tea culture.
  • Riverine hills: green and reprocessed teas near Wuhu, Hefei and Chuzhou, close to consumers.

Tea types and processing

Green tea dominates, but yellow and black teas are equally distinctive. Huangshan Maofeng uses fine buds and leaves, light rolling and drying; the dry leaf is slightly curled and shows white down. Liuan Guapian is a leaf-only green tea that avoids buds and stems and relies on a stronger pan-firing. Taiping Houkui is pressed flat with two leaves holding one bud, hence the saying that both ends of Houkui are pointed. Huoshan Huangya and Huoshan Huangdacha develop yellow liquor and leaves through menhuang, a covered yellowing step. Keemun black tea uses the Zhuye cultivar and goes through withering, rolling, fermentation, drying and refining; its aroma is a blend of honey, flowers and fruit. Processing parameters change with origin, leaf grade and product grade, so no single temperature or timing fits every tea.

History and market

Lu Yu's Classic of Tea already recorded teas from Shouzhou and Shuzhou. During the Tang and Song, tea in southern Anhui expanded with the development of Huizhou and its Buddhist monastery networks; in the Ming and Qing, Huizhou merchants carried Anhui tea into the national market. Keemun black tea took shape in the late nineteenth century and entered export markets, while Huangshan Maofeng, Taiping Houkui and Liuan Guapian were standardized in the late Qing and early Republic. In 2024 the province's tea industry chain exceeded RMB 90 billion in output value. New-style tea drinks, tea tourism and brand building are expanding the market, but export volume, brand value and historical narrative are not interchangeable measures.

Common misunderstandings

  • Anhui is not only green tea. Yellow tea, black tea and reprocessed teas are integral to the province.
  • Huangshan Maofeng is not "all tea from Huangshan". Its protected area, cultivars and processing have clear boundaries.
  • "Keemun aroma" is not a single added flavour. It is a composite of cultivar, processing and ageing, and varies greatly by grade.
  • "High mountain and cloud mist" does not guarantee quality. Altitude, aspect, soil, picking and processing must be read together.
